# Proposal: CHIP, an engine of 8-bit sound chips

Status: **settled** (2026-10-25); the specs lead from here: [../chip.md](../chip.md), and the files and screens
of section 13. A synth engine beside DX7, Juno-106 and TB-303: one virtual sound chip whose
voice takes the parts of the sound chips of the 8-bit era as an instrument's settings, made the way those chips made
sound, in integers. It replaces the CHIP cartridge (`F9`, 32 Juno-106 patches) with 128
instruments of its own, and the demo disk's chip songs with ones written for it.

## 1. What it is

Chip music is a sound of its own: square waves at a few widths, a stepped triangle, a 32-step drawn wave, noise
from a shift register, 4-bit volumes that move in steps 50 or 60 times a second, pitches that land on what a period
register can hold. The Juno-106 and the DX7 can imitate some of it; they cannot make it. CHIP makes it by modelling
the hardware, from the chips' public documentation:

- **the parts of three families of chips, under four names,** each a word of its own on the screens, never a
  product:
  - **BRK**, the handheld: two pulse channels with four widths, a 32-step 4-bit wave channel, a noise channel with a
    long and a short (metallic) shift register, a frequency sweep, a stepped volume envelope (the Game Boy's sound
    hardware);
  - **8BIT**, the console: two pulse channels with their own four widths, a 32-step triangle, a noise channel with
    a long and a short mode and its rate table, a sweep, the non-linear output of its mixer, its output filters
    (the NES's sound hardware);
  - **PSG**, the computers' programmable sound generator: square tones on a 12-bit period, noise from a 17-bit
    register mixed into a tone, a hardware envelope of 16 shapes that can also run fast enough to be a tone (the
    "buzzer"), volumes 3 dB a step, on its three common clocks (General Instrument's AY-3-8910 and AY-3-8912); and
    **16BIT**, the same chip's later version with a finer volume and envelope of 32 steps (Yamaha's YM2149);
- **one voice is one channel** of the virtual chip, any part on any voice: the pool's 16 voices, no chip's limit of
  three or four channels, every part available to every instrument (a PSG square with the handheld's sweep, a
  console triangle on the handheld's clock);
- **canonical where it is heard:** the real clocks and period registers (so high notes go out of tune as on the
  chips), 4-bit volumes, envelopes, sweeps and tables at the chips' own frame rates, the real noise registers and
  rate tables, each family's volume curve and output filter;
- **clean where it is not:** the hard edges are band-limited (no aliasing folding back, as good emulations do),
  fast noise is averaged over each output sample, and nothing of the chips' faults is kept (no mixer crosstalk, no
  DC offset left in).

A CHIP voice costs less than a Juno-106's; its fastest noise about as much (section 9).

## 2. The voice

A voice is an oscillator, a volume, a pitch and a few things that move them once a frame.

### The oscillator (OSC)

| OSC | What | Its settings |
|---|---|---|
| `PULSE` | a pulse: 8 steps a period, high for its DUTY | DUTY: `12.5%`, `25%`, `50%`, `75%` (the BRK's four, or the 8BIT's four with their own phase; PSG and EXACT clocks take the 8BIT's) |
| `TRIANGLE` | the console's triangle: 32 steps, down 15 to 0 then up 0 to 15 | |
| `WAVE` | one of the engine's 16 waves (section 3): 32 steps of 4 bits | the WAVE table |
| `BUZZ` | the PSG's hardware envelope run as an oscillator | SHAPE: `SAW DN`, `SAW UP`, `TRI`; STEPS 16 or 32; GATE: off, or cut by a square at the same note (the "sync buzzer") |
| `NOISE` | the noise register's output, high or low | (the noise settings) |

**Noise mixed in** (NOISE `ON` with any OSC but `NOISE`): the oscillator is heard only while the noise is high, as
the PSG mixes noise into a tone.

### The clock (CLOCK)

Each OSC steps at a rate a period register gives: the step rate is the clock's constant over the register, the
register is a whole number in its range, and a note lands on the nearest register (so tuning is the chips': close
in the bass, coarse at the top).

| CLOCK | The clock | PULSE steps at | TRIANGLE, WAVE steps at | BUZZ steps at | Register P |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `BRK` | 4,194,304 Hz | 1,048,576 / P | 2,097,152 / P | 2,097,152 / P | 1-2,048 |
| `8BIT` | 1,789,773 Hz | 894,886.5 / P (silent under P 9) | 1,789,773 / P | 1,789,773 / P | 1-2,048 |
| `PSG 1.77` | 1,773,400 Hz | the clock / 2 / P | the clock / 8 / P | the clock / 8 / P (STEPS 32), / 16 / P (STEPS 16) | PULSE 1-4,095; else 1-65,535 |
| `PSG 1.00` | 1,000,000 Hz | as PSG 1.77 | | | |
| `PSG 1.79` | 1,789,772 Hz | as PSG 1.77 | | | |
| `16BIT` | 2,000,000 Hz | as PSG 1.77 | | | |
| `EXACT` | none | the note's own pitch, no register | | | |

A PULSE period has 8 steps, a TRIANGLE or WAVE 32, a BUZZ its STEPS (doubled for `TRI`): so on `BRK` a pulse plays
131,072 / P Hz and the wave 65,536 / P Hz, on `8BIT` a pulse 1,789,773 / (16 P) Hz, on the PSG clocks a tone the
clock / (16 P).

**The note's pitch** before the register: the note, plus the ARP table's semitones and the arpeggio command's
(`0xy`), plus the PITCH table's and the vibrato's cents, plus the track's pitch (portamento, the vibrato commands:
what moves the other engines' notes).

### The volume

- **VOL**, 0-15, the channel's volume.
- **ENV:** `NONE` (VOL holds); `DOWN` or `UP`, the step envelope: VOL moves one step every STEP × 1/64 s (STEP 0:
  it holds), on CLOCK `8BIT` every (STEP + 1) × 1/240 s; `PSG`, the hardware envelope:
  SHAPE 0-F (the chip's 16 shapes: down, up, down repeated, the triangles, held high or low) and PERIOD 1-65,535,
  its steps at the clock / (16 × PERIOD) (16 steps) or the clock / (8 × PERIOD) (32 steps, with CURVE `16BIT`), the
  PSG 1.77 clock where the CLOCK is not a PSG's; the envelope's level takes VOL's place.
- **The VOL table** (section 3) scales it, 0-15 (15 = as is), once a frame.
- **RELEASE,** 0-15 (frames a step): once the key is let go, the level falls a step of 15 every RELEASE frames; 0
  ends the note at once. A VOL table that ends at 0 without a loop ends the note.
- **The curve** turns the level (0-15, or 0-31 on a 32-step envelope) into amplitude: `BRK` straight; `8BIT` the
  console's mixer (95.88 / (8,128 / x + 100), scaled to 1 at 15); `PSG` 3 dB a step; `16BIT` 1.5 dB a step of 32.
  The level a step is `VOL × table × release`, each a fraction of 15, rounded to a whole step, as a 4-bit chip
  would.
- **The level meets the oscillator** in one of three ways:
  - `PULSE` and `NOISE`: the level through the curve, while the pulse or the noise is high;
  - `TRIANGLE` and `WAVE`: the oscillator's 4-bit step × the level ÷ its top (15, or 31), rounded, through the
    curve of 16 levels (as a wave channel's volume shifts its samples);
  - `BUZZ`: the buzz's own level through the curve (its 16 levels, or 32 with STEPS 32: `PSG` then 1.45 dB a
    step), × the level ÷ its top, in a straight line.

### The sweep

**SWEEP** `OFF`, `DOWN` (the pitch falls) or `UP` (it rises), with SHIFT 0-7 and PACE 0-7: from the note's first
frame, every PACE × 1/128 s (PACE 0: no sweep), on CLOCK `8BIT` every (PACE + 1) × 1/120 s (PACE 0 too), the
register moves by a share of itself, as the handheld's and the console's sweep units do:

| CLOCK | What moves (by itself >> SHIFT) | Its end |
|---|---|---|
| `BRK` | the chip's frequency x = 2,048 − P: DOWN x − (x >> SHIFT), UP x + (x >> SHIFT) | UP past 2,047 silences the note; DOWN stops where its share comes to 0 |
| `8BIT` | the period P − 1: DOWN up by its share, UP down by it | DOWN past 2,047 silences the note; UP stops where its share comes to 0 |
| the PSG clocks | P: DOWN up by its share, UP down by it | held in the register's range |
| `EXACT` | the step's length: DOWN × (1 + 2^−SHIFT), UP ÷ (1 + 2^−SHIFT) | held between 1/16 sample and 16,384 samples |

(SHIFT 0 moves by the whole: on `BRK` DOWN goes to x 0 at once.)

**The sweep owns the register for the rest of the note:** the tables', the commands' and the track's changes of
pitch are not heard on a sweeping note after its first frame, whose ARP and PITCH set where it starts (a sweep from
two octaves up: ARP `UP 2 OCT`); a silenced note stays silent. A NOISE oscillator is never swept.

### The noise

- **RATE** 0-15: `BRK` 524,288 Hz at 0, then 262,144 / 2^(RATE − 1) Hz; `8BIT` (and `EXACT`) the console's 16
  periods (4-4,068 cycles of 1,789,773 Hz); the PSG clocks the clock / (16 × the period), the periods 1, 2, 3, 4, 5,
  6, 8, 10, 12, 14, 16, 19, 22, 25, 28, 31.
- **MODE:** `LONG`, a 15-bit register (taps 0 and 1), the PSG clocks their own 17-bit one (taps 0 and 3);
  `SHORT`, metallic: on `BRK` the 7-bit mode, on every other clock the 93-step mode (tap 6).
- **KEY:** `KEY ON` moves the rate with the note and the track's pitch (not the ARP table), a step a semitone from
  C-4 (higher notes brighter), clamped to 0-15; on top of a NOISE table's rate too.
- **Faster than the sample rate,** the register steps as many times as fall in each output sample and the sample
  is their average, put out as it is: the average is its own filter.

### The output filter (FILTER)

Each family's own, after the voice: `BRK` a 30 Hz high-pass; `8BIT` high-passes at 90 and 440 Hz and a 14 kHz
low-pass; `PSG` a 20 Hz high-pass; `NONE` a 5 Hz high-pass (the DC taken out). One-pole filters, per voice (the chips
filter their mix; for a linear filter the two are the same).

### Frames

Everything that moves once a frame (the tables, the vibrato, the release) runs at **FRAMES** `60 HZ` (59.73 Hz on
`BRK`, 60.10 Hz on `8BIT`, 60 Hz else) or `50 HZ`, counted in output samples from the note's start. Each frame, in
this order: the VOL table, the ARP table, the PITCH table and the vibrato, the DUTY table, the NOISE table, the
release; then the register is found again (unless a sweep owns it). The step envelope and the sweep keep their own
time. A frame's work, an envelope's or a sweep's step is done at the start of the 64-frame block it falls in (up to
1.45 ms early). **Frames never follow the song:** its tempo, ticks and SWING move the arpeggio `0xy` and the vibrato
commands, not the tables.

### Band-limited, in integers

- **Every step of the output is band-limited:** a change of the voice's amplitude at a moment inside a sample is
  added as a short windowed-sinc impulse (16 taps, 64 phases, cut at 0.45 of the sample rate, a Blackman window) to
  a running sum, as band-limited emulators do. A pulse at 1 kHz is about 3 steps a block, a triangle's or wave's
  32 steps a period about 20 a block at 440 Hz.
- **Integers only:** times in 1/65,536 of a sample, products of 32 by 32 bits into 64, no float and no 64-bit
  division; amplitudes from the curves' tables; the kernel's taps summing to exactly one at every phase. The
  playground's twin (section 11) computes the same integers.
- **Noise faster than the sample rate** is averaged, each sample's average a plain step (no impulse): that keeps the
  fastest noise, 12 register steps a sample, near a Juno-106 voice's cost.
- **Its state is the voice's own** (the running sum, the 16 samples of impulse tail past the block, the filters, the
  registers): two voices of CHIP may render at once on the two cores.

## 3. The tables

An instrument does not store lists of numbers: it picks **tables built into the engine**, by number, and how fast
each runs. So an instrument is 28 bytes and a cartridge holds 128.

- **Five kinds of table,** each a list of values, one a step; `|` marks where it loops back to (without one it holds
  its last value); a step lasts SPEED frames (`X1`-`X4`). A table starts at the note's first frame.
  - **VOL** 0-15: a fraction of 15 of the level (attacks, decays, plucks, tremolos, gates, echoes);
  - **ARP** semitones added to the note (drum drops, chords, octaves, trills, runs, effects);
  - **PITCH** cents added (scoops, falls, detunes, wobbles);
  - **DUTY** 0-3, the pulse's width (sweeps, PWM);
  - **NOISE** 0-15, the noise's RATE.
- **VIBRATO** is not a table but three settings: DELAY 0-62 frames (in 2s), SPEED `X1`-`X4` (frames a step), DEPTH
  0-62 cents (in 2s); its cycle is 8 steps: 0, half, full, half, 0, −half, −full, −half.
- **WAVE:** 16 waves, `0`-`F` (all its 4 bits used), of 32 steps of 4 bits, each drawn from a formula over one period (x from 0 to 1) and scaled to
  0-15: `SAW` 1 − x; `SQUARE`; `SINE`; `TRIANGLE`; `FAT` (a saw with a sine); `ORGAN` (sine, octave, two octaves);
  `HOLLOW` (sine and third harmonic); `BELL` (1st, 5th, 9th harmonics); `PULSE 25`; `PULSE 12`; `HALF SINE`;
  `DOUBLE SAW`; `VOX` (harmonics 1, 2, 3, 5); `CLAV`; `WARM` (sine and some octave); `GRIT` (1st, 7th, 13th).
- **The numbers are fixed for good,** as commands' are: table `01` keeps its values forever; a table added later
  takes the next free number (`01`-`3F` a kind, `00` meaning none). A number past the firmware's (a later firmware's
  table) plays as none. The tables' names (10 characters) are shown on the TABLES page.

## 4. The instrument and the cartridge

**A CHIP cartridge** is a record of a new kind, `0x06` (version 1; [../files/store.md](../files/store.md#a-record)),
laid out as every cartridge ([../files/instruments.md](../files/instruments.md#a-cartridge-record)): its name, the
slots stored, then **128 slots of 28 B**: a full cartridge 3,594 B, as the Juno-106's.

| Field | Bits | Values |
|---|---|---|
| OSC | 3 | 0 PULSE, 1 TRIANGLE, 2 WAVE, 3 BUZZ, 4 NOISE |
| DUTY | 2 | 0 12.5 %, 1 25 %, 2 50 %, 3 75 % |
| BUZZ SHAPE, STEPS, GATE | 2, 1, 1 | 0 SAW DN, 1 SAW UP, 2 TRI; 0 16, 1 32; 0 off, 1 on |
| NOISE: mixed in, MODE, RATE, KEY | 1, 1, 4, 1 | off / on; LONG / SHORT; 0-15; off / on |
| CLOCK | 3 | 0 BRK, 1 8BIT, 2 PSG 1.77, 3 PSG 1.00, 4 PSG 1.79, 5 16BIT, 6 EXACT |
| CURVE | 2 | 0 BRK, 1 8BIT, 2 PSG, 3 16BIT |
| FILTER | 2 | 0 BRK, 1 8BIT, 2 PSG, 3 NONE |
| FRAMES | 1 | 0 60 HZ, 1 50 HZ |
| SWEEP, SHIFT, PACE | 2, 3, 3 | 0 OFF, 1 DOWN, 2 UP; 0-7; 0-7 |
| VOL, ENV, STEP, RELEASE | 4, 2, 3, 4 | 0-15; 0 NONE, 1 DOWN, 2 UP, 3 PSG; 0-7; 0-15 |
| PSG ENV: SHAPE, PERIOD | 4, 16 | 0-F; 1-65,535 (0 reads as 1) |
| WAVE | 4 | the wave table, 0-F |
| VOL, ARP, PITCH, DUTY, NOISE: table, SPEED | 6 + 2 each | 00 none, 01-3F; `X1`-`X4` stored 0-3 |
| VIBRATO: DELAY, SPEED, DEPTH | 5, 2, 5 | 0-62 frames, stored halved (0-31); `X1`-`X4` stored 0-3; 0-62 cents, stored halved |
| name, LEVEL | 60, 8 | as every instrument |
| spare | 35 | |

The fields lie in the table's order from the slot's first bit, each least significant bit first.

**A new CHIP instrument** (INIT) is the reference sound: PULSE 50 %, CLOCK EXACT, CURVE BRK, FILTER NONE, FRAMES
60 HZ, VOL 15, ENV NONE, STEP 0, RELEASE 0, no table (each at `X1`), no vibrato (DELAY 0, `X1`, DEPTH 0), LEVEL 0;
the fields it does not use: BUZZ SAW DN, 16, GATE off; NOISE off, LONG, RATE 4, KEY OFF; SWEEP OFF, SHIFT 2, PACE 2;
PSG ENV SHAPE 9, PERIOD 0320; WAVE 0 (SAW).

## 5. Its commands (0x40-0x7F)

CHIP's own 64 codes ([../files/commands.md](../files/commands.md#the-instruments-parameters-0x40-0x7f)), in the
slot's order, each value as the slot stores it (a value past the field's top acts as its top). A command changes
the instrument's settings for the notes it starts, and the sounding note's from the next 64-frame block:

| Code | The note editor's name | Values | On the sounding note |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0x40 | `OSC` | 0-4 | finds its register again |
| 0x41 | `DUTY` | 0-3 | until a DUTY table's next frame |
| 0x42-0x44 | `BUZZ SHAPE`, `BUZZ STEPS`, `BUZZ GATE` | 0-2; 0-1; 0-1 | SHAPE and STEPS find the register again |
| 0x48-0x4B | `NOISE`, `NOISE MODE`, `NOISE RATE`, `NOISE KEY` | 0-1; 0-1; 0-F; 0-1 | RATE until a NOISE table's next frame |
| 0x4C-0x4F | `CLOCK`, `CURVE`, `FILTER`, `FRAMES` | 0-6; 0-3; 0-3; 0-1 | CLOCK finds the register again |
| 0x50-0x52 | `SWEEP`, `SWEEP SHIFT`, `SWEEP PACE` | 0-2; 0-7; 0-7 | a sweep starts or stops (a silenced note stays silent) |
| 0x54-0x57 | `VOL`, `ENV`, `ENV STEP`, `RELEASE` | 0-F; 0-3; 0-7; 0-F | VOL is the step envelope's level now; ENV restarts the PSG envelope |
| 0x58-0x5A | `PSG SHAPE`, `PSG PERIOD HI`, `PSG PERIOD LO` | 0-F; 0-FF; 0-FF | SHAPE restarts the PSG envelope; each byte of the PERIOD as it comes (on two rows, a period between for a row) |
| 0x5B | `WAVE` | 0-F | |

| 0x68-0x6C | `VOL SPEED`, `ARP SPEED`, `PITCH SPEED`, `DUTY SPEED`, `NOISE SPEED` | 0-3 (`X1`-`X4`) | |

37 codes, 27 free. The note editor says the value as the edit pages do (`OSC TRIANGLE`, `ARP TABLE 06 MAJOR`, `VOL
SPEED X2`, `VIB DELAY 20 FRAMES`, `VIB DEPTH 40 CENTS`).

## 6. The screens

CHIP's colour is **light blue** (role 9, [../ui/colours.md](../ui/colours.md)), which it shares with the command
columns: a cell's note and instrument stand at its left, its commands at its right, so the place tells them apart,
and every theme keeps the colour it gives role 9. On a CHIP track the EDITOR's `*40` sign is light blue as its
commands are; the sign itself says "a parameter". The type's name is `CHIP`; the built-in cartridge `F9 CHIPTUNE`.

## 7. LEVEL and the reference sound

**The reference sound:** PULSE 50 %, CLOCK EXACT, CURVE BRK, FILTER NONE, VOL 15, no table, no envelope, no vibrato;
C-4. The engine is scaled so it comes out at −20 dB RMS, as every engine's
([../files/instruments.md](../files/instruments.md#level-and-the-engines-reference)); a CHIP instrument's LEVEL is
measured as any (C-4 held a second, its loudest 300 ms to −20 dB, the peak under −6 dB).

## 9. Costs

- **CPU** (counted on the device compiler's code; to measure on the FM-1), a 64-frame block: a pulse about 5-7 µs;
  a triangle or wave at A-4 about 14 µs, twice that an octave up (each of its 32 steps a period is a band-limited
  impulse); the fastest noise 25-35 µs (12 register steps a sample, stepped in runs, each sample's average a plain
  step). A Juno-106 voice is 30.5-48.7 µs, a DX7 23.5-40. CHIP adds no voices (the pool stays 16).
- **RAM:** none of its own. A voice's state (228 B: its patch fields, the tables' places, the phases, the running
  sum and its 16-sample tail, the filters) fits in the voice slot the DX7's and the sampler's state already size;
  nothing static, no tables built at start.
- **Flash:** the engine's code about 7 KB, its tables 5 KB (the kernel 2 KB, the curves 0.5 KB, the tables and
  waves 1.5 KB, the clocks', noise's and steps' 1 KB); the built-in cartridge 3.6 KB in place of CHIP's 0.9 KB: about
  15 KB of FWS's free part.
- **Core 2:** the engine runs from the image as the others do, its state the voice's alone, so a CHIP voice runs on
  either core.

  (back through the eras to the PSG, slowing from 160 to 108); between eras a time machine (WHOOSH, WARP; WARP DOWN on
  the way home); 8 tracks (LEAD, ECHO, ARP, BASS, KICK, SNARE, HAT, FX), about 81 s, its tracks mastered by their
  jobs and the song at the other demo songs' loudness; 13 voices at most (of the 16).

  bass, held chords, back to drive in its last bars), each ending on its chord and a run, A minor, 125 BPM; as every
  DJ loop, each track one instrument, tracks 1-4 the kick, hats, bass and hook (TRI KICK, 8BIT HAT, SYNC BUZZ, 8BIT
  4ARP in 3-3-2), then 8BIT OHAT and 8BIT SNARE; 4 voices at most.

  TB-303 patches elsewhere stay.

## 11. The playground's twin and the tests

- **The playground's CHIP is the firmware's integers in JS** (whole numbers in doubles, every one exact; the same
  tables from one generator), not floats: a test renders every instrument of the cartridge at four notes, held and
  let go, bent and with a command, through both and requires them equal sample for sample.
- **The usual:** the engine's reference at −20 dB, every LEVEL as measured, the voices on either core equal to one
  core's, the screens' shots equal between the firmware and the playground, the commands' names, the record's bytes
  both ways (the packer and the device).

## 12. Left to the builder

- The integer details: the phase and fraction widths, where the register's division happens, the kernel's
  symmetry, the filters' coefficients for the device's sample rate.
- Whether the frame work moves inside a block at the frame's own sample (exact) or stays at the block's start
  (up to 1.45 ms early); it starts at the block's start, and moves if a listen says so.
