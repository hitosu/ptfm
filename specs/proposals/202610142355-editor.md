# Proposal: the editor

Status: **set aside** (2026-10-26; proposed 2026-10-14): replaced by [the editor page, EDITOR [beta]](202610260240-editor-beta.md),
designed anew. The page built from it (`editor/`, `dist/editor/editor.html`) stays until the new one takes its
place; the new one takes from its code only what fits.

**The editor** is a page in the browser for changing what PT/FM holds with a computer's screen, mouse and
keyboard: moving files between the FM-1 and the computer, writing songs and patterns, editing cartridges and
settings, converting other programs' files into PT/FM's. It talks to the FM-1 over USB-MIDI, as the install
page does, and plays what it edits through the firmware's own sound code, so what is heard is what the FM-1
plays.

## 1. The page

- **One file,** `dist/editor/editor.html`, opened from the file system in Chrome or Edge (Web MIDI, an
  AudioWorklet); everything inlined, as the other pages: classic scripts, the worklet from a `data:` URL,
  the font, the firmware's WebAssembly. Made by `editor/build.py` from `editor/`.
- **The debug page's look:** black, the EGA's sixteen colours, square boxes, the keys' names yellow, the
  pages' font (Iosevka Aile, cut to what the pages write); a sidebar, a header, the content, a footer.
  - **The sidebar:** the brand, the FM-1's connection (CONNECT, the virtual FM-1, what it runs, its free
    space), the sections, the table's keys (OPEN DISK, SAVE DISK, CLEAR), UNDO and REDO.
  - **The header:** the section's name and what it works on (a song and its position, a cartridge and its
    slot), and the preview's transport: PLAY, STOP, what plays and where.
  - **The content:** the section.
  - **The footer:** what is under way (reading, sending, converting) with its progress, and the states:
    the FM-1, the table, the preview, LISTEN.
- **The sections:** FS, IMPORT, WRITER, CARTRIDGE, SETTINGS, MIDI, LISTEN, each a key in the sidebar and a
  number key (`1`-`7` with Alt). HELP, a key in the header, says what each part of the section does.
- **Computer first:** made for a laptop's screen and keyboard; on a phone's width the sidebar folds into a
  bar on top and the panels stack. Touch works for every key and list; the WRITER's grid wants a keyboard.

## 2. The table

**The table** is the editor's own memory: a set of PT/FM's files, as a disk holds them
([../files/disk.md](../files/disk.md)), that the editor works on before any of it reaches the FM-1.

- **Files are records:** each (kind, number, sub) once, its payload byte for byte as the FM-1 stores it
  ([../files/store.md](../files/store.md#a-record)). Nothing converts between the table, a disk and the
  FM-1.
- **What the user sees are objects,** each made of all its records: a song; a pattern with all its parts;
  a cartridge, a sampler cartridge with every sample's audio; the loops with all their parts; DJ's setup;
  the settings; the MIDI map. An object's size is its records' bytes with their headers, and the blocks
  of 4 KB it takes on the FM-1 (a sampler's audio packed over blocks of its own).
- **Kept in this browser** (IndexedDB) after every change: a reload, or a closed tab, finds the table as
  it was. SAVE DISK writes it all, or the objects picked (FS), as a `.ptd`; CLEAR TABLE empties it.
- **OPEN DISK** into an empty table takes the disk as it is; into a table holding things it asks: ADD, SAME
  NUMBERS REPLACED (the disk's objects replace the table's of their numbers, the rest stays), ADD AT FREE
  NUMBERS (as IMPORT, [section 6](#6-import)), or REPLACE THE TABLE.
- **UNDO and REDO** (Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Y) take back and redo every change to the table, from any section, a
  hundred steps deep.
- **References** ([../files/references.md](../files/references.md)) are worked out from the objects
  themselves: a song's positions name patterns, a cell its instrument, a FUSION slot its pattern, a
  sample instrument its sample, a loop its sound. Every object shows what it uses and what uses it.
- **Numbers:** an object keeps its number. **RENUMBER** gives one another free number and rewrites every
  reference to it in the table (cells, song positions, FUSION slots, the loops). The firmware's numbers,
  `F0`-`FF` for cartridges and patterns, are never given to a new object.
- **The built-in instruments** ([../files/instruments.md](../files/instruments.md#built-in-instruments))
  are known to the editor, from the firmware it carries: they are named, picked, previewed and edited as
  any cartridge. A change makes a **changed copy** in the table at the built-in's own number, which the FM-1
  finds before the firmware's own once it is sent; deleting the copy (BACK TO BUILT-IN, as on the device) brings the
  firmware's back; CLONE puts a copy at a free number instead.
- **Versions:** each record keeps its own ([../files/disk.md](../files/disk.md#versions)). The editor
  reads and writes the versions the FM-1 reads; a file of a version it cannot read stays in the table as
  it is, shown dim, and is not sent.

## 3. The FM-1

- **CONNECT** finds the FM-1 among the computer's MIDI ports by its name (`FM-1`, PT/FM's first), as the
  install page does, and asks for its layout ([../files/blob.md](../files/blob.md#seeding)).
- **What the FM-1 holds** comes from its layout: every live record with its place, size and CRC; its
  regions; the user area's free blocks and the reserve; FWS's free part; the built-in instruments; the
  firmware's version. Then the editor reads every record but the samples' audio (~100 KB for a full
  library, a few seconds), so the FM-1's objects have names and references as the table's do; audio is
  read only when an object goes to the table.
- **No new messages:** the seeding protocol does everything the editor needs ([../files/blob.md](../files/blob.md#seeding),
  `fw/web/seed.js`): LAYOUT lists, READ reads records back, and a seeding's blocks write new and changed
  records and the deleted records that remove objects, as one save. The editor plans each transfer with
  the install page's own planner: records the FM-1 already holds with the same CRC stay where they are,
  the rest goes into free blocks of the user area, the reserve kept free.
- **The FM-1 refuses while it has edits not saved** (`SAVE OR REVERT FIRST`), as for an upload; the editor
  says so and waits. While a transfer writes, the FM-1's sound stops (the flash's erases stop it anyway).
- **After a transfer** the FM-1 mounts and reads everything again: the settings, the MIDI map, the loops,
  DJ's stand, what plays (the firmware reads the loops and DJ's stand again after a seeding, as it does
  the settings).
- **The virtual FM-1:** without the device, VIRTUAL FM-1 connects the editor to the firmware itself,
  compiled for the browser, through function calls instead of MIDI: the same layout, the same planner, the
  same writes. Its flash is kept in this browser apart from the emulator's. It lets the editor be tried,
  and tested, without an FM-1.

## 4. FS

Two panels side by side: **THE FM-1** on the left, **THE TABLE** on the right, each a list of its objects
by kind (SONGS, PATTERNS, CARTRIDGES, SAMPLERS, THE LOOPS, DJ, SETTINGS, MIDI MAP), with its number, name,
size in KB and blocks, how many objects use it, and a mark against the other panel: `=` the same on both,
`≠` different, nothing when only here.

- **Picking:** a click picks one, Ctrl and Shift add, the arrow keys move, a kind's line picks its
  objects. The details below the panels show the one picked: its records, what it uses and what uses
  it, each a link that jumps to that object (in FS, or in the WRITER or the CARTRIDGE editor, where it
  is opened).
- **READ INTO TABLE** (the FM-1's panel) copies the objects picked into the table; **SEND TO FM-1** (the
  table's) writes them onto the FM-1. **WITH WHAT IT USES**, on at first, adds what the objects picked use
  (a song's patterns, their cartridges, a sampler's samples, a FUSION's patterns), so a song travels whole;
  a built-in instrument travels only as a changed copy. The FM-1's own changed copies stay as they are
  unless picked.
- **An object of the same number on the other side is replaced;** nothing else there changes. A transfer
  lists what it writes, what it replaces and what stays, before it starts.
- **DELETE** removes the objects picked from their side, **refused for an object something else uses**:
  the key says what uses it (`USED BY SONG 03, PATTERN 1A`), each a link. Deleting a song never deletes
  its patterns; a sampler goes with its samples' audio.
- **MAKE THE FM-1 HOLD THE TABLE** (the table's menu) sends everything and deletes on the FM-1 what the
  table does not hold, as an upload of a disk does: the FM-1's own work not in the table is listed, red,
  before anything is written.
- **BACK UP THE FM-1** saves everything it holds (FWS's free part included) as a disk, as the install
  page's backup does; **ALL INTO TABLE** reads it all into the table.
- **Space:** a bar for each of the FM-1's areas (the user area: used, free, the reserve kept free; FWS's
  free part) and the table's total against the user area. A transfer's plan draws what it writes on the
  bar before it starts.
- **When it does not fit:** the plan says how many KB are missing and lists, biggest first, what would make
  room: the objects of the transfer (to leave out: a click drops one) and the FM-1's objects the table
  does not hold and nothing uses (to delete in the same transfer: a click adds one), each with its size,
  until the bar shows it fits. Samplers' audio, the biggest part, comes first.

## 5. The preview

- **The firmware's sound code itself:** the firmware is compiled for the browser, as the emulator's is,
  with a small part of the editor's own that calls the player directly (play a song from a position, a
  pattern in a loop, a note of an instrument; stop; mutes; what plays and where). It runs in an
  AudioWorklet, at 44.1 kHz.
- **Fed from the table:** before it plays, the editor lays out what it needs as the FM-1's flash (the song,
  its patterns, their cartridges and samples' audio, FUSION's patterns, the settings) and the firmware
  mounts it, as at power-on. So a song previewed sounds as the FM-1 plays it: the same engines, effects,
  voices and settings. When what it needs is more than the user area holds, FWS's free part takes the rest,
  as a move there would.
- **Edits reach it at once, and what plays goes on:** after the first layout, every change of the table goes
  in through the firmware's edit memory, as the FM-1's own edits before SAVE do: a pattern's cells are read
  again from the next row, a song's record at once; a change of size or of a cartridge reads everything
  again (what plays pauses). Built-in instruments' changed copies always go in that way.
- **What plays and where** shows in the header (the song, its position, the pattern, the row, the tempo,
  the voices) and in the WRITER's grid (the playing row's band, the tracks sounding).
- **AUTO LEVEL** measures an instrument as the FM-1's own AUTO LEVEL does, with the same code
  ([../files/instruments.md](../files/instruments.md#level-and-the-engines-reference)).
- **The computer's keyboard** plays the instrument picked in the WRITER and the CARTRIDGE editor as the
  playground's keyboard does (`Z X C V B N M , . /` F3-A4 with `S D F H J L ; '` black, `Q W E R T Y`
  B4-G5 with `3 4 6`), OCTAVE `-` / `+`; a MIDI keyboard on the MIDI page does too.

## 6. Import

Drop files on the page, or IMPORT's OPEN: each kind is read in the browser and becomes PT/FM's objects in
the table, at free numbers, the references among them rewritten to match. What it made, and what it could
not keep, is listed.

- **Disks** (`.ptd`) and **data blobs**: their objects, as they are.
- **DX7 cartridges** (`.syx`, the 32-voice bulk dump): a DX7 cartridge, exactly
  ([../files/instruments.md](../files/instruments.md#dx7)); a single voice's dump: a cartridge of one.
  CARTRIDGE's EXPORT writes a DX7 cartridge back as a `.syx`.
- **Tracker modules: MOD, S3M and XM,** read as IBXM reads them (the reference the conversion is tested
  against, [../files/commands.md](../files/commands.md#tracker-effects)): the song (tempo, ticks, the order,
  its restart, the global volume, the pans, the slides' kind), its patterns (every channel a track, 16 at
  most; each cell's note, instrument, volume, effect and XM's volume column as commands, S3M's effects as
  their XM twins, `Dxx` rewritten in hex), a sampler cartridge (an instrument for each module instrument
  or sample, its loop, volume, base note and tune from the sample's rate, finetune and relative note) and
  every sample's audio in PTR. **IT is not read** (IBXM does not read it); nor are XM's envelopes, kept as
  the sampler's envelope at most.
- **Sounds** (WAV read by the editor itself, at its own rate and depth; FLAC, MP3, OGG and the rest as the
  browser decodes them): a sample's audio, after picking the part to keep on its waveform (dragging its
  start and end, hearing it), mono or one side, its rate (the editor proposes the lowest that loses
  nothing heard: the highest frequency the sound holds, measured, under half the rate) and its depth (8
  or 16 bits); coded in PTR as the packer codes it ([../files/ptr.md](../files/ptr.md)), into a sampler
  cartridge of the table, new or picked, with an instrument that plays it (its base note given or found
  from its pitch), its LEVEL measured as AUTO LEVEL measures it. 8 bits at first; TRIM SILENCE drops the quiet
  at either end.
- **MIDI files** (`.mid`, formats 0 and 1), a first version: a song and its patterns. Each channel's notes
  become tracks (as many tracks as the channel plays notes at once, 16 in all at most; what does not fit is
  counted); notes are put on the nearest row (ROWS A BEAT 3, 4, 6 or 8, 4 at first; TICKS A ROW 24 / ROWS A
  BEAT, so TEMPO is the file's BPM), velocity becomes the cell's volume, a note's end a note off (`===`, not
  the drums'); the first tempo becomes the song's TEMPO, later changes `Fxx`; patterns of 1-8 bars of the
  file's time signature (4 at first), patterns alike kept once; each channel's first program onto a built-in
  instrument by a table of all 128 General MIDI programs (every PT/FM carries them: the grand piano on KEYS/ORGANS'
  GRAND PNO, the fingered bass on NICE BASS ...), channel 10's drums onto FM DRUM KIT, a sound for each drum
  note, played at C-4; any channel's instrument picked by hand before the song is made. The sustain pedal holds
  notes' ends; pitch bend, modulation and the other controllers are left out and counted.

## 7. The WRITER

Songs and patterns, laptop-friendly in PT/FM's style: not a tracker's screen.

- **The song** (left): its name and time (TEMPO, TICKS A ROW, ROWS A BEAT, SWING, AT THE END, LOOP FROM,
  the global volume, the slides), the order (its positions, a pattern each: add, insert, duplicate, delete,
  move; a click opens the pattern), the mixer (each track's LEVEL, PAN, DUCK and SENDS) and the FX setup
  (ROUTING, the chain, each effect on or off and its knobs), all as fields with their units.
- **The pattern** (the middle): the whole pattern in view, tracks across, rows down, each cell as the
  FM-1's grid shows it (`C-4 0B:05 7F A04`); the current row drawn a little larger, the current row and
  column lit; the playing row marked while the preview plays; the beat's rows lighter. Its size (tracks,
  rows) and EDIT STEP above it.
- **The cell** (right): the current cell as named fields: the note (a list and the keyboard), the
  instrument (a list of cartridges and their slots, by name, the built-in ones included), the volume, and
  up to four commands, each picked from a list grouped as [../files/commands.md](../files/commands.md)
  groups them (by name, the track's engine's parameters by name) with its value in words beside it
  (`ARPEGGIO 0 4 7`, `TAPE STOP 8 ROWS`).
- **The whole pattern** stays in view beside the grid as a strip: each cell a dot in its engine's colour, the
  part the grid shows framed, the current and playing rows marked; a click goes there. The grid fits its
  tracks to the width: FULL cells, NOTE + INSTRUMENT, or NOTES with marks for a volume and commands; ◀ SONG
  hides the song's panel for more room.
- **Keys:** the arrows move (Tab and Shift+Tab a track), PageUp / PageDown a bar, Home / End the pattern's
  ends; the computer's keys write notes as the FM-1's keys play them (`[` `]` the octave), with the
  instrument picked, and move EDIT STEP rows; `=` a note off; Delete clears the cell or the block; Shift and
  the arrows (or a drag) mark a block; Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X, Ctrl+V copy, cut and paste it, Ctrl+A the pattern;
  Ctrl+↑ / ↓ transpose it a semitone (with Shift an octave); Insert puts a row in, Ctrl+Backspace takes one out
  (in the tracks marked); Enter goes to the cell's fields; Space plays and stops the pattern, Shift+Space the
  song from the current position; F1 the help. A click on a track's name mutes it in the preview.
- **New, clone, delete** songs and patterns; a pattern used by a song is refused for delete, as in FS. SEND
  TO FM-1 and READ FROM FM-1 beside the song move it, with what it uses, through FS's plan.

## 8. The CARTRIDGE editor

- **A list of cartridges** (the table's, then the built-in ones: a change to one makes its changed copy in
  the table, [section 2](#2-the-table)), and a cartridge's slots; NEW, CLONE, DELETE (BACK TO BUILT-IN for a
  changed copy); a slot's COPY, PASTE, CLEAR (refused for a slot cells play).
- **A slot's page, by engine,** every parameter as a named field with its range, its words and a slider:
  the DX7's six operators as a table and the voice's fields; the Juno-106's sliders and switches; the
  TB-303's knobs; a FUSION slot's pattern; a sample instrument's sample, START, END, the loop, BASE NOTE,
  TUNE, VOLUME and envelope, drawn on the sample's waveform. Every slot's name and LEVEL, with AUTO LEVEL.
- **A sampler cartridge's samples:** the list with each one's length, rate, depth and size; a waveform;
  NEW INSTRUMENT FOR IT; IMPORT A SOUND ([section 6](#6-import)); DELETE takes the instruments that play it
  along, as the FM-1's does, refused while cells play one of them. A sampler goes to and from the FM-1 with
  its samples; SEND TO FM-1 beside the cartridges.
- **The preview:** the slot picked plays from the computer's keyboard and an on-screen keyboard, through
  the firmware's engines.

## 9. SETTINGS

The settings record ([../files/settings.md](../files/settings.md)) and the MIDI map
([../files/midi.md](../files/midi.md)) as forms: every setting with its values in words, the 16 GATE
patterns as rows of steps (OFF, ON, ×2, ×3, ×4), the MIDI page's settings, and the MIDI map's mappings
(the channel, the CC or note, the FM-1's control, its kind: add, change, delete). READ FROM FM-1 and SEND
TO FM-1 beside them move just these records; the FM-1 applies them at once.

## 10. MIDI

- **The hub:** any of the computer's other MIDI inputs (a keyboard, a controller, a DAW's port) passed on
  to the FM-1's input, each with its channel kept or changed, and the clock passed or not; so an outside
  controller plays and drives the FM-1 through the computer.
- **The monitor:** every message in and out, the FM-1's included (its MIDI OUT's notes and clock), as
  lines with their time, the channel and the message in words, PT/FM's SysEx named.
- **The MIDI settings** and the MIDI map beside them, as in SETTINGS.

## 11. LISTEN

The FM-1's USB audio on the computer's speakers, as the debug page's LISTEN: on / off, the volume, a level
meter, RECORD and SAVE WAV.

## 12. Files and tests

- `editor/`: the page's sources, `editor/wasm/` the part compiled with the firmware for the preview and the
  virtual FM-1, `editor/build.py` (the WebAssembly through the emulator's build, then the page).
- `tests/editor/` (Node, `run_tests.sh`): the records both ways (every record of the demo disk and every
  built-in instrument decoded and coded again, byte for byte, and read as `tools/ptfm_records.py` reads them),
  the table and the FM-1 against the virtual FM-1 (send with what it uses, read back, delete, an edit
  replacing, the whole table, what does not fit, renumbering, UNDO, a disk at free numbers, the preview's image
  played), the converters (DX7 voices through `.syx` and back, a sound, a MIDI file, and the modules given as
  arguments: every cell's instrument there, every sample decoding, the song played by the firmware).
- The module loaders are IBXM's (`editor/js/ibxm_load.js`, BSD-3-Clause as IBXM) and are checked against
  upstream IBXM's own loading of the same files: every header field, order byte, pattern byte, and every
  sample's loop, volume, pan, relative note, finetune and data.

## 13. Open

- **One implementation of the records and converters or two** ([../open-questions.md](../open-questions.md#data-and-tools)):
  the editor has its own in JavaScript; the tools keep theirs in Python; the tests keep them equal.
- **Older versions:** the loops of versions 1 and 2 are kept as they are and not sent; their conversion
  waits for their byte layouts to be written down.
- **MIDI files:** quantising (rows a beat, swing), tracks per channel, the instrument table, the
  controllers: to try on real files.
- **Sending DJ's stand or the loops** while the FM-1 plays them: they take effect after the transfer
  (the FM-1 stops playing for it).
