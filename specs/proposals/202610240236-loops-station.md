# Proposal: LOOPS as a loop station

Status: **settled** (2026-10-24); the specs lead: [../ui/screens/loops.md](../ui/screens/loops.md),
[../files/loops.md](../files/loops.md). It replaces LOOPS as those pages described it before, and changes the EDITOR's realtime takes ([../ui/screens/editor.md](../ui/screens/editor.md#realtime-takes)),
RECORD PATTERN ([../ui/dialogs.md](../ui/dialogs.md#record-pattern)), the edit memory
([../files/ram.md](../files/ram.md#the-edit-memory-and-the-spare-zone)) and the help.

## 1. Why

LOOPS today is a tracker's pattern recorded live: 12 loops, a loop a track playing a note at a time, a record head
switched on and off, overdubs, an eraser, a cursor moved by hand. It edits more than it performs. A loop station
works the other way: a layer is recorded in one pass, then the next over it, and what is kept is a stack of whole
takes, muted, soloed and mixed while playing over them.

The session LOOPS is for: drums from the pattern recorder, then a bass, then pads; then playing over them on the keys,
muting and soloing loops, until something is worth keeping; then recording the whole performance into a song, FX
turns and punch-ins included. The loops can also be stored in a pattern, to edit in the EDITOR or to load back into
LOOPS later.

- **Goals:** a layer a take, recorded without stopping the music; every action a single press; all 16 loops on one
  screen; what is heard while recording is exactly what is kept; nothing lost on a power cycle once saved.
- **Not goals:** editing notes in LOOPS (the EDITOR does that, through SAVE TO PATTERN); loops of different
  lengths; overdubbing into a loop.
- **A failure even if it works:** a destructive change behind a blind press (length changes live in GLO, never on a
  key); the keys sounding one way while recording and the loop playing back another.

## 2. What goes and what stays

**Goes:** the record head as a switch; overdubbing; the chord spread up the loops; the eraser (LFO), SPACE (K4),
MERGE, CUT SILENT END, CLEAR LOOP, DELETE LOOP, DELETE ALL LOOPS and REC held; ALGORITHM moving a cursor
(ALGORITHM PLAYING with it); the notes shown under the stack; 12 loops at most, a loop a track playing a note at a
time; the QUANTIZE values STEP, 1/8 and OFF; COUNT-IN's KEY (a key always starts a take at once).

**Stays, as now:** LOOPS as a screen of the round, with a playback of its own (what plays plays on into LOOPS until
the loops start; the loops play on outside LOOPS until a song or a pattern starts); LOOPS' own FX setup and
METRONOME, FX tap working on it and FX again going back; one length for all loops, set by the first, in whole bars
(a bar the METRONOME's BEATS A BAR beats of GRID's rows a beat), 128 rows at most; GRID only before the first loop;
COUNT-IN; an outside clock (SYNC EXTERNAL) as now; HOME's combination (white keys mute loops); FX held + a white key
a punch-in; voices past VOICES parked ([../ui/screens/settings.md](../ui/screens/settings.md#voices)).

## 3. The loops

- **Up to 16 loops,** each one instrument and one colour of its own (not its engine's), listed top to bottom in the
  order they were made, the newest at the bottom; under them the placeholder `+ NEW LOOP` while fewer than 16 exist.
  A loop's number is its place, `00`-`0F` (renumbered when a loop above it goes); in CONTROL mode (section 6) the
  rows show their white keys, `1`-`16`.
- **A loop keeps its notes once, its base, plus a repeat count.** It plays its base from the loops' start, as many
  times as its count says, then rests until the length's end; where the length ends inside a repeat, that repeat is
  cut there, drawn and played so. So DOUBLE, a pattern repeated and a length change never copy notes.
- **POLY or MONO, chosen before it records** (section 6): a POLY loop holds up to 4 notes at once, its lanes; a MONO
  loop one, a note played over a held one taking over as GLO's MONO LEGATO says: GLIDE (on a TB-303 its slide,
  elsewhere a slide to the note, at a speed the builder picks for a short, clear glide) or JUMP (a plain new note:
  the held one ends, the new one starts with its attack). Keys pressed together that land on the same row compete
  for it: the one registered last wins (the keys keep their order of registration, also within one scan).
- **What a loop holds is notes:** their volumes (KEYS VOLUME, as today), note offs, and with QUANTIZE REAL TIME their
  note delays (`EDx`) and key offs (`Kxx`); the glides of a MONO loop (a TB-303's `SLD`, elsewhere `3xx`). The
  loops' mix turned, FX changes and punch-ins are heard live and written only by RECORD SONG (section 10).
- **Its mix** is a track's mix (LEVEL, PAN, DUCK, CHORUS, DELAY and REVERB SENDS), set by K1-K4 and FX's MIXER,
  where the loops are the columns (and the KEYS column). **A new loop starts with the next loop's mix,** LOOPS' own
  (K1-K4 on `+ NEW LOOP`; plain at first, as a new song's track; the KEYS column is not touched). **During a take the
  keys sound through the recording loop's mix and LOOPS' effects,** not through KEYS GAIN or the keys' DRY, so the
  loop plays back as it sounded; not recording, the keys sound as on every screen.
- **Voices:** 16 loops of 4 lanes can sound more notes than VOICES allows; parking handles it as for any notes.

## 4. Recording

- **REC records where the selection is:** on `+ NEW LOOP` a new loop of the current instrument; on a loop, a
  re-record of that loop.
- **While the loops play, REC arms:** the take starts at the next loops' start. The row says how long until then
  (`ARMED: STARTS IN 3 BARS`, `... 1 BAR`, `... 2 BEATS`, `... 1 BEAT`), the top line's REC is red.
- **With nothing playing, REC arms and waits:** the first key starts the loops from their start and the take with
  that note, at once; PLAY starts them after GLO's COUNT-IN (OFF at first: at once; 1 or 2 bars of clicks). The
  place the loops were paused at is dropped: a take starts at the loops' start.
- **A take is one pass of the length and stops by itself,** the loops playing on; REC during it stops it early,
  what was played kept and the rest empty. It never rolls into a new take. All other loops play while it records.
  REC while armed disarms (`CANCELLED`). Leaving LOOPS for any screen but FX ends a take as REC does, and disarms.
- **The first loop** has no length to fill: it grows a bar at a time while it records, up to 128 rows, where it
  closes by itself and plays on. REC closes it on a bar line, as now: before a line it runs on to the line, the rest
  of the bar silent; up to a beat after a line it is cut back to that line, and what was played after it is not
  kept. Nothing played: no loop.
- **A re-record deletes the loop's notes the moment it fires,** armed included: the loop is silent from then on and
  never comes back with its old notes. It keeps its place, colour, instrument and mix; the keys play its
  instrument while it records.
- **The selection after a take:** recorded on `+ NEW LOOP`, the selection is on `+ NEW LOOP` again, ready for the
  next layer. A re-record leaves it on the loop.
- **RE-RECORD and PEEL keys:** EDIT re-records and LFO peels (removes) the selected loop; on `+ NEW LOOP` both act on
  the last loop. A peel leaves the selection where it was, so after peeling a loop in the middle the selection is on
  the loop that moved into its place. Neither asks: SEL's REVERT brings back what was saved.
- **The rule while recording: the keys sound exactly as they are recorded.** Quantized as QUANTIZE says (a note
  played before its grid line waits for it; one just after the line sounds at once, as close to the line as it
  can, and is written on the line); MONO one note at a time, legato as MONO LEGATO says; POLY at most 4 notes at
  once, a fifth neither heard nor written. Not recording, the keys play freely: every voice, no grid.
- **Note offs** go on the nearest row, at least a row after their note (REAL TIME: the key off at its tick, as
  now). A note still held when the take ends ends at the length's end.

## 5. QUANTIZE, in LOOPS and in the EDITOR

One set of values for LOOPS' takes and the EDITOR's realtime takes, each with its own setting and default:

| Value | What a note lands on |
|---|---|
| `2 ROWS`, `3 ROWS`, `4 ROWS` | the nearest line of that many rows, from the loops' or the pattern's start |
| `ROW` | the nearest row (the 1/32 rows too at GRID 1/32) |
| `REAL TIME` | the row it falls in, with a note delay `EDx` of the ticks since and its end as a key off `Kxx`, as LOOPS' OFF does now ([../files/loops.md](../files/loops.md#recording-on-the-grid)) |

- **LOOPS:** GLO's QUANTIZE and CONTROL mode's black keys (section 6); `ROW` at first.
- **The EDITOR:** SETTINGS' QUANTIZE, beside COUNT-IN; `ROW` at first, which is what a take does today.
- **Both follow the rule of section 4:** the keys sound as they are written, so a note played before its grid
  line waits for it.

## 6. Playing live: the controls

The keys have two modes, **PLAY** (notes) and **CONTROL**; SEQ switches them. The header shows `SEQ MUTE` or
`SEQ SOLO` in CONTROL mode.

| Control | Does |
|---|---|
| SELECT | the loops, top to bottom, then `+ NEW LOOP` |
| PLAY | plays / pauses the loops; held: stop, back to the start |
| REC | records at the selection: new loop or re-record (section 4); again: stops the take early; armed: disarms |
| EDIT | RE-RECORD: the selected loop (on `+ NEW LOOP`: the last) |
| LFO | PEEL: removes the selected loop (on `+ NEW LOOP`: the last) |
| SEQ | the keys: PLAY ↔ CONTROL |
| ARP | the pattern recorder (section 7) |
| ALGORITHM | TEMPO, any time, playing or not, as GLO's TEMPO turns it |
| K1-K4 | the selected loop's LEVEL, PAN, DELAY SEND, REVERB SEND (CHORUS and DUCK on FX's MIXER); on `+ NEW LOOP` the next loop's |
| PRESETS, ENV | the instrument the keys play and the next loop takes; never a recorded loop's (SEL's REPLACE INSTRUMENT does that) |
| OCT- / OCT+ | the keys' octave |
| FX | LOOPS' FX setup, FX again back; FX held + a white key: a punch-in |
| GLO | the properties (section 8) |
| SEL | the menu (section 9) |
| HOME | HOME's combination, as on every screen: white key *n* mutes loop *n* (SEQ: solos) |

**CONTROL mode, the keys:**
- **White keys 1-16: loops 1-16** from the top. In MUTE, a tap mutes or unmutes the loop at once; held, it waits
  for the next bar line (the row shows a yellow `M` meanwhile). In SOLO the same for solos: while any loop is
  soloed only the soloed ones sound; mutes are kept for when the solos clear.
- **Black keys, by their print:** OP1, OP2, OP3 QUANTIZE `2 ROWS`, `3 ROWS`, `4 ROWS`; OP4 `ROW`; OP5 `REAL TIME`;
  PIT all back on (every mute and solo cleared); the key printed GLO MUTE ↔ SOLO; MONO and POLY how the next loops
  record (the footer shows it). OP6 and the unprinted key do nothing.

## 7. The pattern recorder (ARP)

ARP opens it over the loops; the loops play on, so the pattern is heard in the mix while it is built.

- **4 rows of 16 steps, a note a row** (with a kit each row is a drum). SELECT picks the row the white keys switch;
  a white key switches its step on or off, and a step switched on sounds its note while the preview is stopped.
- **The selected row's note:** K3 a semitone a click; or SEL tapped, then a key: that key, white or black, sets the
  note at the keyboard's octave, heard, and the keys go back to the steps (`KEYS: THE NOTE`, `KEYS: THE STEPS`; SEL
  again before a key: back to the steps); SEL held + a key does the same at once, a shortcut. OCT- / OCT+ move the
  keyboard's octave. A new row starts on the note of the row above it. SEQ does nothing here.
- **K1 STEP SIZE:** a step's length, those GRID makes whole rows of (`1/32`, `1/16T`, `1/16`, `1/8T`, `1/8`, `1/4`;
  `A ROW` first where none is a row long); 1/16 at first. **K2 LENGTH:** 4, 8, 12 or 16 steps; 16 at first.
- **K4 TIMES, ×1-×8, only for the first loop:** the length is the steps × TIMES, rounded up to whole bars, the rest of
  the last bar silent (16 steps of 1/16 ×4: 4 bars). With loops, the pattern always covers their length: its steps
  repeat from the loops' start and the last repeat is cut at the length's end (12 steps over 4 bars of 16 rows: 5
  repeats and 4 steps), so it starts again with the loops each pass; the K4 line reads `FILLS 4 BARS`, dark grey.
- **The notes' length:** GLO switches `RING TO THE NEXT` (each note rings until its row's next note) and
  `A STEP LONG` (a note off a step after each note whose next step is off).
- **The preview plays from the moment it opens.** ALGORITHM turns the tempo, as on the screen; PLAY plays and pauses
  the loops as on the screen.
  - **While the loops play,** the step playing is where the loops are, the place the pattern will land after REC (its
    first step on the loops' start), moving with them; ARP only switches the pattern's sound off and on.
  - **While they do not,** ARP starts and stops the preview, alone at LOOPS' tempo.
  - **PLAY pausing the loops** leaves the preview going on its own from where it was; ARP then stops it.
  - **PLAY starting the loops** makes a preview that plays jump to the loops' place, in time with them from then on.
- **REC writes it, at once, and closes:** on `+ NEW LOOP` a new loop of the current instrument; opened on a loop, it
  replaces that loop's notes, its place, colour, instrument and mix kept. Rows with no step on are left out: the
  rows filled are the loop's lanes, a POLY loop, or a MONO one with one row filled (MONO / POLY play no part here).
  The new loop goes on from where the preview was, without a break. No step on: refused (`NO STEPS ON`). **HOME**
  closes it, nothing written.
- **What is set stays** until power-off, as now; it is not saved.

## 8. GLO: the properties

`LOOPS PROPERTIES`: **TEMPO** (as now, ALGORITHM turns it too), **SWING**, **LENGTH** (in bars: longer adds silence at
the end; shorter cuts bars off the end, asking first when notes would go), **GRID** (`1/16`, `1/16T`, `1/32`; only
before the first loop), **QUANTIZE** (section 5), **MONO LEGATO** (`GLIDE`, `JUMP`), **COUNT-IN** (`OFF`, `1 BAR`,
`2 BARS`: before a take PLAY starts; a key always starts at once).

## 9. SEL: the menu

- **REPLACE INSTRUMENT:** the selected loop takes the current instrument (PRESETS / ENV).
- **DOUBLE:** the length ×2, every loop playing twice what it played; nothing copied (a loop that fills the length
  exactly doubles its repeat count; one that rests or is cut at the end has its notes written out to the old length
  first, so the second half is the first again). Refused past 128 rows (`DOUBLE: AT MOST 8 BARS`).
- **RECORD SONG:** arms the song's recording; REC starts it (section 10).
- **SAVE TO PATTERN, LOAD FROM PATTERN** (section 11).
- **CLEAR LOOPS:** every loop goes, after a question; the length goes with them (the next first loop sets it), the
  properties and FX setup stay.
- **REVERT** (while LOOPS has something unsaved): LOOPS back as saved, after a question: LOOPS' unsaved pieces are
  dropped from the edit memory and what the flash holds plays again. No copy is kept for it; it is not an undo.

## 10. RECORD SONG

- **SEL → RECORD SONG arms it** (the top line shows `SONG` armed); REC starts it at the next loops' start (from
  silence, as a take starts); REC again ends it at the end of the pass. The top line shows `SONG`, the time recorded
  and the position being written.
- **It writes a new song** at the first free number: a position a pass of the loops, a pattern a pass, identical
  passes sharing one pattern. Each pattern holds what sounded in that pass: the loops' notes (repeats written out),
  a muted or non-soloed loop left out from the row its mute took effect; the keys on their own tracks, in the
  current instrument, sounding as they are written (the rule of section 4: QUANTIZE, MONO / POLY); FX turns,
  effects switched on and off, punch-ins, tempo changes and the loops' mix turned as commands at their rows; the
  loops' mix at the start as their tracks' mix. Instruments' parameters (turned from a MIDI map) are not written.
- **Leaving LOOPS for any screen but FX ends it at once,** what was written kept.
- **A song records a performance over the loops as they are:** while it is armed or running, REC only starts and
  ends it, and nothing changes the loops: takes, RE-RECORD, PEEL, the pattern recorder, REPLACE INSTRUMENT, DOUBLE,
  LENGTH, CLEAR LOOPS, LOAD FROM PATTERN and REVERT are refused (`RECORDING A SONG`, the key blinking). Mutes, solos,
  the keys, FX, punch-ins, tempo and the loops' mix stay live, and are written.
- **16 tracks:** the loops' lanes and the keys' notes are packed into the pattern's tracks, lanes sharing a track
  where they do not overlap; what does not fit is heard live, left out, and counted
  (`SONG 12: 3 NOTES LEFT OUT`).
- **The song takes LOOPS' tempo, swing and FX setup at its start.**
- **It is an edit, kept in the edit memory:** the FM-1 cannot write the flash while sound plays (an erase or a
  program stops the code), so the song is built in RAM and SAVE keeps it. It runs until the edit memory or the
  song's 256 positions run out, then stops and says why (`SONG 12 STOPPED: MEMORY FOR EDITS IS FULL`).

## 11. Storing in a pattern and loading one

- **SAVE TO PATTERN** opens the pattern list ([../ui/dialogs.md](../ui/dialogs.md#the-pattern-list)), titled
  `SAVE THE LOOPS TO`, with `NEW` first and picked (the first free number); SEL takes it, or another pattern, which
  asks first when it is not empty. It writes one pattern of the loops' length (`LOOPS STORED IN PATTERN 2A`): every
  loop's notes, repeats written out, each note naming its instrument, lanes
  packed into the 16 tracks where they do not overlap (what does not fit left out and counted); each loop's mix as
  mixer commands (`=`) with its first note on a track, and again where a shared track turns to another loop. The
  loops go on as they were.
- **LOAD FROM PATTERN** opens the pattern list (its PLAY previews at the timing LOAD brings, as now) and replaces the
  loops after a question when there are loops. The length is the pattern's rows in whole bars (at most 128 rows);
  the timing as now (the pattern's song's TEMPO, TICKS A ROW, ROWS A BEAT and SWING). **A loop an instrument:** its
  notes from every track that plays it, up to 4 at once as its lanes; more at once makes another loop of the same
  instrument. Each loop's repeat count is 1, its mix from the `=` commands it finds (else plain), POLY, its glides
  (`SLD`, `3xx`) kept. **As much as
  fits:** 16 loops, 4 lanes each; the rest, and commands a loop does not hold, left out and listed
  (`PATTERN 2A IN LOOPS: 3 INSTRUMENTS LEFT OUT`, `... 12 COMMANDS LEFT OUT`).

## 12. Saving and memory

- **SAVE keeps the whole LOOPS setup,** as DJ's stand is kept: the loops (notes, instruments, colours, mixes, MONO /
  POLY), the length, the properties, the FX setup, the mutes and solos, the selection. A power cycle brings it back,
  paused at the start. Not kept: what plays, an armed take, the pattern recorder's steps.
- **LOOPS lives in the edit memory, as any edit;** LOOPS' own RAM (its two images, 4 KB) goes, and the edit memory
  grows into it and into what the build leaves above its spare minimum: about 70 KB instead of 63 (the build
  prints the figure). Past 64 KB the list's 16-bit offsets no longer reach in bytes; every piece already starts on a
  multiple of 4 (its room rounded up to 4), so the list keeps each offset ÷ 4 in the same 16 bits (256 KB reach, no
  RAM more), and the state's figures of the edit memory ([../connections.md](../connections.md#the-state)) count
  4-byte units too.
- **Sizes:** a note 3-5 B (REAL TIME +2, a note off +2); a jam of 8 loops over 4 bars ~5-8 KB; the most (16 loops
  of 4 lanes, 128 rows, every row full) ~35 KB. Saved loops play from the flash; only what changed since takes edit
  memory.
- **The record** ([../files/loops.md](../files/loops.md)) becomes version 4: the header (the setup), the FX setup, a
  loop table (instrument, colour, MONO / POLY, muted, soloed, lanes 1-4, base in rows, repeat count, mix), then each
  loop's notes as one stream of a pattern's cells, encoded as a pattern track's, its lanes interleaved (section 13). The editor page converts version 3 (each
  old loop becomes a loop of one lane, base the length, repeat count 1; its commands other than note delays and key
  offs left out).

## 13. The player

**A song's track costs ~400 B of RAM:** the replay's channel (~290 B: one note's pitch, volume and every tracker
effect's memory), the track's state (~80 B: instrument, mix, DUCK level, parameter commands), its cursor and cell
(~40 B). 64 lanes as tracks would take ~26 KB; the RAM has ~3.4 KB to spare. A replay channel holds one note, so
lanes cannot share one either. But **a track never holds a voice:** the voices are the pool's places, each with its
own note, pitch and envelope and a tag naming its track, whose mix and mute it takes. A track sounds several voices
already: its last note ringing on while the next starts, and a FUSION note's up to 8 layers, each a voice of that
track. So **lanes skip the replay and play their notes the way the keys play theirs** (the keys are polyphonic with
no track at all):

- **A loop is one of the player's 16 tracks** (those a song uses; LOOPS and a song never play at once): its
  instrument, its mix, its mute and solo, its DUCK level, a MONO loop's glide (its one lane's), its cursor. Its
  replay channel stays unused: LOOPS takes only the replay's clock (TEMPO, TICKS A ROW, SWING, its rows and ticks).
- **A lane keeps ~10 B** (64 at most: ~0.6 KB): its note, volume, voice, the ticks left of its note delay (`EDx`)
  and key off (`Kxx`), a TB-303's gate.
- **Each row,** each loop finds its row (row within its base while its repeats last, else rest) and reads its
  lanes' cells; a note starts a voice (after its `EDx` ticks) as a key's does, with the loop's instrument and the
  cell's volume, ending the lane's note before it (which rings on in its voice, as a track's does). The voice is
  marked as the loop's track's, so the loop's mix, mute, solo, DUCK, parking and core 2's voices treat it as any
  track's voice; only its own lane ends it (its next note, its note off, its key off). **Each tick** counts the
  delays and key offs down and moves a glide on.
- **FUSION notes** take the pool songs' FUSION notes already share (4 with tick commands at once, the oldest giving
  way, sounding on).
- **A loop's lanes are one stream** (the cell of lane *l* at row *r* stored as row 4*r* + *l*), so the 16 track
  cursors the player has read the 16 loops.
- **What it costs:** ~0.6 KB of RAM for the lanes, against the 4 KB of LOOPS' images that go; per row 16 loops' reads,
  less than a song's 16 tracks through the replay. **What it rules out:** tracker effects inside a loop (vibrato, slides,
  arpeggios): loops never record them, and LOAD FROM PATTERN leaves them out (section 11).

## 14. The screen

The body as the frame gives it ([../frame.md](../frame.md)): the top line, then a loop a row, all 16 on show.

```

120 BPM 4 BARS  Q 2 ROWS  REC  ## .. ..  tempo, length, QUANTIZE, REC, the beats

01 OPEN HAT P X4   .#..#..#..#..#..#..#    its strip (4 bars, a lane a voice)
02 SNARE    P X2   ..#...#...#...#...#.
03 SUB BASS M      ##.##.###.##.##.###.
04 WARM PAD P      ====....====....====  (the selected loop boxed)
```

- **A strip** shows the length, or 4 bars of it when longer: a lane a voice, the strip's 11 px split among the
  loop's lanes (1: 11 px; 2: 5 + 5; 3: 3 × 3; 4: 2 × 4, a pixel apart), so a MONO loop fills it all; each note a
  block from its start to its end, a pixel's gap only before a note that starts right where it ends; the loop's own
  notes in its colour, its repeats in that colour's deep shade (30 % toward black); black lines at the bars; the
  play line white through all strips. A strip's ground is deep grey.
- **Longer than 4 bars, the strips scroll** as they do now: the play line moves from the start to the middle,
  stays there while the strips scroll under it, and goes on to the end once the window meets the length's end
  (the window's first row clamp(play − 2 bars, 0, length − 4 bars)); when the loops start again, the window and
  the line jump back to the start.
- **Colours:** each loop's colour, given when it is made and kept: light red, yellow, light green, light cyan,
  light blue, light magenta, brown, green, cyan, red, magenta, light grey, round again. Numbers cyan; a muted or
  silenced (not soloed) loop's number, sound and notes dark grey; the recording loop's number, sound and notes red.
- **The pattern recorder** is a box over the loops (the top line stays in view): its title
  (`RECORD PATTERN: NEW LOOP`, `... INTO LOOP 03`) and `PREVIEW` at its right, green while the preview plays; the
  beat numbers over the steps; the 4 rows (the row's note at the left, LENGTH's cells 11 × 13 in fours; on: the
  loop's colour, the step playing white; off: deep grey, the step playing deep blue; the selected row boxed white,
