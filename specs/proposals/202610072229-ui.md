# Proposal: the user interface

Status: **settled** (2026-10-09), after a review (2026-10-08) and rounds in the playground; the specs:
[../ui/](../ui/README.md) and [../playground.md](../playground.md), which lead from now on. Kept as the
record.

This proposal covers the UI only: screens, controls, colours and behaviour. It contains no firmware
code and no data formats. Storage appears only where the UI meets it: saving, and references between
things. Every part is meant to be tried in the playground before it becomes a spec.

**Words used here:**
- **Cartridge:** a bank of instrument slots, all of one type, with a fixed number of slots.
- **Instrument:** one slot of a cartridge. It holds one of:
  - a DX7, Juno-106 or TB-303 patch;
  - a sample instrument;
  - a FUSION instrument.
- **Pattern:** a grid of tracks × rows. A **cell** is one track's entry in one row: note, instrument,
  volume and up to four commands.
- **Command (CMD):** an instruction to the player, such as a pitch slide, a retrigger, a jump or a
  TB-303 accent. Trackers call them effects. Here **FX** means only the audio effects of the FX
  screen. Which commands exist is in the files proposal ([202610080050-fs.md](202610080050-fs.md)).
- **Song:** a list of patterns to play, in order. Patterns are one shared pool: two songs can play the
  same pattern.
- **Current song / pattern / instrument:** what the screens show and act on. Switching screens keeps
  them.

## 1. The controls

### 1.1 Knobs

- **SELECT** moves the cursor vertically, **ALGORITHM** horizontally.
  - The cursor stops at the edges. Only three things wrap: PRESETS within its cartridge, the
    cartridges (K1 on INSTRUMENTS, SEL + PRESETS), and HOME tap's screens.
- **PRESETS** changes the **current instrument** on every screen.
  - It wraps within the cartridge.
  - The footer names the new instrument at once (section 3), so there is no toast for it.
  - The keys play the current instrument wherever they are not taken for something else.
- **K1-K4** change values at the cursor.
  - What they change depends on the screen, and the screen names it (section 3).
  - K1-K4 never move the cursor. Only writing a note or clearing a cell (LFO) advances it.
- **Acceleration:** a fast turn takes bigger steps (2×, 4×, 8× with speed); a slow turn always moves
  one step. The speed is set in SETTINGS (KNOB SPEED).
- **SEL held + a knob** gives coarse steps: an octave for notes, `10` for a `00`-`7F` value.
  - The exceptions: SEL + SELECT / ALGORITHM marks a block, and SEL + PRESETS turns cartridges.

### 1.2 Buttons: tap, hold and layers

- **PLAY, REC and the note keys act the moment they are pressed,** for timing.
  - **The one exception:** on the EDITOR with REC on, PLAY acts on release. Holding PLAY and pressing
    a key starts a take on that note (6.2).
  - **PLAY held** past the hold delay also stops: back to the start (section 7).
- **Every other button acts on its tap**, at release, so it can also be held as a modifier:
  - **tap:** press and release with no other control touched in between;
  - **held + another control:** a combination. The button's own tap does not happen;
  - **held alone past the hold delay** (500 ms; SETTINGS → HOLD DELAY): an overlay shows the button's
    *layer*, meaning what the keys and knobs do with it (HOME: the screens and tracks; FX: the punch-in
    effects).
    - Letting go then closes the overlay and does nothing else.
    - Combinations work at once; they never wait for the overlay.
    - **The overlay stays up as long as the button is held,** through the combinations made with it
      and a change of screen: HOME + OP1, then OP2 with HOME still held, goes on to the EDITOR with
      the layer still shown. It only shows; the keys and knobs act as they would without it.
    - **Two holds are actions instead:**
      - LFO held in JAM erases (6.5). Its erasing starts only after the grace period, so HOME + LFO
        pressed LFO first never erases;
      - PLAY held on the EDITOR with REC on shows how to start a take, and letting go starts it
        (6.2).
  - While a modifier is held, the keys it uses do not play notes.
- **The combination grace period** (100 ms; SETTINGS → COMBO GRACE, 0-250 ms):
  - **Why:** the FM-1's buttons are not precise, and two pressed "together" drift apart.
  - **A modifier still counts for the grace period after it is let go.** HOME released and LFO
    pressed 50 ms later is still HOME + LFO.
  - **A button that can form a combination on the current screen** acts on its tap only once the
    grace period has passed with no partner pressed. HOME tap switches screens, and LFO tap clears,
    only then. So HOME + LFO can never clear a cell.
    - These buttons are usually HOME, LFO, SEL, FX, ARP and SAVE. On the EDITOR with REC on, PLAY
      waits too, since PLAY + a key starts a take there.
    - Their taps act about 0.1 s after release.
  - **Buttons that combine with nothing on the screen act at once** on release: usually EDIT, GLO,
    ENV and SEQ.
  - **Two tap-acting buttons combine in either order,** except OCT: it never waits, so the keys'
    octave never lags behind a fast change. SAVE + OCT- needs SAVE first (or let go within the grace
    period).
  - **With a control that acts on press** (PLAY, REC, the note keys), the modifier has to come
    first, though it may have been let go within the grace period. A note must sound at once, so it
    cannot wait to see whether a modifier follows.
- **The key that opens something closes it:** an overlay, a mode or an editor. The EDITOR entered
  deep from another screen is left with HOME tap (section 5).
- **An edit mode entered with EDIT** (an instrument's edit page, a song's order, the note editor,
  GATE's steps) **keeps a copy of what it edits, as it was at EDIT:**
  - **EDIT** leaves it keeping the changes;
  - **HOME** leaves it dropping them: what it edits goes back to the copy. The drop is one edit, so
    UNDO brings the changes back; with nothing else unsaved, nothing is unsaved after it.
  - **EDIT never goes deeper from inside one:** that is in its SEL menu (EDIT PATTERN, RENAME).
  - **The copy** is small: the instrument records the session touched (a few hundred bytes each), a
    song's order (at most 256 B), the cells changed, the gate patterns. It comes from the edit
    memory. The pattern editor itself is not an edit mode: its edits have no drop, and UNDO takes
    them back.
- **HOME tap:**
  - **on a main screen, with nothing open:** the next screen in the order (section 5). After the
    last screen it wraps to the first;
  - **deep:** **back**. It closes the topmost level and returns to where you were. Deep means one of:
    - an overlay, a mode or an editor is open;
    - EDIT has taken you into the EDITOR from another screen: from PLAYBACK, from a song position,
      or from a FUSION instrument (section 5).

### 1.3 What each button means

One meaning per button across screens; each screen applies it to its own content (section 9 has every
screen).

| Button | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **EDIT** | Opens the thing at the cursor for editing: a cell (the note editor), a song (its order), an instrument (its editor), GATE's steps. Pressed again, it leaves keeping the changes; HOME leaves dropping them (1.2). On PLAYBACK it opens the EDITOR on what plays, entered deep: HOME tap returns. |
| **SEL** | Select and act. **Held** works as a shift: SELECT / ALGORITHM mark a block, PRESETS turns cartridges, K1-K4 take coarse steps. **Tap** opens the actions menu for the marked block, or for the item at the cursor (copy, paste, move, delete...). |
| **ENV** | `EXPLORE NAMED VAULTS`: the list of containers where things live (patterns, cartridges, samples), with SEL actions inside the list. |
| **GLO** | Properties of the current container: the pattern, song, cartridge, jam, FX chain or view. |
| **SEQ** | `SHOW ELSE, QUICK`: the next subview: the track count, the visualiser, the edit page, the jam's part. |
| **ARP** | `AGAIN, REPEAT, PLEASE`: repeat. **Tap:** hands-free audition: on the EDITOR the cursor's row; on INSTRUMENTS and FX the last note played; nothing on SONGS and PLAYBACK. **Held + SELECT:** the rhythm. **In JAM** it is the arpeggiator, and the latch: ARP pressed while keys are held keeps those notes sounding as if still held; **ARP held + LFO** lets them go. |
| **LFO** | **CLR**, `LEAVE FIELD OPEN`: clear, or reset to the default. On a cell it clears, and on an empty cell it writes a note off. On a value it resets. Held in JAM, it erases. **HOME + LFO** is the help: "LOOK FOR ONSWERS". |
| **REC** | Record. In the EDITOR it switches writing on and off: with it off the keys only play; with it on they write, step by step or in a realtime take. In JAM it overdubs into the loop. |
| **PLAY** | Play / pause (section 7). |
| **HOME** | **Tap:** the next screen; when deep, back. **Held:** the navigation layer: **+ black key** goes to a screen; **+ white key** mutes or unmutes that track (key 1 = track `00` … key 16 = track `0F`) (held past the hold delay: solo); **+ PLAY** rewinds; **+ REC** turns all sound off; **+ LFO** opens the help for this screen ("LOOK FOR ONSWERS"). |
| **SAVE** | Writes all changes to flash. **SAVE + OCT-:** REVERT, after a question (section 8). |
| **FX** | **Tap:** the FX screen (again: back). **Held + white key:** a punch-in effect while the key is held. |
| **OCT- / OCT+** | The keyboard's octave, −4 to +4, with a toast (`OCT +3`) at each change. On the FX screen they switch the selected effect off / on instead. |

- **Mutes** apply to what plays: the song, the looped pattern, or the jam. They are performance state
  and are not saved.
- **Solo:** HOME + a white key held past the hold delay solos that track. The same again on the
  soloed track ends the solo; on another track it moves the solo there.

### 1.4 Current song, pattern and instrument

- **The current song** is the song under the cursor on SONGS. PLAY on SONGS plays it, and so does PLAY
  on the other screens when nothing has played yet (section 7).
- **The current pattern** is the one the EDITOR shows. It is set by ENV on the EDITOR, by EDIT
  PATTERN on a song position or a FUSION instrument's edit page (their SEL menus), and by EDIT on
  PLAYBACK.
- **The current instrument** is set by PRESETS, and by the cursor on INSTRUMENTS. The footer shows it
  on every screen.

### 1.5 Overlays, menus and dialogs

- **An overlay** covers part of the screen: a list, a menu or a set of properties.
  - SELECT moves through it.
  - The key that opened it confirms the highlighted item: ENV in the pattern list opens that pattern.
  - HOME cancels.
- **Properties (GLO):** SELECT picks a property and K1 changes it, at once. GLO or HOME closes the
  overlay; there is no cancel, and UNDO takes a change back.
- **Menus (SEL):** the last action used is preselected, so repeating it is SEL, SEL.
- **MOVE (and SWAP), in every SEL menu, never asks for a number:** the item floats where it is, boxed
  yellow (SEL lit). SELECT carries it, and the list shows it in its new place and what that would
  change; SEL puts it down (a used item asks about its references first, section 8); HOME takes it
  back. Where the places are fixed numbers that other things refer to (instrument slots, pattern and
  cartridge numbers), nothing is pushed aside: MOVE goes only to empty slots or free numbers, SWAP to
  any slot. Songs and the FX chain are orders: there the others make room. The EDITOR's blocks
  float with the cursor the same way (6.2).
- **Questions** are all yes or no, the answers in their own words (`REVERT` / `KEEP`, `FILL` /
  `EMPTY`):
  - **OCT+** answers yes and **OCT-** no, whatever started the question. The two answers sit over
    their buttons' names, no on the left and yes on the right, as the buttons sit.
  - **HOME** cancels: nothing happens.
  - Nothing else acts while a question shows.

### 1.6 LEDs

- **A button's LED:**
  - lit while its overlay or mode is on (GLO, ENV, EDIT, SEL marking, ARP repeating);
  - dim where the button has a function on the current screen;
  - dark where it has none.

  The dim state shows at a glance which buttons do something here.
- **PLAY** (green and red): section 7.
- **REC:** lit while writing is on (EDITOR) or the loop records (JAM); blinking during a realtime take or an overdub.
- **SAVE** follows its own rule, not the dim one: dim while there are unsaved changes, dark when there
  is nothing to save; blinking dim while a save waits for a pause.
- **OCT- / OCT+** follow their own rule, not the dim one: dark at OCT 0, and OCT- lit for the octaves
  below it, OCT+ for those above:
  - dim at 1 octave, full at 2;
  - blinking slowly at 3, fast at 4.

  OCT 0 is the keys' F-3 to G-5; KEYS OCTAVE (6.9) only sets where the octave starts at power-on.
- **The keys light in layers:**
  - black keys: the screens (the current one bright);
  - white keys: unmuted tracks;
  - the punch-in effects: keys 1, 5, 9 and 13 dim, where each group of four starts, and the one
    running bright;
  - gate steps, and JAM's drum steps.

  **With no layer up, a played key lights with its note:** full while the note is loud, dim once it
  has fallen under half its loudest, dark under an eighth (or once it ends). A plucked or struck sound
  flashes and fades, a held pad stays lit: the keys show what sounds, not only what is pressed.

### 1.7 Text entry (names)

Names are edited the arcade way:
- **ALGORITHM** moves along the name; **SELECT** changes the character there.
- **SEL + SELECT** jumps between letters, digits and signs.
- **LFO** deletes a character; **OCT+** inserts a space.
- **Done:** the key that opened the entry. **Cancel:** HOME.
- **Characters:** capitals A-Z, digits, space and `: . - ? | / \ * # $ @ %`.

## 2. Hardware the UI works with

- **Knobs:**
  - 7 endless knobs, with no push switches: SELECT, ALGORITHM, PRESETS, K1-K4.
  - 24 clicks a turn; they give deltas only.
  - MASTER is a pot: the volume, never a UI control.
- **Buttons:** 14, each with an LED.
  - PLAY has two LEDs, green and red; REC's is red.
- **Keys:** 27, F-3 to G-5 at OCT 0, each with an LED.
  - **OCT** shifts them by −4 to +4 octaves, reaching C-0 to G-9 (MIDI 12-127, 116 notes): every
    note a pattern can hold.
  - **At OCT −4** the 7 lowest keys (F to B, below C-0) are silent, since the notation has no name
    for those notes.
  - **16 white keys:** used as 16 tracks, 16 steps or 16 effects.
  - **11 black keys, printed:** OP1 OP2 OP3 | OP4 OP5 | OP6 PIT GLO | MONO POLY | (blank). Used for
    screens and, in JAM, drum lanes.
- **Labels that read wrong.** The printed labels name the FM-1's own synth functions. Most still fit
  their PT/FM job: FX, SEL, EDIT, HOME, SAVE, PLAY / STOP, REC, OCT, and GLO as "global". The others
  get readings of their own:

  | Label | Printed for | Read as | Its job |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | LFO | low-frequency oscillator | `LEAVE FIELD OPEN`; with HOME, `LOOK FOR ONSWERS` | clear (1.3); the help (6.10) |
  | ENV | envelope | `EXPLORE NAMED VAULTS` | the lists of patterns, cartridges and samples |
  | SEQ | sequencer | `SHOW ELSE, QUICK` | the next subview |
  | ARP | arpeggiator | `AGAIN, REPEAT, PLEASE` | repeat; in JAM the arpeggiator, as printed |
  | OP1-OP6 | FM operators | `OPEN PAGE 1` … `OPEN PAGE 6` | SONGS, EDITOR, INSTRUMENTS, PLAYBACK, FX (OP6 reserved) |
  | PIT | pitch | `PICTURES IN TIME` | VISUAL |
  | MONO | mono | `MY OWN NERDY OPTIONS` | SETTINGS |
  | POLY | polyphonic | `PARTY ON, LOOP YOURSELF` | JAM |

  - **Where they show:** only in the help, once in each screen's help, at the button's first mention
    (6.10).
  - **The blank key** is spare and has no reading.

## 3. The screen frame

```
EDITOR 1/3                ▶ ▌▐██        header, 16 px
───────────────────────────────
(the screen's body, 210 px)

───────────────────────────────
0B:05 ICE HARP      0A:1F RUST BASS     footer, 12 px (EDITOR)
0B:05 ICE HARP DX7                      footer, 12 px (other screens)
```

- **The screen:** 240 × 240.
- **An instrument ID** is the cartridge and the slot, both in hex: `0B:05` is slot 05 of cartridge
  0B. Cells, lists, the footer and messages all use this form.
- **Indexes and values are hex; counts are decimal.**
  - **Indexes,** which one of several, are hex and start at 0: cartridges, slots (so instrument IDs),
    songs, patterns, samples, rows, tracks, song positions, pages, the GATE patterns, JAM's lanes,
    bars and steps.
    - **Every row number, shown or referred to:** the row numbers left of the EDITOR's and
      PLAYBACK's rows (FUSION patterns' too), the cursor's and the playing row (`ROW 3F`), and a
      command's row (`D20` breaks to row `20`).
    - **Tracks** are `00`-`0F`: white key 1 is track `00`, white key 16 track `0F`.
    - **Song positions** are `00`-`FF`, the numbers `Bxx` jumps to.
  - **Values** are hex: every value in a cell (its volume, its command's parameters: `7F`, `05`,
    `A04`; the note editor also says them in words, 6.2), and every value without a unit: an
    instrument's parameters (a DX7's 0-99 are `00`-`63`), the MIXER's, FILTER's CUT (`LP 1F`).
    Signed values keep their sign (`+18`, `-07`).
  - **Counts and values with a unit are decimal:** TRACKS, ROWS, EDIT STEP, ROWS A BEAT, TICKS A
    ROW, BITS, BEATS A BAR, voices, times, and everything shown with its unit (BPM, %, dB, ms, µs,
    kHz). Note values stay fractions (`1/16`).
- **Numbers keep a constant length,** so nothing shifts as they change:
  - a number from a fixed range is zero-padded to the range's width: tracks `00`-`0F`, rows
    `00`-`FF`, slots `00`-`FF`;
  - **N/M, where you are of several,** is the index and the last index, both hex, N padded to M's
    width: `00/11` is the first of 18 song positions, `0/3` the first of 4 pages. Two counts stay
    decimal: `06/12 VOICES`;
  - live figures sit right-aligned in fixed fields (STATUS, 6.8).
- **The header:** 16 px, then a 1-px line. From left to right:
  - **The screen's name,** in its group's colour (section 5).
  - **The page, N/M:** where you are in this screen's content. It is hidden when there is only one
    page. What it counts on each screen:

    | Screen | Page counts |
    |---|---|
    | EDITOR | pages of rows |
    | PLAYBACK | song positions (`02/0B`) |
    | INSTRUMENTS | pages of slots |
    | SONGS | pages of the list |
    | STATUS, the help | pages |
  - **Toasts:** messages for 2 s (`SAVED`, `OCT +3`, `12 VOICES`, `TRACK 02 MUTED`).
    - A toast takes the place of the screen's name and page while it shows; the transport, CPU and
      battery stay. That leaves about 27 characters of S (`EDIT MEMORY LOW: SAVE SOON` is 26); a
      longer toast falls back to T (below).
    - What the footer already shows (the current instrument, its cartridge) gets no toast.
    - `12 VOICES` stays decimal; it is a count of voices, not an index.
  - **The transport:** ▶ green while playing, ❚❚ red while paused, ● red while recording.
  - **CPU:** a small bar, 4 × 12 px.
    - It fills with the load, and its colour follows it: green, then yellow, then red.
    - It flashes red at a late audio half (a dropout).
    - The figures are on STATUS.
  - **Battery:** three bars, blue on USB power.
- **The footer:** a 1-px line, then 12 px of 6 × 12 text (40 characters), **on every screen**.
  - **Left: the current instrument,** the one a key plays, in its engine's colour:
    - its ID and its name;
    - its engine (DX7, JUNO, TB-303, SAMPLE, FUSION), except on the EDITOR.
  - **Right, on the EDITOR: what K1's slot of the cell under the cursor holds,** in words, as the
    knobs show it while turned: the command (`A04 SLIDE DOWN 4`) or the volume; an empty slot dim.
  - **Right, on other screens:** what that screen needs:

    | Screen | The footer's right side |
    |---|---|
    | SONGS | the song's tempo and length |
    | INSTRUMENTS | the cartridge's ID and name |
    | PLAYBACK | the play position (`POS 02 ROW 1F`) |
    | FX | the selected effect's CPU share |
    | VISUAL | the view's name |
    | STATUS | the free storage |
    | SETTINGS | the item's default |
    | JAM | the loop's position (`BAR 1/3 STEP 06`) |
  - **While a knob turns,** on any screen, the right side shows that knob's name and value.
- **Text that does not fit falls back to a smaller font,** in the header and the footer alike. The
  heights never change, and the smaller text is centred in the same space.
  - **The header:** S falls back to T, so up to 36 characters fit beside the symbols.
  - **The footer** is in T already, the smallest font, so a name that still does not fit is cut
    short.
  - **The transport, the CPU bar and the battery** are symbols of their own, always drawn the same.
- **Fonts:**

  | Font | Size | Across the screen | Used for |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | T | 6 × 12 | 40 characters | numbers, cells, lists, the footer |
  | S | 8 × 16 | 30 characters | the header, labels, notes |
  | B | 12 × 24, Terminus bold | 20 characters | the focus row's notes in the 2-track view, the note editor |
  | L | 16 × 32 (S doubled) | 15 characters | the note editor's note |

  - **All from Terminus** (SIL OFL 1.1). B is Terminus's 12 × 24 bold, `ter-u24b`.
  - **Capitals only, everywhere,** the help included. Every font holds just the characters the screens use,
    about 58 glyphs:
    - space, `A`-`Z`, `0`-`9`;
    - the characters names may use: `: . - ? | / \ * # $ @ %`;
    - signs the screens need besides: `= + < > ( ) , ! ~` (`~` for XM's volume-column commands, 6.2).

    That is about 4-5 KB of flash for all four fonts.
  - **Symbols are drawn, not glyphs:** the transport's ▶ ❚❚ ●, the `◆ SENDS` mark, bars, dots and
    meters. A ratchet shows as `X2`.
  - **S in 12-px rows:** S's capitals, digits, `-` and `#` sit in its rows 2-11, so in a 12-px row S
    is drawn cropped to its middle 12 rows. A note (`C#4`) fits whole.

## 4. Colours and themes

### 4.1 The default palette: soft EGA

**What changes from the exact EGA colours:**
- The 16 hues stay. The dark ones are lifted so they read on black, and the bright ones are
  softened a little.
- **Black stays black.**
- **Each colour has a deep shade** (about 30 %) to fill a band behind text: the playing row, a
  selection, a bar.
- These are first values, to be tuned in the playground.

| # | EGA | EGA RGB | PT/FM default | Main roles |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | black | `000000` | `000000` | background |
| 1 | blue | `0000AA` | `4C5CD0` | (deep shade) the playing row's band |
| 2 | green | `00AA00` | `4AAA4A` | on / OK states, playing, the PLAY group |
| 3 | cyan | `00AAAA` | `3AA8A8` | labels, beat-row numbers |
| 4 | red | `AA0000` | `C84C44` | recording, warnings, missing references |
| 5 | magenta | `AA00AA` | `B054B0` | (deep shade) a marked block; the SYSTEM group |
| 6 | brown | `AA5500` | `C87C34` | volumes, the HEAR group |
| 7 | light grey | `AAAAAA` | `B4B4AC` | normal text, note offs |
| 8 | dark grey | `555555` | `5C5C5C` | grid lines, empty cells, muted tracks |
| 9 | light blue | `5555FF` | `7C8CF8` | command columns, the MAKE group |
| 10 | light green | `55FF55` | `84E884` | **TB-303** |
| 11 | light cyan | `55FFFF` | `84E8E8` | **Juno-106** |
| 12 | light red | `FF5555` | `F87C74` | **DX7** |
| 13 | light magenta | `FF55FF` | `E884E8` | **FUSION** |
| 14 | yellow | `FFFF55` | `F0E074` | **samples**, unsaved |
| 15 | white | `FFFFFF` | `F4F4EC` | bright text, the cursor |

### 4.2 What carries meaning

- **Colour tells the kind:**
  - the engine (DX7, Juno-106, TB-303, samples, FUSION);
  - the field (volume, command);
  - the state (playing, recording, missing).
- **Brightness tells importance:** bright for the cursor and active things, normal for values, faint
  for empty and muted.
- **Shapes mark positions, never colour alone:**
  - the cursor is a box;
  - the playing row is a band;
  - the focus row is larger, with lines above and below;
  - a marked block is a filled area.

  This keeps every theme usable, the one-colour themes included.
- **Deep shades never carry text on black;** they only fill.
- **In cells:**

  | Part | Colour |
  |---|---|
  | the note and the instrument | the instrument's engine colour |
  | volume | brown |
  | command | light blue |
  | `---` and dots | dark grey |
  | `===` (note off) | light grey |
  | a missing instrument | red on its deep shade |

### 4.3 Themes (SETTINGS → THEME)

**How themes work:**
- Every screen draws by role (label, value, DX7, cursor...), never by a fixed colour, so a theme is
  just a palette.
- Switching a theme changes no layout.

**The themes.** All are on black. A theme is 16 colours (32 bytes), so there can be many.

| Theme | Kind | What |
|---|---|---|
| **DEFAULT** | colour | the soft EGA above |
| **EGA** | colour | the exact EGA colours |
| **PASTEL** | colour | the same hues, lighter and softer: chalk on a blackboard |
| **NEON** | colour | saturated magenta, cyan, violet and lime: a night-time arcade |
| **SUNSET** | colour | warm: reds, oranges, golds, with teal for contrast |
| **ICE** | colour | cool: blues, teals, violet, white, with orange for contrast |
| **CLEAR** | colour | for colour-blind eyes: the engines and states use the Okabe-Ito colours (orange, sky blue, bluish green, yellow, blue, vermilion, reddish purple), which stay distinct with the common kinds of colour blindness |
| **GREEN** | one colour | green phosphor: every role is one of about 6 intensities of green |
| **AMBER** | one colour | the same in amber |
| **WHITE** | one colour | the same in grey |

**In the colour themes:**
- Every theme keeps the 5 engines, the states (playing, recording, missing) and the fields (volume,
  command) apart: 10 roles.
  - Most themes give each its own hue. In DEFAULT, recording and a missing reference share red: a
    missing one is always drawn on red's deep shade, so the two never look alike.
  - CLEAR has only 7 Okabe-Ito colours. The 5 engines get 5 of them, and the states and fields share
    the other 2 and white, told apart by brightness.
- The playground shows each theme on every screen before any is settled.

**In the one-colour themes:**
- The engines are told apart by intensity and by the instrument ID in the cell, not by hue.
- Rule 4.2 keeps the cursor, the playing row and selections visible.

|---|---|---|---|

| OP2 | **EDITOR** | MAKE | editing patterns |
| OP3 | **INSTRUMENTS** | MAKE | cartridges and instruments: choose, try, edit |
| OP4 | **PLAYBACK** | PLAY | what plays, following it; not editable |

| PIT | **VISUAL** | HEAR | visualisers |

| MONO | **SETTINGS** | SYSTEM | global settings and tools |
| POLY | **JAM** | apart | live playing: loops, a drum machine, recording; a playback of its own |
| (blank) | spare | | |

- **There is no manual screen.** Help comes on every screen: HOME + LFO, "LOOK FOR ONSWERS" (6.10).

- **The groups follow the gaps between the black keys** (3, 2, 3, 2), so the keys can be found by
  touch:
  - **MAKE:** the hierarchy from song down to sound;
  - **PLAY:** what plays, and the effects played on it;
  - **HEAR:** what comes out, seen and measured, with room for one more screen;
  - **SYSTEM:** the settings, and JAM beside them, apart from the rest. JAM's name is in the PLAY
    group's colour.
- **HOME + black key:** go to that screen.
  - While HOME is held, the screen keys light dim and the current one bright. LFO lights too, for
    the help.
- **HOME tap:**
  - on a main screen: the next screen in this order, wrapping from SETTINGS to SONGS. JAM is never
    in the round;
  - the round follows the black keys from left to right, so OP*n* (`OPEN PAGE n`) is literally the
    round's *n*th page: OP5 is page 5, FX. A screen given to OP6 later becomes page 6;
  - deep: back (section 1.2).
- **JAM is a world of its own,** reached only with HOME + POLY:
  - going in pauses whatever plays, which keeps its place; in JAM, only the jam plays;
  - HOME tap in JAM goes back to the screen it was entered from, and the jam pauses. After a
    power-on straight into JAM, that screen is STATUS;
  - HOME + a black key and FX tap leave JAM too, and the jam pauses the same way;
  - outside JAM, PLAY resumes what played before (the song or the pattern loop), never the jam.
- **FX tap** goes to FX; pressed again, it goes back.
- **Jumps into the EDITOR** go deep:
  - EDIT on PLAYBACK: the playing pattern, with the cursor on the playing row;
  - SEL → EDIT PATTERN on a song position (in its order): that position's pattern;
  - SEL on a FUSION instrument's PATTERN, on its edit page: its pattern.

  EDIT stays lit while you are there, and HOME tap returns to where you came from, still in the order
  or on the edit page. Inside, EDIT still opens the note editor.
- **One of them is locked:** a FUSION instrument's pattern (6.3).
  - HOME + black key and FX tap are refused, and EDIT flashes.
  - The way out is HOME tap, back to the instrument's edit page.
  - From PLAYBACK and SONGS, HOME + black key may leave for another screen; the deep level is then
    dropped.
- **The screen at power-on** is STATUS, the start screen, unless SETTINGS → POWER-ON SCREEN says
  otherwise: LAST (the screen used last) or any screen.

## 6. The screens

### 6.1 SONGS

- **The body:**
  - **The list of songs:** number, name, tempo, positions, length (mm:ss). The cursor's song is the
    current song.
  - **Below it, that song's order:** a grid of pattern numbers, 8 a line. The playing position is on a
    band.
- **EDIT on a song** moves the cursor into its order (EDIT lit). In the order:

  | Control | Does |
  |---|---|
  | SELECT / ALGORITHM | move through the positions |
  | K1 | changes the pattern at the position |
  | LFO | removes the position |
  | ENV | opens the pattern list; the highlighted pattern goes in at the position |
  | SEL menu | EDIT PATTERN (the EDITOR on the position's pattern; HOME tap comes back), INSERT, DELETE, COPY, PASTE, CLONE PATTERN (copy the pattern to a new number and use the copy here), UNDO |
  | EDIT | leaves the order, keeping the changes |
  | HOME | leaves the order, dropping them (1.2): the order as it was at EDIT; a pattern cloned meanwhile is deleted again |
- **GLO:** the song's properties: NAME, TEMPO, TICKS A ROW, ROWS A BEAT, SWING, AT THE END (LOOP /
  STOP) and LOOP FROM (the position it loops to).
- **SEL menu on the list:** NEW, CLONE, MOVE, DELETE, RENAME. MOVE floats the song along the list
  (1.5). Nothing refers to songs, so moving one needs no reference update.
- **PLAY** plays the current song.

### 6.2 EDITOR

**Subviews (SEQ):**

| Subview | Rows | A cell shows |
|---|---|---|
| 8 tracks | 16 | the note only (8 × 16) |
| 4 tracks | 12 | the note and the instrument ID (6 × 12); 4 whole cells do not fit (section 10) |
| 2 tracks | 8 | the cell with one of its commands: the note in 8 × 16, the rest in 6 × 12 (the cell in 8 × 16 is 4 characters too wide) |

In every subview the footer shows the current instrument and, on the right, the command (or volume)
in K1's slot of the cell under the cursor (section 3).

**One command at a time.** A cell holds up to four commands, and the grid shows one: the one in the
slot K1 picks (below), the first while K1 is on the volume. The 2-track view shows it after the volume,
with a pip under it for each command the cell holds (the one shown bright), and names the slot above
the column (`VOL`, `CMD1`-`CMD4`); a line under the cursor's cell marks the field K1 picked. The note
editor shows all four.

**A command takes 3 characters:**
- a tracker effect: its letter and value (`A04`);
- an XM volume column's effect: `~`, FastTracker 2's sign, and its digit (`~-4` a volume slide down);
- PT/FM's own: its name (`ACC`, `SLD`, `A+S`);
- every other command: its group's sign and its value, so a sweep shows as its values climbing:
  - `*40` an instrument parameter, the sign in the instrument's engine colour;
  - `=70` the track's mixer;
  - `#3C` the FX, the sign in the effect's colour.

  Which parameter it is, the note editor says.

**Commands never change what you are editing.** A song's `*`, `=` and `#` commands change what they
name as they play, except what is being edited right now; those commands are skipped, and nothing
jumps when the page opens (what last played stays until your hand or the next command that may act):
- **FX, EFFECTS page:** every `#` command (knobs, ON / OFF).
- **FX, MIXER page:** the `=` commands of the mixer shown (the song's, or the jam's).
- **JAM:** the `=` commands of the lane or live track K1-K4 edit.
- **An instrument's edit page:** the `*` commands for that instrument; its notes play as edited,
  without the parameters commands gave its tracks.
- **A song's properties (GLO on SONGS), or the jam's:** its `Fxx` commands; while they are open it
  plays at its own TEMPO, TICKS A ROW, ROWS A BEAT and SWING, as they change.

Tracker effects stay music, not edits: `8xx` and the pan slides still pan a track while the MIXER is
open.

Once the page is left, commands act again: the song's next command for a knob you turned moves it.
The FX page and the MIXER show what plays: a value a command moved off the setup's is drawn in the effect's colour
(on the MIXER: white, `BY THE SONG`).

**Drawing:**
- **The focus row** (the cursor's) is larger, with a 1-px line above and below. The cursor's cell is a
  bright box.
  - **2-track view:** the notes are in B (12 × 24 bold), and the gaps in that row shrink to 4 px to
    make room: 2 × (36 + 72 + 4) + 12 = 236 px.
  - **4- and 8-track views:** B is too wide (8 notes in B take 288 px), so the row is only taller,
    with more space around the text: 16 px in the 8-track view (12-px rows), 20 px in the 4-track
    view (16-px rows).
- **The playing row,** when this pattern plays, sits on a deep blue band.
- **The view never follows the playback,** except during a realtime take, when it follows the row
  being recorded.
- **Row numbers** are hex, `00`-`FF` (section 3). Beat rows are cyan.
- **In a FUSION pattern** the row numbers are hex too; a row is the note it plays (`C-4` is row
  `3C`), so the rows of the Cs (`0C`, `18`, ... `78`) are the cyan ones, and the note editor names the
  row's note (`ROW 3C C-4`).

**Scrolling** (nothing wraps):
- **Vertical:** the cursor moves a row at a time. Passing the last row of a page shows the next page.
- **Horizontal:**
  - The 2-track view pages by 2 tracks.
  - The 4- and 8-track views keep one track of look-ahead. Moving onto the last visible track, when
    more follow, shifts the view by 2 tracks, and the cursor appears to jump back.

**Writing (REC):**
- **REC switches writing.** With REC off the keys only play the current instrument, so sounds and
  notes can be tried freely. With REC on (REC lit), the keys write.
  - REC gates only the note keys in the grid. K1-K4, LFO, the SEL menu and the note editor change
    cells whether REC is on or off: each is a deliberate edit.
  - **REC while the EDITOR's loop plays: overdub.** The loop goes on, and the keys write in realtime
    at the playing row, as in a take (below): on the cursor's track, a chord spreading, snapped to the
    nearest row, a note off on release. The view follows the playing row, and the cursor sits on
    the row a key pressed now would land on, in a take too.
    - REC off: back to listening; the loop goes on.
    - PLAY: the loop stops and REC stays on, so the next PLAY starts a take.
  - **REC while a song plays:** the song pauses (it keeps its place), since the pattern shown may not
    be the one playing. Writing starts in silence.
- **Step by step** (REC on, stopped):
  - a key writes its note at the cursor, with the current instrument and KEYS VOLUME (6.9), so it
    plays back as it was heard;
  - the note sounds;
  - the cursor moves down by the edit step (GLO; 0 = stay).
- **The instrument** comes with the note: a key writes it with the current instrument (PRESETS). No
  knob changes it.
- **K1-K4: the cell's volume and commands.** They do not advance the cursor.

  | Knob | Does |
  |---|---|
  | K1 | picks the slot: the volume, or command 1-4, up to one past the last command (to add one) |
  | K2 | the slot's command: no command, the tracker effects (each E and X one an entry of its own), XM's volume-column ones, PT/FM's own, then the instrument's parameters (`*`), the track's mixer (`=`) and the FX (`#`). A code another slot of the cell holds is skipped: each at most once. Back to no command removes it, and the later ones move up |
  | K3, K4 | its value, a digit each: K3 the first, K4 the second; a one-digit command (E, X, `~`) takes K4. In the three groups K3 picks which parameter, mixer value or FX knob (for `*`, from the table of the track's instrument's engine) and K4 sets its value |

  - **On the volume,** K3 steps by 16 and K4 by 1. Past `7F` the cell has none: the instrument's
    default.
  - No command has more than two values: a command is a code and one byte.
- **LFO (CLR):** clears the cell and advances. On an empty cell it writes a note off (`===`).

**EDIT: the note editor.**
- **The cell, large:**
  - the note in L;
  - in B: the instrument's ID and name, the volume in words (`50 %`), and each command's name and
    value in words (`SLIDE`, `ACCENT`, `ARPEGGIO 0 4 7`): everything the cell holds.
- **The keys** change the note, with the current instrument: it sounds, and the cursor does not
  advance.
- **K1-K4** work as in the grid, with their names shown; the slot K1 picked is boxed.
- **SELECT / ALGORITHM** go to the neighbouring cells without leaving the note editor.
- **EDIT** closes it, keeping the changes; **HOME** closes it, dropping them: every cell changed
  since EDIT as it was (1.2).

**GLO: the pattern's properties.**
- TRACKS (1-16), ROWS (1-256), EDIT STEP (0-16).
- A change that would drop notes asks first.

**ENV: the pattern list.**
- **Each line:**
  - the number, `00`-`FF`;
  - tracks × rows;
  - a mini map (notes per track, as dots);
  - how many songs use it (an unused pattern is dim).
- **ENV again** opens the highlighted pattern.
- **PLAY** plays the highlighted pattern on its own, looped from its start (wherever the list opens:
  the EDITOR, a song's order, a FUSION slot). Whatever played pauses and keeps its place. Moving the
  highlight moves the preview; PLAY again stops it; picking a pattern or HOME ends it, and PLAY then
  resumes what played before.
- **SEL menu:** NEW, CLONE, MOVE, DELETE. MOVE floats the pattern to another free number (1.5). MOVE
  and DELETE ask about references (section 8).

**SEL: blocks.**
- **Hold SEL** and turn SELECT / ALGORITHM to mark a block of rows × tracks, like Shift and the arrow
  keys.
- **Tap SEL** for the menu:
  - COPY, CUT, PASTE, PASTE MIX (into empty cells only), CLEAR;
  - MOVE, SWAP: the marked block floats with the cursor; SEL puts it down. MOVE leaves its old place
    empty; SWAP puts what was at the new place into the old one. HOME cancels;
  - INSERT ROW, DELETE ROW;
  - TRANSPOSE (with K1), INTERPOLATE: a sweep. On each track a command that the block's first and last
    rows both hold goes on every row between, its value stepping from one to the other; else the
    volumes step;
  - UNDO.
- **The clipboard** stays across patterns until power-off.

**ARP: row preview.**
- **Tap:** the cursor's row repeats at the song's tempo, in a rhythm.
  - ARP held + SELECT picks the rhythm, a row a step, `#` played and `_` a rest, round and round:
    `#`, `#_`, `#__`, `#___` (the first), `##_`, `###_`, `##__`, `#_##_`, and two slow ones for long
    sounds, `#` and 7 or 15 rests. The footer shows it.
  - Moving the cursor moves the preview.
- **How long a note rings:**
  - to the end of its row, if the next row would end it (a new note or a note off on its track);
  - otherwise through the rest.
- **Jumps and pattern breaks** are ignored.

**Realtime takes** (REC on). A take is one pass from the cursor's row to the end of the pattern:
- **Starting a take:**
  - **PLAY tap:** the take starts when PLAY is let go, after the METRONOME's COUNT-IN (OFF, 1 or 2
    bars; 6.6).
  - **PLAY held + a key:** the take starts with that note, on the cursor's row. PLAY can then be let
    go. A chord pressed this way lands on that row.
  - **PLAY held past the hold delay** shows an overlay with both ways: `LET GO: START THE TAKE`,
    `PRESS A KEY: START ON THAT NOTE`. A quick tap never shows it.
- **How a take records:**
  - Notes are quantized to the nearest row.
  - A note goes to the cursor's track, unless an earlier note is still held there; then it goes to
    the next track with no note held. So a melody stays on one track, and a chord spreads.
  - Releasing a key writes a note off. A note off that would land on its own note's row goes to the
    next row.
  - Existing cells stay unless a note lands on them (overdub).
- **At the end of the pattern** the take stops. REC stays on (back to step by step), and the cursor
  returns to the row the take started from, ready to hear it or to record it again.
- **PLAY during a take** stops it early.
- **REC blinks** during a take or an overdub, and the header shows ●.

**PLAY with REC off** loops the shown pattern (section 7).

### 6.3 INSTRUMENTS

**Browsing:**
- **The top:**
  - a line naming the cartridge: ID, name, type, slots used;
  - its slots in two columns of 8 (index, name). The page is N/M.
- **The bottom:** the current instrument in detail:

  | Instrument | Shown as |
  |---|---|
  | DX7 | operators |
  | Juno-106 | sliders |
  | TB-303 | knobs |
  | sample instrument | waveform |
  | FUSION | its pattern, as a small map |

**Navigating:**
- **The cursor (SELECT / ALGORITHM) is the current instrument.**
- **PRESETS** steps one slot at a time, turning the pages, and wraps.
- **K1** turns the cartridges, and wraps.

**Listening:**
- **The keys** play the current instrument.
- **ARP** repeats the last note in the chosen rhythm (ARP held + SELECT, as the EDITOR's row
  preview's: `#___`, `##_` ...), so both hands are free for the knobs. Keys pressed together take
  turns, in the order they were pressed; a key pressed with none held starts the turns anew.

**EDIT: edit mode** (EDIT lit). The list gives way to two panels: the parameters, and a picture of the
instrument that follows the cursor.
- **Patches:** the parameters below, the picture above:

  | Engine | Picture |
  |---|---|
  | DX7 | the algorithm, and the selected operator's envelope |
  | Juno-106 | its envelope and filter |
  | TB-303 | knobs and envelope |
- **Sample instruments:** the parameters above, the waveform below with its markers. The parameters:
  SAMPLE, START, END, LOOP (OFF / FORWARD / PING-PONG), LOOP START, LOOP END, BASE NOTE, TUNE (in
  1/128 of a semitone), VOLUME (a module's default volume, 0-64), LEVEL, and on a second page an
  ENVELOPE as a DX7 operator's (R1-R4, L1-L4), drawn above it.
  - START and END make an instrument from a cut of a sample.
  - The loop repeats while the key is held; at release the sound goes on from where it is to END,
    while the envelope releases.
  - **RELEASE** sits on the first page with the loop (K4 of its row): the envelope's R4, the same
    value, shown as a time: how long the tail (the rest of the loop and of the sample) takes to fade
    to silence (-60 dB, from full) once the key is let go. The sample's speed stays.
    - Right is longer. The quickest is 4 ms (R4 99), instant to the ear and without a click; the
      default 33 ms (R4 80).
    - **It goes as far as the tail's own length:** START (with a loop, LOOP START) to END at the base
      note. A longer fade would never be heard: the sample ends first.
    - Neighbouring rates that fade alike are one step. The times are the envelope's, measured: R4 70
      116 ms, 60 309 ms, 50 0.9 s, 40 3 s, 30 8.5 s, 20 30 s, 10 79 s; under 7 it never fades
      (`ENDLESS`, set on the ENVELOPE page).
- **The controls in edit mode:**

  | Control | Does |
  |---|---|
  | K1-K4 | edit the group at the cursor (a DX7 operator's 4 rates) |
  | ALGORITHM (or SEQ) | turns the edit pages (DX7: OPERATORS, ENVELOPES, PITCH / LFO, VOICE) |
  | PRESETS | still changes the instrument; editing goes on with the new one (HOME drops its changes too) |
  | LFO | resets the value at the cursor |
  | SEL on NAME | text entry (1.7) at once; on the other rows SEL opens the menu |
  | SEL on PATTERN (FUSION) | the pattern in the EDITOR, locked, at once (the menu also has EDIT PATTERN) |
  | ENV on SAMPLE / PATTERN | the cartridge's samples / the pattern list |
  | EDIT | leaves edit mode, keeping the changes |
  | HOME | leaves edit mode, dropping them (1.2): every instrument edited since EDIT as it was |
- **LEVEL:** every instrument has one (dB). SEL → AUTO LEVEL measures and sets it.

**SEL menu** (the slots are fixed):
- COPY, PASTE;
- MOVE, SWAP (the instrument floats over the slots, 1.5: SELECT one at a time, ALGORITHM by 8; MOVE
  only to empty slots, and not at all in a full cartridge), DELETE: these ask about the cells that
  use the instrument (section 8);
- INIT, RENAME, AUTO LEVEL, UNDO.
- **In edit mode** only what changes the instrument itself: EDIT PATTERN (FUSION), COPY, PASTE,
  INIT, RENAME, AUTO LEVEL, UNDO. MOVE, SWAP and DELETE move slots and their references, so they
  wait until edit mode is left.
- PASTE and INIT over a used slot change its sound, as editing does; the cells keep playing that
  slot.
- SEL held + SELECT marks several slots.

**ENV: the cartridges,** while browsing, on every type. An overlay listing each cartridge's ID, name,
type (in its colour) and slots used.
- **ENV again** shows the highlighted cartridge.
- **SEL menu:** NEW (pick its type), CLONE, MOVE, DELETE, RENAME, AUTO LEVEL ALL.
  - NEW and CLONE take the number after the last cartridge's, so the new one is at the end of the
    list (once `FE` is taken, the first free number).
  - MOVE floats it to another free number (1.5).

**GLO: the cartridge's properties:** its NAME, with its type and the slots used shown beside it.

**Sampler cartridges:**
- **What they hold:** their samples (audio comes from the PC; the device does not record), and up to
  64 sample instruments that use them. One sample can serve several instruments.
- **The samples list:** on the SAMPLE parameter, **ENV** opens the cartridge's samples (name, length,
  rate, waveform).
  - The keys play the highlighted sample; ENV picks it.
  - SEL → DELETE is the only change to a sample. It warns first when instruments use it
    (`DELETE SAMPLE? 3 INSTRUMENTS USE IT AND ARE DELETED TOO`), and deletes them with it.
  - Deleting an instrument never deletes its sample; CLEAN UP removes samples no instrument uses.

**FUSION cartridges** (a new type):
- **What a slot holds:** a reference to a pattern of the shared pool (`00`-`FF`), as a song
  position does.
  - **EDIT on a FUSION instrument** opens its edit page, as on every type: PATTERN, NAME and LEVEL,
    with a map of the pattern above them. On PATTERN:
    - **ENV** opens the pattern list; ENV again makes the highlighted pattern the slot's. In it the
      keys play the highlighted pattern as this slot would, a row a key, so patterns can be tried
      before one is picked;
    - **K1** steps through the patterns there are;
    - **SEL** opens the pattern in the EDITOR, locked (below), at once.
  - **EDIT on an empty FUSION slot** makes a new pattern of 128 rows (the next free number) and opens
    it. Its EDIT STEP is 0: writing a note keeps the cursor on the key's row.
    - **First it asks:** `FILL IT WITH 01:00 TINE EP?`, offering the last instrument picked that is
      not a FUSION slot. **K1** offers another cartridge's (its first instrument), **SELECT** the next
      or previous instrument, and the keys play the one offered. **OCT+ (FILL)** writes that
      instrument's own note on each row `0C`-`7F`, on track `00` (`C-4` on row `3C`), so the slot
      plays as that instrument from the start, ready for layers; **OCT- (EMPTY)** leaves the pattern
      empty; HOME makes nothing.
  - **INIT** empties the slot: it refers to nothing and is silent.
- **Which row plays:** row *r* plays for MIDI note *r*. `C-4` (note 60) is row `3C`.
  - The keys at OCT 0 play rows `35` (F-3) to `4F` (G-5).
  - Rows below and above are notes too, reached with OCT and from song patterns. A pattern of 128
    rows (`00`-`7F`) covers every note.
- **The rows as a workspace:**
  - Any row can hold a row kept for later, and SEL → MOVE or SWAP brings it to the note where it
    should play.
  - A kept row is still a note, so it sounds if OCT or a song pattern reaches it.
- **How it plays:** a key plays every cell of its row; letting go of the key releases them.
- **Voices** are shared. Rows with many notes take voices from other sounds; keeping that in hand is
  up to the musician.
- **SEL on PATTERN** on its edit page opens the EDITOR on the pattern, locked:
  - EDIT stays lit;
  - the screen keys and FX tap are refused, and EDIT flashes;
  - **with REC off the keys play this FUSION**, as it is being written, whatever the current
    instrument; the rows they play light up (green) in the grid. With REC on they write, with the
    current instrument, as everywhere;
  - HOME tap returns to the edit page.
- **The same pattern** can also be edited from the EDITOR in the usual way, unlocked.
- **No nesting:** a FUSION instrument can be written anywhere, a FUSION's own pattern included, but
  when a FUSION plays its row, a cell naming a FUSION (itself or another) is silent. So a FUSION
  never plays more than its own row's cells: no chains, no loops.
- **Drum kits:** a FUSION instrument can be a kit, with a different sound on each key.

**PLAY** pauses and resumes what played last (section 7).

### 6.4 PLAYBACK

- **A view of what plays.** It shows the playing pattern and scrolls with the play position. It has no
  cursor, and nothing on it can be edited.
- **The controls:**

  | Control | Does |
  |---|---|
  | SEQ | 8, 4 or 2 tracks |
  | ALGORITHM | scrolls the tracks sideways when they do not all fit |
  | SELECT | plays row by row. The first click, either way, pauses playback and plays the current row again. Then each click forward plays the next row, and each click back the previous one, as if it came next (no reset, no replay from the pattern's start). PLAY resumes from there |
  | K1 | another position of the song: playing, the jump comes as the current row ends, as a Bxx would (clicks within one row add up); paused, PLAY resumes from that position's first row |
  | GLO | the view's options: SCROLL ROWS (the playing row stays in the middle) or PAGES (the highlight moves, the pages flip) |
  | EDIT | the EDITOR on the playing pattern, with the cursor on the playing row; HOME tap brings you back |
  | keys | play the current instrument over the song |
  | HOME + white keys | mute tracks |
- **The header's page** is the song position. While the EDITOR's pattern loop plays, there is no
  song position: the header shows the pattern (`PAT 0C`) and the footer `PAT 0C ROW 1F`.

### 6.5 JAM

A scratch loop for playing live. SAVE keeps it, like any change; SEL also stores it in a pattern. It
has its own FX setup and metronome (6.6), so it sounds the same whatever song is current.

**The loop:** 1, 2 or 4 bars. GLO sets LENGTH, TEMPO, SWING, QUANTIZE and KIT.

**Two parts; SEQ switches the view between them,** and so does ALGORITHM: DRUMS' bars and LIVE are
one strip, LIVE past the last bar.
- **DRUMS** (a step grid):
  - **The kit is any cartridge,** picked with ENV (its first slot) or in GLO with its first slot: lane
    *k* plays slot *first + k*.
    There are 8 lanes.
    - A lane plays its slot at C-4; a sample instrument plays at its BASE NOTE, so a drum sounds as
      recorded.
    - Usually it is a sampler cartridge of drum samples, but any type works: DX7, Juno-106, TB-303,
      FUSION.
    - No special kit format is needed.
  - **Black keys** pick a lane and play it.
  - **The 16 white keys** are the selected lane's 16 steps of the selected bar: lit = on. An empty
    step that starts a beat (keys 1, 5, 9, 13) is dim, to find your way along the keys.
    - Here the step keys act on release, like buttons: a tap toggles the step.
    - **A step held + K1** sets its level; **+ K2** its ratchet: the hits that divide the jam's TICKS
      A ROW, so each lands on a tick (`X1`, `X2`, `X3`, `X6` at 6). Turning a knob while a step is
      held never toggles it.
  - **SELECT** picks the lane; **ALGORITHM** picks the bar, and past the last one goes to LIVE.
- **LIVE** (4 tracks):

  | Control | Does |
  |---|---|
  | SELECT | picks the track |
  | ALGORITHM back | DRUMS, its last bar |
  | PRESETS | the track's instrument |
  | ENV | the cartridges: the track plays the picked one's first instrument |
  | keys | play it |
  | REC | records into the loop: overdub, quantized |
  | LFO held | erases the track's notes as the loop passes them (with keys held: only those notes) |
  | ARP, no keys held | the arpeggiator on / off; ARP held + SELECT picks its mode |
  | keys held, then ARP | latch: those notes keep sounding as if still held, and can be let go of; the arpeggiator plays them too |
  | ARP held + LFO | lets the latched notes go |

**Both parts:**
- **PLAY** starts or pauses the loop. The metronome (FX) keeps time.
- **ENV:** the cartridges, as on INSTRUMENTS: on DRUMS for the kit, on LIVE for the selected track.
- **SEL menu:** STORE IT IN A PATTERN, LOAD A PATTERN; the pattern list follows, and SEL picks.
  - Store: a new or existing pattern to keep the jam in. Each lane or track used becomes a track: 8 +
    4 ≤ 16.
  - Load: a pattern into the jam.
- **K1-K4:** the selected lane's or track's level, pan and sends (settled in the playground).
- **Muting:** HOME + white keys 1-8 mute the drum lanes, and 9-12 the four live tracks.

### 6.6 FX

**SEQ** switches between two pages, EFFECTS and MIXER. The header's page shows which (1/2, 2/2).

**The FX setup belongs to the song; the jam has one of its own.**
- **What it covers:** the chain's order, ROUTING, every effect's knobs and on / off, the mixer, and the
  METRONOME: different songs can count differently.
- **When it applies:** starting a song loads its setup. A pattern played from the EDITOR uses the
  current song's; the jam uses its own.
- **Which setup the FX screen edits:** in JAM, the jam's; elsewhere the playing song's, so that what
  you change is what you hear. With nothing playing, the current song's.
- **CLONE on SONGS** copies the setup with the song.
- **Not part of it:** the punch-in effects, which are performance only.

**EFFECTS:**
- **The top:** the chain in its order, one line an effect: its name in its colour, ON / OFF, an
  activity meter.
- **The bottom:** the selected effect's picture and its knobs.
- **The controls:**

  | Control | Does |
  |---|---|
  | SELECT | picks the effect |
  | ALGORITHM | the knob page (for effects with more than 4 knobs) |
  | K1-K4 | the effect's knobs |
  | OCT+ / OCT- | switch the effect on / off, so it can be set first and switched on after; the selected row shows the one that changes it (`OCT+ ON`, `OCT- OFF`) |
  | LFO | resets the last knob turned |
  | SEL | the menu: MOVE (reorders the chain), RESET (the effect's defaults), UNDO |
  | GLO | the FX properties: ROUTING (below) |

**Routing** (GLO → ROUTING): two models; switching between them loses nothing.

- **GLOBAL:** one chain on the whole mix.
  - Every effect can be moved anywhere in it; only LIMITER stays last.
  - The MIXER has level, pan and the duck amount.
- **SENDS:** CHORUS, DELAY and REVERB leave the chain and become shared sends.
  - There is still one of each, and each track sets how much of its sound goes into each (the
    MIXER's send columns). A pad can be wet while the drums stay dry.
  - Their returns join the chain at a movable item, `◆ SENDS`:
    - the effects above it act on the dry mix only (LO-FI there leaves the reverb tail clean);
    - the effects below it act on everything.
- **What a switch keeps:**
  - Switching to GLOBAL puts CHORUS, DELAY and REVERB where `◆ SENDS` was. Switching back takes them
    out, and `◆ SENDS` takes the place of the first of them.
  - The tracks' send amounts are kept while unused.
- **Cost:**
  - The effects cost the same in both models.
  - Sends add the routing: about 6 multiply-adds a voice a frame (estimated: 1-2 % CPU at 16 voices)
    and ~6 KB RAM.
  - A send no track uses is skipped.

```
GLOBAL                      SENDS (the same setup, switched)
  GATE                        GATE
  FILTER                      FILTER
  LO-FI                       LO-FI      <- dry mix only
  CHORUS                      ◆ SENDS    <- CHORUS, DELAY, REVERB join
  DELAY                       DRIVE      <- everything
  REVERB                      LIMITER    (last)
  DRIVE
  LIMITER    (last)
```

**The set:**

| Effect | Knobs: values (default) | Picture |
|---|---|---|
| GATE | PATTERN `00`-`1F`, 16 built in (`00`-`0F`) and 16 of yours (`00`); RATE 1/8, 1/16, 1/32, 1/8T, 1/16T (1/16); DEPTH (100 %); SMOOTH (20 %) | 16 steps with ratchets and the playhead |
| FILTER | CUT LP 40 … OFF … HP 3F, low-pass to the left, high-pass to the right (OFF); RESO (30 %) | response curve |
| DRIVE | DRIVE (30 %); TONE (50 %) | transfer curve |
| LO-FI | BITS 2-16 (8); RATE 1-44 kHz (22); DUST (20 %) | stepped wave |
| CHORUS | MODE I, II, I+II: Juno-style (I); RATE (30 %); DEPTH (50 %); MIX (50 %) | the modulated delay |
| DELAY | TIME 1/32, 1/16T, 1/16, 1/8T, 1/16., 1/8, 1/4T, 1/8., 1/4, 1/2T, 1/4., 1/2, up to 1 s, with PING-PONG up to 0.5 s: a longer one plays the longest that fits (1/8.); FEEDBACK (40 %); TONE, each repeat's lowpass, up to ~13 kHz (60 %); PING-PONG OFF, ON (OFF); MIX (30 %). Echoes are mono (one line, the files proposal's RAM); PING-PONG puts them left and right in turn | echoes on a beat grid |
| REVERB | a plate, Dattorro's: SIZE, its decay, about 1.3 s to 11 s to −60 dB (60 %); DAMP, the tail's lowpass (40 %); PRE-DELAY 0-100 ms (10); MIX (25 %) | decay curve |
| LIMITER | CEILING −12 to 0 dB (−1); RELEASE 10-1000 ms in 10s (100) | gain reduction |
| DUCK | SOURCE a track, `00`-`0F` (`00`); DEPTH (60 %); RELEASE 20-1000 ms in 10s (150) | the source's level and the gain it takes off |
| METRONOME | TEMPO FOLLOW or 30-300 BPM (FOLLOW); BEATS A BAR 1-16 (4); SOUND CLICK, BEEP, WOOD, COWBELL (CLICK); LEVEL (60 %); COUNT-IN OFF, 1 or 2 bars, before a take or a jam recording (1 bar) | the bar's beats |

A knob shown with only a default runs 0-100 %. The FX setup keeps each knob as its step from the
bottom of its range (the files proposal, section 7).

- **DUCK and the METRONOME are not part of the chain.** EFFECTS lists them after it, apart, and MOVE
  does not reach them.
- **In SENDS,** the MIX knob of CHORUS, DELAY and REVERB becomes their return level.

**DUCK** works on the tracks before they are mixed, so its source is never lowered with the rest:
- **The source** is a track. Its level is followed, rising at once and falling over RELEASE.
- **Every other track** is lowered by that level × DEPTH × the track's own amount, the MIXER's DCK
  row (none by default, as the files proposal keeps the mixer). The bass and the pad can duck fully
  while the hats do not.
- **The sends** take a track's sound after ducking, so a ducked pad's reverb ducks with it.
- **The cost:** following the source adds one addition a frame for each of its voices, and each
  track's gain is updated once a block, eased over the block so it never clicks: well under 1 % CPU.

**GATE:**
- **A pattern** is 16 steps, each OFF, ON, `X2`, `X3` or `X4` (ratchets).
- **RATE:** 1/8, 1/16, 1/32, 1/8T or 1/16T.
- **The patterns:** 16 built in, and 16 of your own.
  - **EDIT on GATE:** the white keys edit the steps; a tap cycles a step through the values. EDIT
    again keeps the changes; HOME drops them (1.2): the gate patterns and GATE's knobs as they were,
    a built-in pattern's copy among yours included.
  - **ENV on GATE:** the pattern list.

**METRONOME:**
- It is mixed after the effects and never takes a voice.
- TEMPO FOLLOW uses the song's or the jam's tempo. A number sets its own, so it works with nothing
  playing.

**Punch-in effects:** FX held + a white key runs that effect on the whole mix while the key is held,
locked to the tempo.

| Key | Effect | Key | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | loop 1/4 | 9 | low-pass sweep |
| 2 | loop 1/8 | 10 | high-pass sweep |
| 3 | loop 1/16 | 11 | bit crush |
| 4 | loop 1/32 | 12 | gate 1/16 |
| 5 | stutter 1/16T | 13 | gate 1/32 |
| 6 | reverse | 14 | echo throw |
| 7 | tape stop | 15 | reverb swell |
| 8 | half speed | 16 | vinyl stop |

- **Vinyl stop** works like a hand on a spinning record:
  - **pressed:** the speed falls to zero in a short time (about 0.2 s, tuned in the playground);
  - **held:** silence;
  - **let go:** the sound spins back up in about 0.1 s, straight into the song. The song keeps time
    underneath, so it returns on the beat.
  - **Tape stop** (key 7) is its slow sibling: it winds down over about a beat, as an effect rather
    than a cut.
- **Some punch-ins record the last beat:** the loops, the stutter, reverse, tape stop, half speed and
  vinyl stop.
  - At 60 BPM a 1/4 note is 1 s: about 176 KB in 16-bit stereo; at 120 BPM, 88 KB.
  - **The buffer holds the last 0.5 s in stereo** (88 KB; the files proposal, section 11): a beat at
    120 BPM and faster. Where a beat does not fit (slower tempos), these punch-ins use the longest
    note value that does: a 1/4 loop becomes a 1/8 loop.
- **The set is a first pass.** It is taken into the playground as it is, and trimmed or changed once
  the FX screen has been tried and the costs are measured.
- *(optional)* **FX held + black keys** switch the chain's effects on / off directly.

**MIXER:** a grid of all 16 tracks, with the tracks across and their values down. The track count
belongs to each pattern, so the mixer always has all 16; a track no pattern uses is dim.

```
FX  1/1                    ▶ ▌▐██
 MIXER
     00  01  02  03  04  05  06  07
LVL  ▮   ▮   ▮   ▮   ▮   ▮   ▮   ▮
PAN  <   ·   >   ·   <   >   ·   ·
DCK  S   ▮   ▮   ·   ▮   ▮   ·   ·
CHO  ·   ▮   ·   ·   ·   ·   ·   ·
DLY  ·   ▮   ▮   ·   ·   ·   ·   ·
REV  ▮   ▮   ·   ·   ·   ▮   ·   ·
```

- **The values:** LEVEL, PAN and DUCK (DCK: how much the track ducks; `S` marks DUCK's source). With
  ROUTING at SENDS, also the sends: CHORUS, DELAY, REVERB.
  - The DCK row is shown only while DUCK is on.
- **Muted tracks** are drawn dark. Muting is HOME + white key, as everywhere.
- **The controls:**
  - ALGORITHM picks the track; 8 show at a time, and tracks `08`-`0F` scroll into view.
  - SELECT picks the value; K1 changes it; LFO resets it.
  - The playground may also give K1-K4 a value each.

### 6.7 VISUAL

**SELECT** turns the visualisers as pages; **SEQ** cycles them:

| Visualiser | What |
|---|---|
| **VOICES** | a scope for each voice |
| **SCOPE** | the master output as an oscilloscope: left and right, triggered on a rising zero crossing |
| **WAVE** | the master's waveform scrolling by, as peaks over the last several seconds |

| *(suggested)* **LEVELS** | a meter for each track |

### 6.10 Help: HOME + LFO, "LOOK FOR ONSWERS"

A short manual for the screen you are on, instead of a manual screen.
- **Opening it:** HOME + LFO, on any screen and at any depth.
  - It opens as an overlay. The header reads `LOOK FOR ONSWERS` and the page (`LOOK FOR ONSWERS
    1/6`) in place of the screen's name; the screen is named by the first page's title
    (`EDITOR: WRITE PATTERNS`).
  - The grace period (1.2) makes sure the combination never clears anything.
- **What it shows, for the screen it was opened on:** the screen's controls (knobs, buttons, and what
  held buttons do there), then the shared pages. The text of every page is in section 13.
- **How a page looks:** up to 17 lines of T, 40 characters each, colour-coded as the screens are.
  - **By the line's shape:**
    - the first line is the page's title, in cyan; so is any line with no `:` that is not indented
      (`BLOCKS`, `SAMPLE SOUNDS`);
    - every other line is one pair: what you do, then `:`, then what happens. The text is light
      grey; a line indented by two spaces goes on from the one above.
  - **By the words:**
    - **every key name is yellow,** wherever it stands, so a key reads as a key at a glance: buttons
      (`HOME`, `OCT+`, `SEL`), knobs (`SELECT`, `K1`), black keys (`OP3`, `POLY`), and `KEYS`,
      `WHITE KEY`, `BLACK KEY`;
    - a reading in brackets, `(SHOW ELSE, QUICK)`, is muted, in dark grey: the label stays the thing
      to look for;
    - a screen's name is in its group's colour (`EDITOR`, `FX`, `JAM`);
    - the word `RED` is red;
    - engine names stay plain here: yellow belongs to the keys on these pages.
  - **How it is stored, as a terminal does it:** a few reserved tokens only set the colour for the
    characters that follow, until the next such token or the line's end. A colour token costs one
    byte.
  - **Marked in the source, not guessed:** a word list would also colour the verb "play" or the
    MONO in "MONO FOR THE SPEAKER". So the source marks them: a key as `{HOME}`, a screen as
    `[EDITOR]`. Braces and square brackets are not in the fonts, so they can never be meant as
    text; the build turns them into colour tokens. Section 13 shows the text without the marks.
- **The help names only what works.** Keys kept for later (OP6, the blank key) are never mentioned.
- **Moving and leaving:** SELECT turns its pages (N/M in the header); HOME closes it, as always.
  - The first page of every help ends with `PRESS HOME TO GET BACK`. The help draws that line
    itself, so a first page keeps its last two lines free.
- **What is shared by all screens** (the global keys, the control rules of section 1, saving, the
  themes) sits on the last pages of every screen's help. On STATUS those pages come first instead
  (6.8).
- **Readings** (section 2): a button whose label reads wrong is named with its reading once in a
  screen's help, at its first mention in page order: `ENV (EXPLORE NAMED VAULTS): PATTERNS`. Every
  later mention, on that page or the next ones, uses the label alone.
  - The shared pages are stored once. The build applies the first-mention rule in each help's own
    page order and records, for each screen, which buttons it has named: the shared pages give a
    reading only where that screen has not. On STATUS the shared pages come first, so they carry
    the readings there, and STATUS's own page uses the labels alone.
  - LFO's reading, `LEAVE FIELD OPEN`, is given only where LFO clears, deletes or resets something.
    `LOOK FOR ONSWERS` is the help's own header.
  - The screen keys go by theirs on the shared SCREENS page: `OP1 (OPEN PAGE 1): SONGS`.
  - Every line has to fit its 40 characters with any reading it may get; the build checks it.
- **The help is stored compressed, by a tokenizer.**
  - **Its size:** 28 pages (section 13), about 12 KB as plain text.
  - **The method: byte-pair encoding,** as language models tokenize text, trained by the build on
    the help's own text:
    - it starts from the characters (the fonts' ~58, the line break and the indent);
    - it merges the commonest pair of symbols into a new token, again and again, until the table is
      full; common words and phrases (`HOLD `, `SELECT: `, `HOME COMES BACK HERE`) become single
      tokens;
    - the table stores each token as its two halves (2 bytes a token with one-byte tokens, 3 with
      12-bit ones), never the spelled-out words;
    - a token is one byte (a table of 256) or 12 bits (up to 4,096). The build tries several table
      sizes and keeps the smallest total of text and table. For 12 KB, a table of 256 is likely
      best: about 5 KB in all.
  - **Unpacking** expands each token through its halves with a small stack, for the one page on
    screen, into a line buffer: no RAM beyond that, and no time worth measuring.
  - **The readings:** on a screen's own pages the build writes them in, since it knows the page
    order. On the shared pages, a button that may need one is a token of its own, and the help adds
    the reading when that screen's pages have not named the button.
  - **Written for the tokenizer:** the text uses few words, always in the same phrasings (`HOLD X +
    Y:`, `HOME COMES BACK HERE`, `X: ON / OFF`). When two wordings say the same, the one that repeats
    an existing phrase wins.
  - **Part of the build toolchain:** the help is written as plain text in the repository, and a tool
    in `tools/` packs it on every firmware build:
    - it checks every page (40 characters, 17 lines, the fonts' characters, the readings' room);
    - it adds the readings and the colour tokens, trains the tokenizer and writes the packed help and
      its table as a C source for the firmware;
    - a host test unpacks every page with the firmware's own code and compares it with the text.

## 7. The transport

- **PLAY** (acts on press) plays or pauses. There is one transport, and one thing plays at a time:
  the current song, the EDITOR's pattern loop, or the jam's loop.
  - **On SONGS, the EDITOR and JAM, PLAY plays the screen's own thing:** the current song, the shown
    pattern looped from the cursor's row, the jam's loop (6.5).
    - If something else is playing, PLAY switches at once. What was playing pauses and keeps its
      place.
    - If the screen's own thing is already playing, PLAY pauses it.
    - On the EDITOR, a song that is playing the shown pattern hands over to the loop on the same row,
      so the switch is seamless.
    - On the EDITOR with REC on, PLAY records a take instead (6.2).
  - **On every other screen, PLAY pauses and resumes what played last** (the song or the pattern
    loop; the jam plays only in JAM, section 5), so on FX or VISUAL it toggles what you hear. If nothing has played yet, it plays the current song.
  - Each of the three continues from where it was paused, else starts from the beginning.
  - **No song to play** (every song deleted, or the song has no positions): PLAY is refused. It
    blinks quickly and says `NO SONG TO PLAY`.
- **Tempo:**
  - **A song starts at its TEMPO** (a property, SONGS → GLO).
  - **A TEMPO command** (a global CMD, from any track) changes it from its row on.
  - **`TEMPO 00`** returns to the song's own tempo.
  - **A pattern played on its own** (from the EDITOR) uses the current song's tempo, and each pass
    of the loop starts at it again.
- **HOME + PLAY** rewinds what plays, or played last, to its start: the song, the pattern or the
  jam's loop. If it is playing, it restarts from there.
- **PLAY held** past the hold delay stops: its press has paused (or started) what plays, and holding
  on takes it back to its start, so the next PLAY plays it from the beginning (on the EDITOR, from
  the cursor's row, as always there). On the EDITOR with REC on, PLAY held is the take's instead (6.2).
- **At the song's end:** it loops or stops, as the song's property says.
- **The two LEDs of PLAY:**

  | State | Green | Red |
  |---|---|---|
  | at the start (after power-on or HOME + PLAY while paused): nothing to resume | off | off |
  | playing a song | on, brighter on each beat | off |
  | playing a loop (the EDITOR's pattern, the jam) | on, brighter on each beat | dim |
  | paused | off | on |

  Lit together, the two read as amber where they blend, or as green beside a dim red where they do
  not: either way a loop looks unlike a song.
- **REC:** lit = writing on (EDITOR) or recording (JAM); blinking = a take or an overdub is recording.

## 8. Saving and references

**How saving works:**
- **Edits go to RAM.** SAVE writes them all to flash. The only other thing that writes to flash is a
  reference update; REVERT writes nothing.
- **The SAVE LED** is dim while anything is unsaved, dark when nothing is.
- **SAVE during playback** is queued: SAVE blinks dim until playback pauses, and the write happens then, in
  silence. A save still waiting is lost if the FM-1 is switched off first.
- **SAVE + OCT-:** REVERT, after a question: the changes are dropped and everything is reloaded from
  flash. SAVE held alone only shows its layer, as any held button does.
- **The RAM for changes is limited.**
  - **Running low** (10 % left): a toast, `EDIT MEMORY LOW: SAVE SOON`. STATUS's EDITS FREE is yellow
    by then, and red under 10 %.
  - **Full:** an edit that needs more memory is not made. A question comes up instead, and stays
    when the screen changes:

    ```
    MEMORY FOR EDITS IS FULL
    SAVE BEFORE MAKING MORE EDITS
    23 UNSAVED CHANGES

       OCT-            OCT+
       BACK          SAVE NOW
    ```

    - **OCT+** writes everything at once. If music is playing, it pauses first: this save does not
      wait for a pause, since editing cannot go on without it.
    - **OCT-** (or HOME) goes back without saving. Playing, listening and moving around still work;
      the next edit that needs memory brings the question back.
    - **After the save,** the refused edit can be made again. It is not made by itself.
- **Storage full:** a SAVE that does not fit in the flash writes nothing, and asks:

  ```
  NOT ENOUGH STORAGE TO SAVE
  12 KB MORE NEEDED
  CLEAN UP FREES 30 KB

     OCT-            OCT+
     BACK          CLEAN UP
  ```

  - The changes stay in RAM, so nothing is lost while the FM-1 is on.
  - OCT+ runs SETTINGS → TOOLS → CLEAN UP (its question included), then saves if it now fits.
  - Otherwise deleting a pattern, an instrument or a cartridge makes room; the free storage is on
    STATUS.
- **Switching off loses unsaved changes.**

**References.** Things that refer to other things:
- a cell refers to an instrument;
- a song position and a FUSION instrument refer to a pattern;
- a sample instrument refers to a sample.

**Actions that change what a reference points at** ask first: MOVE, SWAP or DELETE of an instrument, a
cartridge, a pattern or a sample. For example, `0B:05 IS USED 12 TIMES. UPDATE REFERENCES?` YES (OCT+) / NO
(OCT-); HOME cancels.
- **YES:**
  1. Playback stops.
  2. Everything pending is saved.
  3. The change and every reference it touches are written to flash in one step, with a progress bar.

  The directory switches last, so a power cut leaves the old state whole.
- **NO:**
  - The change is made, and the references are left as they were. They now point at whatever is
    there.
  - A reference to nothing is drawn in red and plays nothing.
- **HOME:** nothing happens.

**Tools (SETTINGS → TOOLS):**
- **CHECK REFERENCES** lists the missing ones.
- **CLEAN UP** finds unused patterns, instruments and samples, and deletes them after a question.

**UNDO** (in the SEL menus):
- **One level:** it takes back the last edit or SEL action. Used again, it redoes it. A whole take or
  overdub counts as one edit.
- **The cost:** one copy of what changed, at most a pattern's worth of RAM.
- **Its reach:** not past a SAVE or a reference update.

## 11. The playground

- **Its content is the demo disk** (`demo-disk/`, free licences only), all of it, with the disk's
  numbers: its cartridges, the pattern pool, the songs with their timing, mixer and FX setups, and the
  jam. `tools/gen_playground.py` turns the disk's sources into `playground/content.js`; the playground
  knows nothing of disks.
- **It sounds:** the engines are written again in plain JS, after the same models as the firmware's
  (MSFA, KR-106, Open303, the effects), and run in the browser's audio thread. They are close to
  the ear, not the firmware's bits; the firmware's engines stay the reference.
- **It plays the commands** tick by tick, as XM players do, after IBXM's rules: each track's pitch,
  volume and pan drive the voice its note plays, a synth's as a sample's; parameter, mixer and FX
  commands turn the knobs they name. Amiga-period slides play as linear ones.
- **No build:** the page is opened from the file system and edited as it is. Only its content is
  generated (`tools/gen_playground.py`), when the demo data changes.
- **What it fakes:**
  - the CPU figures, estimated from what sounds and each engine's cost on the FM-1;
  - the battery;
  - the flash: SAVE keeps the changes in the browser tab, and a reload starts from the demo data
    again.
