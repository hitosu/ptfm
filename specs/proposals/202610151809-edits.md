# Proposal: edits as pieces, heard at once, REVERT, and the RAM

Status: **settled** (2026-10-15); its specs are listed in [section 8](#8-what-changes-when-settled). It changes
how the edit memory holds changes
([../files/ram.md](../files/ram.md#editing-and-saving), [../ui/saving.md](../ui/saving.md#the-edit-memory)),
replaces UNDO with REVERT for the current object, makes every edit heard while it plays, and lays out the
RAM anew ([section 6](#6-the-ram)). The flash's records ([../files/store.md](../files/store.md)) do not
change.

## 1. Why

- **The edit memory fills with edits made, not with changes held.** Every edit stores a whole new copy of
  the record it touches (a pattern's part, up to 4 KB; a whole cartridge, up to 3.6 KB), and the copies it
  replaces are given back only when nothing plays and no pattern edit is under way. Measured on the PC with
  the demo disk: a note written into a 750 B pattern, then its command changed a click at a time: every
  click takes 760 B more, and the 84th brings `MEMORY FOR EDITS IS FULL` with 776 B really changed, stopped
  or playing.
- **The playing pattern does not hear its edits.** The player keeps reading the pattern as it was when it
  started it: measured, the EDITOR's loop playing, every cell cleared, the old notes go on, past the loop's
  wrap, until the pattern is started again.

## 2. The idea

- **The flash holds whole records, as now. The edit memory holds only what differs from them, as pieces,
  each piece once.** A piece changed again is rewritten where it is; nothing piles up, and nothing waits to
  be tidied.

  | Record | Its pieces |
  |---|---|
  | a pattern, and the loops' pattern | each track's stream; the pattern's head (tracks, rows, EDIT STEP) |
  | a cartridge | each slot; the cartridge's head (its name, its slots' count); a sampler's samples' table |
  | a song, the settings, DJ's setup, the MIDI map, the loops' head | the record whole (they are small) |
  | sample audio | not edited in RAM: what writes it writes the flash, as now |

- **Reading never builds a record.** A pattern is read as each track's stream, from wherever it lies:
  the flash's tracks in place, the changed ones from the edit memory. A cartridge's slot is read the same
  way: the changed slot if there is one, else the flash's. The player reads them as it reads the flash.
- **A piece that becomes what the flash holds goes** (as "back as saved" does now for a record): nothing
  is unsaved by it.
- **SAVE** builds each changed record from its flash bytes and its pieces, one record at a time in the
  store's 4 KB scratch, and writes it as now ([../files/store.md](../files/store.md#saving)); then the edit
  memory is empty. No more RAM than now.
- **The cost of a change:** its piece, once. A cell changed costs its track's stream (about 100 B on the
  demo disk's songs) however many times it is changed again; a DX7 parameter costs its slot (112 B).
  The edit memory's 63 KB then holds about 600 changed tracks.

## 3. Heard at once

- **Every edit is heard from the next row** (a pattern) **or the next note** (an instrument), whatever
  plays it: a song, the EDITOR's loop, LOOPS, either DJ deck.
- **How:** the edit itself, with the sound held off for those few microseconds, rewrites the piece and
  sets the player's place on that track: it walks the new stream from its start to the row the player is on
  and puts the player there. The player does nothing of its own. A piece that outgrows its room moves in the same step, and its old room is free
  at once. So no copy is ever kept for the player's sake.
- **The player's steps cost nothing more:** each row is read from where the player stands on each track, as
  now, everywhere. The walk is the edit's, once an edit, and only for a track the player is reading then (a
  few microseconds). No index of the rows is kept: a cell whose length changes moves every row after it, so an
  index would be rewritten at every edit, and it would take up to 8 KB (256 rows, 16 tracks, 2 B); the player's
  own place is the one entry that matters.
- **Room:** pieces of many sizes come and go, so free room breaks up; it is joined again by moving pieces
  down one at a time (each a short step, the player told as above), never the whole memory at once.
- **In the EDITOR,** writing and playing go together this way
  ([../ui/screens/editor.md](../ui/screens/editor.md#writing-rec), [../ui/transport.md](../ui/transport.md)):

  | What plays | REC on | PLAY, REC on |
  |---|---|---|
  | a song | the song pauses (as now) | as stopped (below) |
  | the EDITOR's loop | the loop plays on; the keys write step by step at the cursor, which moves on by EDIT STEP and is not followed by the view; the loop plays what is written as it reaches it | the loop pauses; REC stays on, writing goes on step by step |
  | nothing | the keys write step by step | **a realtime take that loops:** from the cursor's row (after COUNT-IN, as now), round the whole pattern again and again, until PLAY or REC stops it; existing cells stay unless a note lands on them; the view follows the row being recorded; at the stop the cursor goes back to the row the take started from |

  - **The overdub goes:** REC while the loop plays no longer records in realtime at the playing row; the
    looping take is the realtime way.
  - **ARP's row preview stops whatever plays** before it starts.
  - REC off, the transport is as now: PLAY switches to the EDITOR's loop (a song playing the shown pattern
    handing over on the same row), PLAY again pauses it.

## 4. Smaller still (a second stage)

- **A piece nobody is reading** (not the playing pattern's, not the EDITOR's own) **may be kept as its
  difference from the flash's bytes when that is smaller:** where, how many bytes go, the bytes that come
  (a cell's change: about 6-10 B against a track's 100). The smaller of the two is kept.
- **It is made readable again before it is read:** the EDITOR opening the pattern, or the player about to
  reach it (prepared ahead by the main loop, as a pattern coming next).
- Built only if the first stage's memory proves short in use; its own review then.

## 5. REVERT replaces UNDO

- **REVERT, in the SEL menus where UNDO was, takes the current object back to the flash's,** after a
  question (`REVERT PATTERN 0A TO SAVED?`, `REVERT` / `KEEP`); then `REVERTED`. With nothing unsaved in it,
  it is not listed.

  | Screen | The current object |
  |---|---|
  | EDITOR | the pattern shown |
  | INSTRUMENTS | the slot (a slot's menu); the cartridge (the cartridge list's menu) |
  | SONGS | the song: its positions, names, mixer |
  | FX | the effects of what plays (the song's, LOOPS', DJ's) |
  | LOOPS | all of LOOPS: every loop, the length, the loops' mixer (`REVERT LOOPS TO SAVED?`) |
  | SETTINGS | the settings; the MIDI map on the MIDI page |

- **REVERT ALL** is today's REVERT TO SAVED, renamed: SETTINGS → TOOLS → REVERT ALL, SAVE + OCT- its
  shortcut, its question and its texts otherwise as now.
- **No UNDO,** a step back, in this version.
- **Asking first, now that nothing takes a step back:** DELETE ALL LOOPS, DELETE LOOP, CLEAR LOOP and AUTO
  LEVEL ALL ask before they act. The small actions stay one press (a block's CUT, CLEAR, TRANSFORM, LOOPS'
  LENGTH made shorter, a MIDI mapping deleted): REVERT is their way back.
- **An edit mode** (EDIT keeps, HOME drops, [../ui/controls.md](../ui/controls.md)) stays as it is; its drop
  is no longer something UNDO brings back.
- **RAM:** UNDO kept the older copy of what the last edit changed; that goes.

## 6. The RAM

**Where it stands** (the build of 2026-10-15): of the 512 KB, the data region's 450,560 B hold the
variables (48,508) and the pool (393,728), 8,320 B spare against the 8,192 kept. Every large buffer of the
sound is used in every audio block, so none can share its room with another. The CPU is the limit now,
not the RAM: the room below goes to work that saves CPU.

**About 44 KB the firmware never uses,** left aside by the device layer it started from: 0x01C00BD4-0x01C08000
(room for more code in RAM after the flash driver's 3 KB, and an old debugging area) and 0x01C7C04C-0x01C7FD4C (the
rest of the area kept across restarts, after the crash record and the boot guard, 76 B).

**Measured on the FM-1** (a debug build that patterned that RAM, 0x01C01000-0x01C07F00 and 0x01C7C100-0x01C7FD40,
checked it once a second, placed the audio's and the screen's DMA buffers in it, and scanned all the RAM after a
restart; the startup's 256 B at 0x01C07F00 were left out of the pattern):
- **Untouched:** the pattern stayed whole through a 21-minute AUTOTEST (songs, DJ, USB audio, saving) and 236
  checks after a restart. Nothing else writes there while PT/FM runs.
- **One speed:** a load takes 10.1 cycles there, 10.0 in the data region.
- **DMA reaches it:** sound and screen ran from buffers there the whole run.
- **A restart** (a software reset, as a crash's) changed 23,532 B, all below 0x01C07634: the chip's scratch while
  it boots (0x01C00000 +16, 0x01C00A00 +1,536, 0x01C02000 +22,068, and the startup's own 20 B at 0x01C07F20).
  Nothing from 0x01C08000 up changed. A watchdog's reset was not measured.
- **An update's restart** (a core reset) also unpacks the update loader at 0x01C0A800 (80 KB) and runs it on
  stacks below 0x01C72000; the loader keeps only the update record.
- The chip's boot info starts at 0x01C7FD4C, four bytes inside the area kept across restarts as laid out now (to
  0x01C7FD50).

**The layout, from scratch** (the device test's debug code removed once it had answered):

| Address | Bytes | What |
|---|---:|---|
| 0x01C00000 | 4,096 | code in RAM (the flash driver, 3,028 B used; the link fails beyond) |
| 0x01C01000 | 490,496 | **one data region**: the pool, then the variables; everything set up at power-on |
| 0x01C78C00 | 256 | guard band |
| 0x01C78D00 | 16,128 | the main loop's stack |
| 0x01C7CC00 | 256 | guard band |
| 0x01C7CD00 | 12,032 | the interrupts' stack |
| 0x01C7FC00 | 256 | records kept across restarts (76 B used), where a restart changes nothing |
| 0x01C7FD00 | 76 | unused, write-protected with the chip's own |
| 0x01C7FD4C | 692 | the chip's own: boot info, the boot-state mailbox, the update record, the vectors |

- **The data region grows from 440 to 479 KB, in one block,** from 0x01C01000: the code in RAM keeps its 4 KB, so
  the region holds only RAM measured untouched while PT/FM runs (the 256 B at 0x01C07F00 proved with the rest,
  below). Its bottom 26 KB is the chip's scratch while it restarts, and an update's restart writes the update
  loader over the next 80 KB: neither matters for anything set up at power-on.
- **The pool comes first, the effects' at its very bottom:** the linker takes the effects' object's pool first, the
  DELAY's line (58.8 KB), the REVERB's (44.5 KB) and the punch-in buffer (36.8 KB), about 150 KB that only the CPU
  reads and writes, so the boot's scratch holds those (a stray write there could only be heard). The variables
  come last. Every buffer a
  DMA uses for USB, the screen's line buffer and MIDI IN's ring then lie above 0x01C08000, as they always have
  (the sound's and the screen's DMA were measured to work below it, USB's was never tried there); the build checks
  it, and reads the region's bounds and its parts from the linker's symbols, not from numbers of its own.
- **Before every reset, no DMA writes RAM:** a core reset may leave the peripherals running while the next boot
  writes the loader there, so the sound's DMA and MIDI IN's stop first (USB is detached already) on an update, on
  a crash and on the way to the chip's USB update mode. The rescue starts neither.
- **The startup's notes of the boot's registers** (at 0x01C07F20, read by nothing) go.

**The stacks and their guards:**
- **The interrupts' stack grows to 12,032 B for now:** 6,068 of 7,936 B were seen, only on the paths the
  autotests took; the sound's own frames are large (the effects' block 2.7 KB, the DELAY's 1.4 KB, the REVERB's
  1.4 KB, the player's 1 KB), and the effects over the half change them. **Its size then comes from the worst
  case the build works out from the device's code:** each function's frame and its deepest call (a call through a
  pointer counted at its deepest target), for the audio interrupt, TIMER5's on top of it and the fault handler on
  top of both; the build prints it with the stacks, so a change that deepens it shows. **The code cannot show
  where a call through a pointer goes,** so the build keeps a table of every hook the interrupts call and its
  possible targets (MIDI's note hook, USB audio's tap, the output's, the sampler's FWS reads, the store's audio
  context, ...), and refuses a call through a pointer it does not list, and any recursion: else the worst case would
  only be a lower bound, as the deepest seen is.
- **Each stack gets a window of its own for the stack pointer** (the interrupts' stack pointer within the
  interrupts' stack, the main loop's within the main loop's). Today one window covers both, so the interrupts'
  stack running into the main loop's is not reliably caught: the registers a function saves at its frame's top
  land in the 256 B guard band, but the locals of a larger frame can lie past it, untouched by the check. If the
  chip refuses two windows, the interrupts' stack goes below the main loop's instead, so the shared window's
  bottom catches the tighter one. The guard bands stay, a second net.
- **The write protection of the chip's own area starts at 0x01C7FD00,** so it covers the boot info's first four
  bytes too (today it starts at 0x01C7FD50).
- **Each stack's bottom and top are named in the linker script,** and everything that needs a stack's ends (the
  stack-pointer windows, the guard bands' write limits, the figures' stack depths) uses those names, never a guard
  band's (the figures take the main loop's top as the guard band above it today): either order of the stacks then
  works unchanged.

**Proved on the FM-1 before it is used,** by actions of the debug build:
- **A watchdog's restart keeps the records:** the main loop stops feeding the watchdog; twice in a row must end
  in USB RESCUE (the boot guard counted both restarts), the crash record readable after the first.
- **Each stack's overflow is caught and reported:** a deliberate overflow of each ends in the crash screen naming
  the stack check, and the next start finds a crash record that says so; no false alarm in a full AUTOTEST. The
  fault handler saves its registers and draws the crash screen on the stack that overflowed: should the screen or
  the record not come, it switches first to a crash stack of its own, 1 KB below the records, from the free room.
- **The 256 B at 0x01C07F00 stay untouched:** patterned at power-on in the same build and checked through a full
  AUTOTEST and a restart, as the rest was.

**What the room goes to:**

| What | Bytes | For |
|---|---:|---|
| the interrupts' stack, 7,936 -> 12,032 B (beside the data region, above) | 4,096 | room over the sound's large frames |
| the effects run over a whole half, not block by block (its mix and sends for four blocks) | 4,480 | CPU: the effects' code and state fetched once a half |
| a song for a DJ deck made ready by the main loop before its LOAD's bar: a third song arena, in what DJ borrows from the FWS cache | (6,400) | the LOAD's 1.1-1.7 ms in one half, which made DJ's late halves |
| a DJ deck's own mix and sends over the half, in DJ's borrow too | (3,840) | CPU on DJ |
| the screens' variables: their cap from 24 to 28 KB | 4,096 | the screens' next additions |
| the punch-in buffer as middle and sides: the last 0.5 s, its middle at 29.4 kHz (29,400 B) and its sides at 7.35 kHz (7,350 B), 36,750 B (was 88,200 in stereo at 44.1 kHz) | -51,450 | room back from an effect seldom used |
| **the FWS cache, a zone of its own** (64,512 B), so the edit memory's 63 KB are all the edits' | 64,512 | the edits and the cache each sure of their room; DJ never waits on the edits |
| the edit memory's index of a fixed size goes: the list of pieces lives in the edit memory | -1,280 | |
| kept spare, as now | 8,192 | |

- **The punch-in buffer keeps the mix as its middle and its sides:** the middle, (L + R) / 2, at 29.4 kHz (2/3 of
  the output's rate, as the DELAY and the REVERB run), so a replayed slice keeps everything up to ~13 kHz; the
  sides, (L − R) / 2, at 7.35 kHz (1/6 of it), so the slice keeps the song's stereo up to ~3 kHz: the pans of
  drums, bass, chords and pads stay where they were, and only the width of the top (hats, cymbals, the REVERB's
  air) comes to the middle. Played back, left = middle + sides and right = middle − sides, so a mono output sums
  to the middle exactly. The length stays 0.5 s, a beat at 120 BPM.
- **Its cost:** while anything plays, the middle goes through a 3:2 converter (as the DELAY's and the REVERB's
  inputs) and the sides through a 6:1 one, far lighter; while a punch-in plays, both are read back through their
  converters. The CPU the recording adds is measured before it is kept. The punch-ins that do not replay the
  buffer (bit crush, the gates, the sweeps, echo throw, reverb swell) work on the live mix as before.
- **Heard in the playground before it is kept,** against the buffer as it is now.

- **The edit memory and the FWS cache are two zones,** 63 KB each, no longer one shared one (where the edits had
  32 KB for sure and the cache what they left, giving way):
  - **The edit memory is all the edits':** the pieces grow up from its bottom and their list down from its top
    (8 B a piece, as many as the pieces need), and nothing borrows from it, so `MEMORY FOR EDITS IS FULL` comes
    only from real changes, about 600 changed tracks.
  - **The FWS cache** holds the copies of what plays from FWS's free part (its patterns, then its cartridges, then
    its samples; what does not fit is read as it plays, at a CPU cost: 16 sample voices streamed from there cost
    about 15 % more, measured on the FM-1). It is idle while nothing that plays is in FWS's free part.
  - Each zone is under 64 KB, so their offsets stay 16-bit, as the state's figures of the edit memory do
    ([../connections.md](../connections.md#the-state)).
- **What only DJ uses is borrowed from the FWS cache while DJ plays,** as its second deck's song arena and tracks
  already are, now from the cache alone: DJ's borrow grows from 13,044 to 23,284 B, leaving the cache 40 KB then.
  DJ never waits on the edits (`NO ROOM FOR DJ: SAVE YOUR EDITS` goes); a record of FWS pinned in the cache where
  DJ borrows still makes DJ refuse to start (the demo disk keeps nothing in FWS): the FWS review's (closed by
  [202610201630-fws-buffer.md](202610201630-fws-buffer.md), which took the cache away).
- **The three song arenas swap between the decks** at each LOAD (the one made ready becomes the deck's, the deck's
  old one the next to make ready). Leaving DJ, what plays next starts on the pool's own arena, and the borrow goes
  back only once no deck uses its arenas, as now.

That leaves about 19.2 KB of the data region free beyond the spare (490,496 - (442,236 - 51,450 - 1,280) - 8,576 -
64,512 - 8,192 = 19,710 B), for what is measured next (each voice's blocks rendered in a row, for one; the crash
stack, 1 KB, if the overflow test asks for it). Should the FWS review show the cache needs more, it grows from
there, or past 64 KB with its offsets counted in 4-byte units.

**Kept as they are:** the edit memory's 63 KB (now all the edits'), the DELAY (1 s at 29.4 kHz), the REVERB, the
main loop's stack (16,128 B, 6,808 seen at most: measured again on the paths no autotest takes, a power-on seeding,
CHECK STORAGE, a compacting SAVE, before it is cut).

**In reserve, not done:** the screen's strip as 8-bit colours (it draws from 32 colours only: 28,800 B
back, the screens unchanged), USB audio's buffers borrowed from the FWS cache while a computer takes
them (6.5 KB), the DX7's frequency table read from the flash (4,100 B), the scopes in 8 bits (1,024 B).

## 7. The list of pieces and the second stage

- **The list of pieces** lives in the edit memory itself, growing down from its top as the pieces grow up from its
  bottom, 8 B a piece: it takes only what the pieces in it need, and no table of a fixed size (today's 160
  entries, 1.3 KB, could not hold a pattern of 16 tracks' 17 pieces many times over). Nothing else uses the edit
  memory's top: the FWS cache and DJ's borrow are a zone of their own ([section 6](#6-the-ram)).
- **The second stage** ([section 4](#4-smaller-still-a-second-stage)) is built only if the first's memory proves
  short in use.
