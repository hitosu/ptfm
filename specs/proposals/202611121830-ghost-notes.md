# Proposal: ghost notes

Status: **settled** (2026-11-12). It replaces parking: a note that loses its voice's sound engine, or finds none,
goes on as a **ghost**: its voice without an engine, every rule of the player still acting on it, and comes back
worked forward to where it would be when an engine is free. Muted notes are ghosts at once. The specs it changes, once
settled: [../ui/screens/settings.md](../ui/screens/settings.md#voices),
[../second-core.md](../second-core.md#the-voices-on-core-2), [../ui/screens/loops.md](../ui/screens/loops.md) (Voices,
RECORD LIVE), [../ui/screens/fx.md](../ui/screens/fx.md) (DUCK, FILTER's envelope),
[../ui/screens/dj.md](../ui/screens/dj.md#voices), [../ui/screens/status.md](../ui/screens/status.md) (STEALS),
[../ui/screens/visual.md](../ui/screens/visual.md), [../ui/README.md](../ui/README.md), [../chip.md](../chip.md),
[../files/ram.md](../files/ram.md), [../files/patterns.md](../files/patterns.md), [../hardware.md](../hardware.md)
(the freeze screen's places), [../debug.md](../debug.md), [../open-questions.md](../open-questions.md) (parked notes).

## 1. Why

Every note takes a voice of one pool (VOICES 8, 12 or 16). A held note taken for a new note is parked today: the
whole voice (its control part, 72 B, its engine's state, 444 B, and its 128-B scope) moves to a place past the count,
and its engine's skip step runs every block, so its envelopes, LFOs and oscillators go on unheard. The pool holds 16
notes at most (20 at VOICES 16), parked ones included. That falls short where it matters most:

- **LOOPS sounds far more notes than that** (16 loops of 4 lanes, FUSION layers, the keys on top). With the places
  full the note parked longest is dropped, however long it is still held: a pad pushed out in a busy bar never comes
  back.
- **A muted loop keeps its voices:** mute is a mix gain, so a muted loop's notes start in free voices, and a muted
  loop's note can come back from parking into a freed voice ahead of an audible one waiting longer.
- **More places cost RAM** (644 B each) and CPU (a skip step a place, every block).

What a note needs to be followed, held, let go and cut is its voice's control part; only its sound needs the engine.
So the pool splits the two: **24 ghosts** wait where parking kept 4 (8 at VOICES 8), each about 92 B and nothing a
block, and when an engine is free a ghost's engine starts its note again and works its envelopes and LFOs forward over
the time it was gone, mostly in closed form (section 5); the note fades in where it would be by then.

## 2. A ghost is a voice whose engine was taken

**The pool:** 16 engines (each an engine's state and its scope row: the places VOICES counts, 10 on core 1 and 6 on
core 2 at VOICES 16, as now) and 40 voices (each a voice's control part: whose note it is, its instrument and note, its
level, gain, pan and bend as last set, a Juno-106's patch copy, its times). A sounding note is a voice with an engine;
a **ghost** is a voice without one. 40 voices: the 16 sounding and 24 ghosts.

**The rule holds by construction:** the player finds, holds, follows, lets go, legatos, bends, levels and cuts voices,
whether they have an engine or not, as it does every voice now: by its track and note count, its lane and the stage's
column mark, its key, a FUSION runner's layer; through a note off while the note is its track's latest; LEG, 3xx, a
lane's legato and glide; NOTE CUT, all sound off, CUT, a FUSION layer's ECx, DJ's cuts at the end of a BRAKE, BACKSPIN,
ECHO OUT or REVERB OUT, K1's restart, the second replay's tracks moving; LOOPS' MOVE, a new loop's track; FILTER's
envelope counting a held note. What the engine held and the player still sets on a note is kept in its voice while it
has none: its pitch (the bend the track, the lane's glide and legato, a FUSION layer's arpeggio give it), its level and
gain (a FUSION layer's slid volume among them), a Juno-106's key (as `juno_key` sets it at a legato), a sample's tune; a
parameter command turned on a ghost goes into its Juno-106 patch copy, as on a sounding Juno-106 voice, and for the
other engines is taken again at its return (below). Its engine gets them all when it comes back.

**Only what needs a sound differs:**

- **it is not rendered:** no peak, no scope, nothing into DUCK or the meters (as a parked note now);
- **its times:** each voice keeps its start and its let-go on a block clock (a 32-bit count of the blocks made); a
  ghost's sound is worked out from them when it comes back (section 5);
- **its end by itself** (a one-shot sample past its END, a CHIP note whose VOL table or release ends) is worked out
  when it becomes a ghost and again whenever something moves it (its let-go; a parameter command: END, LOOP, TUNE, the
  CHIP's RELEASE, VOL table, FPS; its pitch: a bend, a slide, DJ's KEY or BRAKE, a sample's end coming sooner or later
  at another pitch), and it ends then, as its voice would have (FILTER's envelope released, its voice free): a held
  ghost keeps its end's block in the 4 B its let-go takes once let go, and the list keeps the soonest of them, one
  compare a block; a return that finds its note past its end drops it. **A sampler ghost keeps its place** in its voice (6 B: an exact place needs at least 38 bits; it takes the
  sample offset's room), moved on at
  each pitch change by the time at the pitch it had: its end and its place at its return come from that same count,
  so they never disagree;
- **RECORD LIVE's tap** counts only notes with an engine, as now (section 8).

**Its parameter commands at its return:** keys' notes and LOOPS' lanes have none; a FUSION layer comes back with its
cell's, as it started, and its track's on top as they turn a sounding layer's voice (its cell read again: the ghost is
dropped if that cell no longer holds the same note and instrument); a Juno-106 with its patch copy, which holds them;
a song's DX7, sampler or CHIP note with its track's as they are at its return while the track is still on the ghost's
instrument (none once it took another): a parameter its engine takes only at a note's start (most of the DX7's, a
sample's START) that the track changed while the note was a ghost comes back changed. A ghost let go is dropped when
its track takes another instrument.

**The list:** the ghosts are kept in the order they were made (a ghost ending closes the gap), so "made last" and "a
ghost longest" need no stamp. Ghosts made at once (a mute, a smaller count, a fallback) are made in their engines'
order, the lowest first, in the firmware and the playground alike.

## 3. Becoming a ghost

- **A held note taken** for a new note (which note is taken does not change, but for DUCK's source and a note fading back in, section 5: release tails
  first, then the songs' held notes, the quietest first, a muted one as silent, the keys' last; on LOOPS the keys play
  through the stage's lanes, so their notes rank as the loops') fades out in its engine at once (section 7), and its
  voice, without the engine, is a ghost. A note started in the same block was never heard: it gives its engine up with
  no fade. A release tail taken fades out the same way and goes, as today. A take needs a voice for the new note: the
  one a full list's drop frees (section 6), or the taken note's own when it is not kept.
- **A note that may take no engine starts as a ghost:** a song's note while the keys hold every engine, or while a
  key's held ghost waits for one, a return of a key's under way among them (as today's "starts parked"); a muted track's note (below). A note of a DJ deck
  faded out that finds no engine of its own deck is not played, as today.
- **A smaller VOICES, core 2 stopping** (16 counting as 12): the held notes in the engines above the count fade out and
  become ghosts; their release tails ring out where they are, as today.
- **A fallback during a voice job** (core 2 stuck, a misread, a fault), the count falling to 12: a job core 2 never
  started leaves its engines' state as it was: those in places 11 and 12 sound on, those in 13-16 become ghosts. A
  job core 2 started may have left its engines' state half done: every note of it becomes a ghost (its voice holds all
  it needs), made last, so that within their kind they are the first back; a TB-303's is cut. Today those voices are
  cut.
- **Muted:** the moment a note's track is muted (its own mute, another track's solo, or the note moving into a muted
  or solo-silenced track: a lane sent by REC into a muted loop; a song's track as a loop's), its held notes fade out
  with the mute's ramp and become ghosts, and their engines are free from the next block; its release tails become
  ghosts let go. A muted track's new note starts as a ghost: a muted note never takes an engine, and costs no CPU.
  Unmuted, its ghosts come back as any (4 a block at most, each fading in over 5.8 ms where the mute's ramp is 1.45 ms;
  under about 10 ms with engines free), and past the 24 a solo's ghosts are lost when it ends. On VISUAL a muted
  track's notes are then not shown (no tiles on VOICES, nothing on its LEVELS meter), where today they are measured
  before their mute; DUCK's source's muted notes, keeping their engines, are shown as today.
  Not so:
  - **DUCK's source:** its muted notes keep their engines, as now, so DUCK follows the source muted or not; they rank
    as unmuted when an engine is taken, and its ghosts (taken for other notes) rank as unmuted for returns and drops.
    Any change of the source in force (SOURCE by the knob or an FX command, DUCK on or off, BYPASS, a song's setup at
    its start, a DJ deck's own DUCK and its LOAD) acts as a mute on the old source's muted notes (they become ghosts)
    and as an unmute on the new source's muted ghosts;
  - **a TB-303's note** (below);
  - **the stage's column** (the preview, the stage, the keys on it, the pattern recorder's single notes): never
    muted, never ghosted by a mute.
- **A TB-303's note never becomes a ghost:** it slides from note to note in its own engine. Taken, it fades out and
  goes; one that would start as a ghost is not played; above a smaller count (core 2 stopping, a job core 2 never
  started) it is let go and rings on where it is; muted, it keeps its engine: all as today.

## 4. Ending

- **What ends a note ends its ghost** (section 2); a ghost let go goes on as its note's release would.
- **A ghost let go is dropped** when its level is under hearing at its return (section 5), 10 s after it was let go,
  or when its track takes another instrument. A Juno-106's whose RELEASE puts it under hearing by now (a table of the
  ticks each RELEASE takes from its envelope's top to where its VCA's gain is 0, which is under hearing at the highest
  gain a note can have, so at any level; VCA GATE: a tick) is dropped without any work; past 10 s (RELEASE 127 takes
  some 26 s) the 10 s drop comes first.
- **A ghost whose instrument is gone** by its return (a cartridge deleted) is dropped.

## 5. Coming back

**The order**, whenever an engine of the count is free at a block's start (after the block's new notes have taken
theirs):

1. the keys' held ghosts;
2. the songs' and loops' held ghosts;
3. the keys' ghosts let go (every arpeggiator note's tail among them);
4. the songs' and loops' ghosts let go.

Within each, the one made a ghost last comes first. **A muted track's ghost never comes back while muted;** unmuted,
it comes back in its turn. **At most 4 returns begin a block.** A return takes a free engine first; with none, **a
held ghost takes a release tail's engine** (a note let go and ringing out, the least missed: the quietest first, as a
new note takes one), the tail fading out there over a block as at any take (counted among the block's fades,
section 7), never a held note's. A ghost let go takes only a free engine. So in a full pool held ghosts come back as
soon as tails ring (in LOOPS, at every bar line).

**The forward work.** The engine's state starts from zero (a fresh voice: filters at rest, the LFOs from their start),
the engine starts the note (its instrument and parameters as section 2 says, at the note its voice started at, then
its voice's pitch, level, key and tune as they stand), then works it forward over its time since its start:

- **the DX7 and the sampler** (MSFA's envelopes: each operator's, the DX7's pitch envelope, the sampler's): a stage
  moves by a fixed step a block towards its target, a rising one by a step fixed within each of 17 bands of the
  level: each stretch is one sum, a stage after another, a hold (a stage that stands) its count. Exact, a few steps a
  note;
- **the Juno-106:** its firmware loop's envelope worked over its ticks (about 236 a second): its attack in one sum;
  its DECAY and RELEASE (a factor a tick, up to about 6,000 ticks for the slowest, DECAY or RELEASE 127, some 26 s) in
  runs where each tick takes off the same amount (while the distance's high byte and its rounding band stay the same:
  a run one sum, at most some 135 for the slowest), the fast ones stepped a few ticks a step; nothing once at its
  sustain. Exact. Then its cutoff's and its VCA's smoothing set where they head (as a skip step leaves them);
- **the CHIP:** its tables (a table's place from its speed and its loop), its step envelope and its PSG envelope (a
  place from its period and shape) in one sum each, its sweep stepped until it reaches its limit, its release counter
  (a level every RELEASE frames after its let-go; at 0 the note has ended) in one sum;
- **the LFOs:** from their start, by their speed, in one sum, with their delays: the DX7's (its delay's two rates,
  each in one sum), the Juno-106's (its delay and ramp), the CHIP's vibrato's; the DX7's sample-and-hold takes one
  random step a cycle;
- **the clocks:** the Juno-106's ticks and the CHIP's frames in a time are counted as their engines count them (the
  same accumulators), with no 64-bit division (a long count by a hand-made one);
- **not kept:** the oscillators' phases (a note fading in from any phase sounds the same); the frequencies' history (a
  bend, a slide, DJ's BRAKE, the LFO's and the pitch envelope's pull on the DX7's operators: the note comes back at
  its pitch now); a sample's place is the one its voice kept (section 2), exact to the tick its pitch changed at; the
  LFOs' phases and the sample-and-hold's random values differ from the note never
  taken's where that note's engine carried them over from an earlier note (the Juno-106's always, the DX7's without
  its SYNC, its random state even with it);
- **a ghost let go:** its note off at its let-go time on the way, then on to now.

**Under hearing.** A ghost let go, in its release, is dropped at its return when its level after the forward work is
under hearing: its engine's level (the DX7's carriers' envelopes, the Juno-106's VCA target, the sampler's envelope
gain, the CHIP's volume) times the note's gain, on the 16-bit output scale, under the level a released note's peak is
freed at now (`PL_QUIET`, 33: -60 dB), the same in the playground. A held ghost is never dropped for being quiet: it
may rise again (a slow attack, a later stage louder). A return that drops its ghost does not count among the block's 4.

**The budget**, a safety net counted in steps (each engine's step at its cost measured on the FM-1, a note's start
included: the cold code, the patch, the engine's note start; never in measured time, so the PC, the browser and the
FM-1 decide alike). A return's work goes in pieces, the budget spent on the returns under way in the order above
(the keys' held first); one not finished goes on in the next block, its engine held for it. Most returns take a few
steps and sound from the block their engine frees; a Juno-106 note deep in a slow DECAY or RELEASE may take some
blocks.

**A return under way** is still a ghost until its fade in begins: a new note may take its engine first (it is silent:
the least missed), and the ghost stays a ghost; whatever acts on a voice acts on it.

**Then it fades in** over 5.8 ms (4 blocks), as a parked note does now. The sound is close to the note never taken:
its levels the same, its phases, filters and a bent sample's place not.

**While it fades in** a note back counts as just started for the take order: its loudness counts as unknown (the last
of its kind to be taken, as a note started in the same block is now) until its fade in ends, so its faded peak never
makes it the quietest (today a parked note coming back can be taken again at once for that). It is the same voice it
was (its note count, which the take order's ties and the FUSION runners read, its start, its place among the keys'
notes for "the oldest first"), so taken again it is worked forward from its real start. A song's note never takes a
key's returning note, as it never takes a key's held one. The lanes' scans for their current note (a glide, a legato,
MOVE) pass ghosts let go by (sounding tails they take as today), so a ghost let go whose note count comes round again
(a lane's wraps every 32 notes) is never taken for the current one; a track's following (its volume slides, its
glide) goes on through a note off, as section 2 says.

## 6. The list full

The 24 ghosts in use, the one that goes for a new one:

1. ghosts let go, the one let go longest ago first;
2. muted ghosts, the one a ghost longest first;
3. the songs' and loops' held ghosts, the one a ghost longest first;
4. the keys' held ghosts, the one a ghost longest first.

A note that would become a ghost and ranks below every ghost in the list is not kept: taken, it fades out and goes;
starting, it is not played. In LOOPS a bar line lets many notes go at once, so ghosts let go are dropped often: by
design, as they are the least missed.

## 7. The pool

- **16 engines, none past VOICES 16:** VOICES 8, 12 or 16 of them take notes (16 is 10 on core 1 and 6 on core 2).
  After VOICES falls, the release tails above the new count ring out where they are.
- **A take fades out in its own engine, in the same block:** when a note (held or a tail) is taken, wherever the take
  happens (a tick, the keys' note-ons at a half's start, a chase or a start), the engine keeps a **fade note** of what
  its mixing needs (the taken note's track or the keys, its column mark, pan, gain, its Juno-106 chorus bus: about
  8 B; 8 of them, the most fades a block) and its own engine state as it is; the voice goes to the new note (or stays
  a ghost) at once. A Juno-106 engine
  plays from its own patch copy, kept in its engine's room (a Juno-106's state is under half an engine's), so nothing
  of the voice it served is read again. Then, in the block's voices pass, before core 2 is handed its voices (where
  the block's slot, mix, sends, Juno-106 buses, tick and noise and its peaks are ready), core 1 renders each such
  engine's fade: the taken note's last block, its gain going down to 0 over the block, mixed by its fade note (its
  peak into its track's and the meters, the block not silent); then starts the new note in it from the new note's voice
  (as a return starts a note, with no time to work forward: the level, pitch and parameters the take's tick gave it).
  The new note's first block is rendered with the others: on core 2 in core 2's places, as today. The new note sounds
  from that block, on time. It is the work today's places past the count do for a fade, done in the engine itself. **As many such fades a block as today's places past the count** (4 at VOICES 16 and 12, 8 at 8); past
  them a taken note is cut at once (as today when every place is fading), and either way a held one becomes a ghost.
  The Juno-106's noise is made for the block when a Juno-106 engine sounds or one starts in it (a take's deferred
  start, a return), as a parked Juno-106 counts today.
- **A new note takes a free engine first,** the first core's first, as now. A note just come back into an engine is a
  sounding note: it may be taken, as the last of its kind while it fades in (section 5). An engine a return under way
  holds is taken before any sounding note.
- **What the pool holds at most:** 40 notes, the sounding ones and the ghosts. A note keeps its engine while it sounds
  and moves between the cores only as a ghost.

## 8. What else changes

- **STATUS' STEALS** counts as now: a note giving its engine up to a new note.
- **RECORD LIVE's tap:** a note made a ghost by a take, a smaller count or a fallback gets its note off where it lost
  its engine, and a ghost coming back is not written again, as for parking. A mute is written as now (it ends that
  loop's notes in what is recorded); its ghosts coming back after it are not written.
- **DJ:** the deck lower in the mix gives way first, as now; its ghosts come back in the same order as any.
- **The code:** the voice's struct splits into its control part (the voices, 40) and its engine (the engines, 16, each
  with its scope row); a voice points at its engine or at none. What scans notes scans the voices in use (the
  control's paths: a take, a tick's following, a cut, FILTER's held notes), guarded by counts where a block's path
  would walk them (the held ghosts a track); what renders and mixes walks the engines.
- **The playground** carries the same pool (16 engines, 40 voices, the same places), the same rules and the same
  forward work, ported to its JS engines (the DX7, the Juno-106 and the sampler get their own, as the CHIP has a skip
  now). Today it parks its quietest held notes at a smaller count where the firmware parks those in the places above
  it, and it stops following a note at its note off where the firmware follows it while it is its track's latest:
  both are fixed with this. On its own blocks of 128 frames at the page's rate, its events on their own sample in
  them: 8 returns begin in one of its blocks (4 for each 64 frames, the FM-1's rate), its budget the same for each 64
  frames, and as many fades as the FM-1's for each 64 frames; a take's fade lasts 64 frames from the take's sample,
  carried past its block's end, and "never heard" means not yet rendered. Its returns land in its blocks, not block
  for block with the FM-1's (as its notes' starts already do).

## 9. RAM

- **Today:** 20 places of 516 B (a 72-B control part and a 444-B engine state) and a 128-B scope row each: 12,880 B.
- **With ghosts:** 16 engines of 448 B (the state's 444 B after its kind and its voice's link, 4 B with the union's
  alignment: a note's start zeroes the state or carries it over by the engine last there, as now: bit-exact) and
  their scope rows, 9,216 B; 40 voices of about 92 B (today's control part, the let-go stamp, the start in the parked
  stamp's 4 B, the engine's link, the pitch, key and tune kept for a ghost, a sampler ghost's place (6 B, in the
  sample offset's room and 2 B more), a FUSION layer's place), 3,680 B (some 80 B more with the place's 2); the ghosts' ordered list, about 24 B; the held ghosts' counts, about 36 B; the
  fade notes, 8 of about 8 B (the most fades a block), 64 B: about 13,020 B, some 140 B more than today's pool. The
  engine's 2 spare bytes can hold what only a sounding note has (its block's peak), so a voice keeps a 2-B hole for a
  ghost's own fields.
- **The room:** the release had about 290 B over its 8 KB spare (the build with the freeze screen's diagnostics: 288
  B); the pool split into voices and engines (20 of each, as today's places) took 80 B of it (208 B left in the DEV
  build), so ghosts leave about 150 B.

## 10. Cost and the debug build

- **Nothing a block while ghosts wait** (parking ran every parked voice's skip step each block).
- **Returns:** a note's start and a few steps each (measured on the PC: a DX7 skip step ~3 % of its block's render, a
  Juno-106's under 1 %, the CHIP's ~9 % of its much cheaper render; the forward work's steps are lighter than skip
  steps), a Juno-106 in a slow DECAY or RELEASE more; 4 a block at most, within the budget; nothing otherwise.
- **More notes heard:** ghosts lift the pool's 16-note cap, so engines freed are filled again more often. Measured on
  the FM-1 before: eight places past 12 instead of four kept more notes sounding in DJ HEAVY (11.3 on average against
  10.5) and ran late more often (117 halves in 30 s against 68). VOICES is the cap; section 12 measures it.
- **The control:** scans over 40 voices where there were 20 places: in the control's paths (a take, a tick, a cut)
  over the voices in use, with counts as guards on a block's paths; the returns' code on the rare path (out of the
  usual way's cache lines).
- **The debug build's FIGURES** ([../debug.md](../debug.md#the-figures)), each over the window: a stage of its own,
  GHOSTS (the returns' forward work, inside VOICES, where parking's work is now: its sum and its longest in one half);
  the ghosts now and the most; ghosts made (taken, started as ghosts, muted, past a smaller count, core 2's), returns,
  ghosts ended (let go and over, cut, ended by themselves, an instrument gone), ghosts dropped (the list full: which
  kind; under hearing at a return); the forward work's steps and time, each engine apart (DX7, Juno-106, sampler,
  CHIP) and the notes' starts (the step costs the budget counts by); the budget used, its most in one block; the
  longest wait of a return (blocks from its engine being free to its fade in) and the oldest note brought back.

- **The player's tests:** the return order, 4 a block, the budget's carry-over in the return order, a return under

- **The stress test:** LOOPS over the count with keys held, mutes, solos and VOICES changes at random, under the
  address and undefined-behaviour sanitizers.

## 12. On the FM-1: the AUTOTEST's GHOST NOTES

A scenario of its own in the AUTOTEST's LOOPS group ([../debug.md](../debug.md#the-scenarios)), ticked at first,
built as the loop station is (every step checked through LOOPS' cursor and LOOPS' bytes in the state), the notes
far past the count so ghosts are made and come back all the time. About 2 minutes measured.

1. **Setting up:** VOICES 8 (an edit, checked); the loops cleared (SEL's CLEAR LOOPS), the length 1 BAR (GLO's
   LENGTH), QUANTIZE `ROW`; the keys POLY (SEQ held + POLY); five loops of 4 lanes played from the keys onto the stage
