# PT/FM 1.0

The work in this version, task by task: why it was done and how, with the commits that hold its code. The changes
in short, a line each: [CHANGELOG.md](../../CHANGELOG.md).

## Ghost notes

**Why.** Every note takes a voice of one pool. A held note taken for a new one was parked: its whole voice (its
control, 72 B, its engine's state, 444 B, its scope row, 128 B) moved to a place past the count, and its engine's skip
step ran every block to keep its envelopes and LFOs going unheard. The pool held 16 notes at most (20 at VOICES 16),
parked ones included, and that fell short where it mattered most. LOOPS sounds far more notes (16 loops of 4 lanes,
FUSION layers, the keys on top): with the places full the note parked longest was dropped however long it was still
held, so a pad pushed out in a busy bar never came back. A mute was a mix gain, so a muted loop kept its voices, and
its notes could come back from parking ahead of an audible one waiting longer. More places cost RAM (644 B each) and a
skip step each a block. What a note needs to be followed, held, let go and cut is its control; only its sound needs
the engine. The design is [the proposal](../proposals/202611121830-ghost-notes.md); the specs
[settings.md's VOICES](../ui/screens/settings.md#voices) and those its head names.

**How.**

- **The pool split** into 16 engines (448 B each: a kind of engine's state with its kind, its voice's link, a taken
  note's fade and a return waiting there; and a scope row each, 128 B) and 40 voices (a note's control, 92 B). A
  sounding note is a voice with an engine; a ghost is a voice without one, in a list in the order made (24 B), so
  "made last" and "a ghost longest" need no stamp. What finds notes walks the voices in use, what renders walks the
  engines. Every rule of the player acts on a voice, so a ghost is held, followed, let go, bent, levelled and cut as a
  sounding note; what its engine would hold (its pitch, level, a Juno-106's key and patch copy, a sample's TUNE, its
  parameter commands) its voice keeps. The pool takes 12,984 B (the engines, their scopes, the voices, the list and
  the fade notes) where parking's 20 places took 12,880; the release keeps 560 B of RAM over its spare.
- **A take fades out in its own engine,** in the block of the take: a fade note of 8 B keeps what its mixing needs (8
  of them, the most fades a block; a Juno-106 plays from a patch copy in its engine's room), core 1 renders the fade in
  the voices pass before core 2 is handed its voices, cuts the engine and starts the new note there. No place past the
  count is needed for it. A note never heard, silent or muted gives its engine up with no fade.
- **Each kind of engine's forward work,** its contract in `fw/core/eng/fwd.h` (the units still to go held, then let
  go; a budget of steps; in pieces, resumed from the engine's own state), mostly in closed form: the DX7's and the
  sampler's envelopes by MSFA's stages, a stretch of a stage in one sum within each of the level's 17 bands; the DX7's
  pitch envelope and its LFO with the delay's two rates; the Juno-106's envelope over its firmware loop's ticks in runs
  where each tick takes off the same amount (its slowest DECAY or RELEASE in some 135), its LFO's delay and ramp, its
  cutoff's and VCA's smoothing set where they head; the CHIP's tables, envelopes, sweep and release by sums, with long
  divisions done by hand (the FM-1 has no 64-bit division). Each kind also gives a bound of how loud its note can
  still be (for the drop under hearing) and, for the sampler and the CHIP, the end of a note by itself. A sampler
  ghost keeps its place in its sample (6 B: an exact place needs at least 38 bits), walked on once a block however
  often its pitch moves, so its place and its end come from one count. A CHIP ghost's end comes from two figures
  worked out when it becomes a ghost and again as its return's note starts, so its commands meanwhile act from its
  return and its release is counted from its note's start frame (at most a frame later than the note never taken).
- **The returns** come in the proposal's order, 4 a block, a held ghost into a release tail's engine when none is
  free (the tail fading out over that block, the return beginning in the next, its checks made again then), under a
  budget of 44 units a block (3 % of a block; a unit about 1 µs, each weight its work's mean measured on the FM-1 by
  GHOST NOTES: a DX7's step 2, its note's start 63, the sampler's decoder seek 95, a sampler's checks 109), so a block
  mostly begins one return, its forward work going on in the blocks after (a DX7's usual return sounds from its third
  block); a return not finished goes on in the next block, its engine held, its work kept in its voice (no RAM). A
  ghost let go is dropped at its return under -60 dB (each kind's bound times the most gain its render takes, the
  track's LEVEL and DUCK left out so a note that would be heard is never dropped), a Juno-106 past its RELEASE with no
  work (a table of each RELEASE's ticks), a song's when its track has taken another instrument; a song's DX7, sampler
  or CHIP ghost comes back with its track's commands as they are then, a FUSION layer with its own while its cell still
  holds it.
- **Mutes** are caught in the voices pass, at the end of the block whose mute ramp ends at silence, rather than at each
  place a mask changes: a mute, a solo, a note moved into a muted loop and a change of DUCK's source all reach it
  there. DUCK's source, a TB-303 and the stage's column keep their engines; a take ranks a note muted now as silent.
- **Core 2's fallbacks:** a voice job never begun is a smaller count (places 11-12 sound on, the held notes in 13-16
  ghosts); a job begun makes every note of it a ghost, made last, where they were cut before.
- **Choices set aside:** keeping each Juno-106 ghost's peak against its loudness bound (its filter's LFO and envelope
  move its output against its VCA, so one block's ratio bounds nothing later); carrying a call's overshoot into the next
  block (it lowers no worst block and needs RAM); counting a return when it begins (it counts when it comes back, so a
  dropped one never does); applying a parameter command to a return under way (the DX7's live change between forward
  calls is untested: the command starts the return again); giving up, of several returns under way, the latest in the
  order (the highest-numbered engine's goes: the order would cost code on the take's path); a drop the moment a track
  changes instrument (looked at the return: a track gone and back keeps its ghost).
- **Room in the slot:** the forward work and the ghosts' code take about 13 KB of the firmware slot. A size pass made
  first, bit-exact, gave back 7.4 KB: the DX7's detune table as one exact step a note (a 7.5 KB table for a 0.5 KB
  one, checked at every build), event work out of core 2's room back under its 256-B step, and six cold paths. The
  release leaves 8,976 B of the slot, the debug build 3,008 B.
- **The debug build** counts the ghosts over each window, sent in FIGURES 2 (37, its version 3, 366 B): a GHOSTS stage
  inside VOICES, the ghosts now and the most, made, back, ended and dropped by kind, each kind of engine's forward work
  and its time, the budget's use, the longest wait and the oldest note brought back; the device measures its own timer
  pair so the times are given net of it ([debug.md](../debug.md#the-ghosts-bytes)). The AUTOTEST's GHOST NOTES, in
  LOOPS, sets up 20 held notes against VOICES 8, then measures six stretches (over the count, mutes, a solo, VOICES
  changed, the keys over a song, a long note) and checks each on those figures.

## Juno-106 and Juno-60 banks on the EDITOR page

**Why.** Juno-106 patches go around as `.syx` captures of its patch messages, the Juno-106 Librarian's `.106`
libraries, `.pat` patch lists and recordings of its cassette dumps, the way Roland and patch makers ship whole banks;
Juno-60 patches exist only as cassette recordings. The EDITOR read DX7 `.syx` alone, so none of these reached PT/FM's
Juno-106 engine. The design is [the proposal](../proposals/202611111954-juno-import.md), the spec
[the editor's](../editor.md#juno-106-banks). The firmware does not change.

**How.**

- **The files.** `editor/js/conv_juno.js` reads the three kinds by their content: a Juno-106 `.syx` goes there before
  the DX7 test, which would take it for a broken DX7 dump; a `.pat` only when every line is a patch line, since other
  instruments share the extension. Each patch is the Juno-106's own 18 bytes, which convert into a cartridge slot and
  back exactly. The `.106` format was worked out from the files: big-endian, strings after their length, each value
  stored times one of 16 keys chosen by its place and the patch's; its check values are not read (their rule is not
  known, and nothing depends on them).
- **The tapes.** `editor/js/juno_tape.js` reads both machines from the sound's zero crossings, so neither the level nor
  the rate matters, only the ratio of lengths. The Juno-106's recordings turned out to be high-passed hard: the square
  wave falls to nearly 0 within its long halves, where noise made false crossings. So the hysteresis is a share of the
  halves' own peaks, not of the mean level, and each crossing is timed where the sound passes half the hysteresis on
  its new side (an edge is steep there, a droop never gets there). The Juno-60's bytes are read at their bits' middles
  from each start bit, the bit's length followed from frame to frame. Its records turned out to come with an idle frame
  between them, so each record is the bytes between two gaps, each byte placed by its time: a byte lost moves no other.
  The question on a drop and the dialog of tapes that did not read are `drops.js`'s; the reading as a take comes in,
  the line and SAVE AS CARTRIDGE `recorder.js`'s, which reads the whole take with what read it live and only a selected
  part again.
- **A Juno-60 patch as a Juno-106 one.** The same slider is a different sound on the two machines, so each Juno-60
  value is taken to a quantity by KR-106's Juno-60 model (its sound code, not its value displays, which differ from
  what it plays for VCF ENV, LFO DELAY and ENV D and R), and the Juno-106 value is the one whose quantity on PT/FM's
  own engine comes closest. `editor/tools/juno60/` holds the quantities (`quant.h`), the tables and rules
  (`convert.h`), a generator writing the page's tables (`editor/js/juno60_tables.js`, 113 KB) and made-up patches
  with their conversions (`vectors.json`), and the check; `editor/js/juno60.js` is `convert.h`'s twin, equal to it
  on every made-up patch and on every factory patch. Eleven sliders are tables; ENV R and VCF ENV, LFO and KYBD are
  rules on the patch's own values (the filter's movements depend on its cutoff, where the Juno-106 bends near its
  top; the Juno-60's release heads below zero, so its times depend on where it starts); ENV A and ENV S are two tables
  each, by VCA MODE.
- **What the check changed.** ENV A first matched the envelope's own times to half its peak and to its peak; slow
  attacks came out too slow, because with VCA MODE ENV the sound's rise is the envelope through each VCA's curve, which
  differ most at low levels: with ENV it now matches the sound's times to within 12 and 3 dB of its peak. ENV R a table
  of times from the sustain to a tenth of it missed the check's own best byte by far; from the level a second into a
  note it lands near it (a patch's best 40, the rule 42; another's 44 to 64, the rule 59). The measures themselves
  first measured artifacts: the engines' steps of DC, PT/FM's integer floor near −70 dB pulling an amplitude-weighted
  centroid up an octave on closed filters, release tails where the Juno-60 reaches silence and PT/FM's fades on. On
  the sound high-passed at 20 Hz, with a power-weighted centroid, a release in times to fall 10, 20 and 30 dB and pitch
  tracked near the note, the yardstick tightened from 16 dB to 0.77 octaves of time (release), 1,300 to 37 cents
  (vibrato depth), 4.7 to 0.85 octaves (brightness).
- **The choices the check made.** The Juno-60's 122 Hz HPF goes to the Juno-106's 236 Hz (summed error 13.9 against
  18.5 for FLAT, over the 17 factory patches using it). PWM SOURCE ENV, used with PULSE on and DCO PWM above 0 by 13
  factory patches, becomes MAN at the width the envelope gives on average over a note's first second: within every
  measure for 12 of the 13, against 11 for MAN at the sustain's width, 12 at the peak's (with larger errors) and 11 for
  LFO, which is never closer.

**The conversion's check** (one voice, C2, C4 and C6, 2 s held and 2.5 s of release, each measure a patch's worst
note): 111 Juno-60 factory patches (KR-106's data for both tapes, 3-4 of the second without its sliders there)
against the yardstick of the 128 Juno-106 factory patches.

| Measure | Yardstick (95th percentile) | Converted within it |
|---|---|---|
| attack | 3.46 octaves of time | 107 of 111 |
| level while held | 6.37 dB | 109 of 111 |
| release | 0.77 octaves of time | 107 of 111 |
| brightness | 0.85 octaves | 107 of 111 |
| pitch | 51 cents | 105 of 111 |
| vibrato depth | 36.5 cents | 100 of 111 |

Within every measure: 90 of 111. The overall level (reported, not counted): a median of −0.5 dB, −3.5 to +4.0 dB
between the 5th and 95th percentiles. Of the 21 past the yardstick, 17 have a self-oscillating filter or no
oscillator (the sound is the filter's ringing or noise), where the two models differ most: the yardstick's own 31
patches past its 95th percentile include 11 of that kind. The other four: two with an inverted envelope, whose level
rises slowly as the filter opens (the attack's measure is unsteady there); one with its filter nearly shut while held,

590 Hz filter, its level while held 7.5 dB off against 6.4 at C6. None points at a table.

**Tested** on the PC, with files and tapes the tests make (`tests/editor/juno_test.js`, from `juno_synth.js`): every

**Reviewed** twice; what the reviews found is fixed, each with a test: sounds whose data held a Juno message's first

**Checked on real files** (none of them in the repository): the Juno-106 factory patches as a `.pat` list and as a

its first a copy of the panel's sound (left out), gaps in its numbers closed; the Juno-106 factory cassette's
recording, bank group B equal to the factory patches in all 64 and group A in 62 (the recording holds other patches at
A74 and A88, which the libraries agree on); the Juno-60 factory cassette's recording, both dumps whole, every patch

with its checksum right. The decoders read those recordings resampled to 11 to 96 kHz, at −54 dB, clipped, and with
noise down to about 15 dB signal to noise (the Juno-106's) and 14 dB (the Juno-60's).

**Left:** writing a Juno-106 cartridge out (`.syx`, a tape); KR-106's own banks; Alpha Juno, Juno-6 and MKS-7; the
Juno-60's DCB messages. **Not yet:** a Juno's tape output recorded through the recorder from the machine itself.

## The crash screen names the culprit

**Why.** Two crashes of the 0.27 release under the heaviest two-core load (LOOPS with many Juno-106 loops, sends and
effects, mutes switched) stopped at program counters that cannot fault: one a write into a guarded window
(`DBG 00002000`) reported inside the DX7's render, the other an invalid write over the AXI bus (`DBG 00000020`)
reported inside the Juno-106's oscillator, whose traced branches pass no store. A bus fault is raised some cycles
after the access, so the screen's PC names the code running then, not the writer: it could have been core 1 a few
instructions earlier, core 2, or a DMA. The same session run on the PC (core 1's and core 2's work interleaved, every
effect on, mutes, solos and knobs at random, under the address and undefined-behaviour sanitizers) found nothing.

**How.** The chip's debug unit keeps who made the bad access: eight culprit registers after the debug flags (the
write-limit windows hit by each core and the DMAs, the DMA that hit one, the bus masters of an invalid AXI read and
write, the DMAs of an MMU error). The fault handler reads them first, with the debug flags, before anything else can
add a flag; then, with core 2 running, it pauses core 2 through its debug bank and reads its PC, as the chip's SDK
does in its own exception handler, before the hold that every crash makes. The screen shows the registers in two
rows (window masks two hex digits, master numbers four: they are about ten bits) and core 2's PC on its line,
`CORE 2 RUNNING AT`. To make room the smaller font's rows went from 13 to 12 px; core 1's window names were shortened
to fit the first row. The crash record kept for the next start is unchanged (its words, RECORDS' bytes): the new
values are the screen's alone, as both stack pointers already were.

**Checked** on the PC: the screen's host test draws a crash with the last one's words, a window hit with the stack
past its end, the widest each row can be (every window's name), and every state of core 2 with and without a PC;
every row in its box and clear of the others, release and debug. **Not yet** on the FM-1: the pause and the PC read
were never run on the device (only the SDK's handler uses them), and the registers' values are known only from the
SDK; the next crash reads them for the first time. **Left:** write windows for core 2 and the DMAs over core 1's
stacks and bands, so that a stray write by either faults at once, where it is made.

## A save in silence

**Why.** A save is written while nothing plays (SAVE waits for a pause), but the notes let go and the effects' tails
rang on, and with them the voices' and the effects' work on both cores between the save's flash steps. Each step
stops the flash for a moment with the interrupts off, after core 2 is idle and the output silenced, so the tails came
out in pieces anyway. An FM-1 froze until the watchdog restarted it after SAVE and PLAY pressed one after the other,
and a crash came during a save over ringing DELAY and REVERB tails; the same steps on the PC (the firmware's own code
on a simulated FM-1, under the sanitizers) save cleanly, so what is left is what only the device does: the flash's
timing, core 2 working beside core 1, the real load.

**How.** A save now begins with CUT's all sound off: every note cut, the output faded out over about 12 ms, the
effects emptied once it has faded, no note or effect worked on while it holds, so core 2 is handed nothing. The
writing starts 25 ms after SAVE, the fade over; the sound is back when the save ends, whatever its result. A CUT held
by hand and a save keep the sound off together: it comes back when neither holds it. The playground does the same.

**Checked** on the PC: a save over four loops of ringing Juno-106 pads, SAVE while they play, PLAY right after:
nothing is written before CUT is on, CUT holds through the whole save and lets go at its end, `SAVED`. **Not yet** on
the FM-1.

## PT/FM FROZE

**Why.** A crash leaves the red screen with what the fault found; a freeze left nothing: the watchdog restarts the
chip after about 8 s and PT/FM starts as if nothing had happened. Without where the main loop stood there is no way to
tell a flash step that never returned, a wait for core 2, a main loop starved by the audio interrupt, or interrupts
left off.

**How.** A watchdog restart keeps RAM, so a small record there (beside the crash record) holds what the next start
needs: the main loop's step, set by each part of its pass as it starts (the core's parts through the platform's step
call, the device's own around every flash step, the wait for core 2 before it, USB and an update), a flash step's
address, the time of the last pass and the interrupts' counts then, and the counts since, each interrupt marking
itself in and out. Core 2's queue keeps its own counts in memory nothing clears before core 2 starts. The start takes
the record before anything marks a new step, arms a fresh one, and once the panel and the keys work, P33 naming the
watchdog, shows it on a blue screen, PT/FM FROZE, until a press. All of it is stores: nothing measurable in the sound.
The debug build's own watchdog test never reaches the screen (its restarts run its test).

**Checked** on the PC: the screen's host test draws a freeze in a flash erase and the widest each row can be, with the
longest step names, in a release's and a debug build's version; every row fits. **Not yet** on the FM-1: no freeze
since has shown it.

## Core 2's own fault on the red screen

**Why.** The culprit registers did their job at the first crash after them: the invalid AXI write came from the
chip's second processor, PT/FM's core 2, twice, once over the save of ringing tails and once with nothing playing but
the keys. Core 1 was drawing the screen both times, innocent. But core 2's PC on the screen, read through its debug
bank, sat both times within a few bytes after the loop core 2 parks in after a fault of its own: core 2 had faulted
too, recorded its fault and parked, while core 1 still counted it as running and showed where the pause found it,
which says nothing about the faulting code. Core 2's record, with its PC and return address, was there all along.

**How.** At a crash, a core 2 whose fault record has grown since its start is shown as stopped by its fault: `CORE 2
FAULT AT` its record's PC and `R` its return address, in place of where it was paused. The record lives in core 2's
own memory, which restarts and installs leave alone and only a power-off loses, so a fault no screen showed (core 1
held core 2 and went on, or an older build's crash read the paused place) is shown at the next start, once, on a red
screen of its own, CORE 2 FAULTED: its words and the seconds that run had been up, to match it to the crash. A screen
that shows a record marks it shown, so none appears twice. The record's place in memory is the same as in the build
the last crashes came from, so the first start after an install shows their record if the FM-1 stayed on.

**Checked** on the PC: the screen's host test draws core 2's own fault on the red screen and the new start screen,
and the widest each can be; every row fits. **On the FM-1:** the first start after the install showed a record left by
the build before, core 2's fault inside its own memset, its handler hit by a second exception; that pointed at the
next change.

## Core 2's fills and copies stay in the RAM

**Why.** The record named core 2's memset as the place of the invalid write: a fill whose destination or length took
it out of the RAM. Crashes so far came when the sound was silent or nearly so, when the output stage and the effects
clear their buffers.

**How.** Core 2's memset and memcpy check their destination and its end against the RAM first, a compare and a branch
a call. One that would leave the RAM writes nothing: core 2's fault record says what it was (fill or copy, the caller,
the destination and length, the time up) and core 2 parks as at a fault of its own, so core 1 holds it at its next
half and goes on alone, as its existing fallback does; on core 1 (core 2 off, its jobs run there) the record alone.
STATUS' DEVICE names the refused write at once, under SECOND CORE, and the start screen after a restart shows it too.
The crash such a write caused cannot happen any more from these two; the cause, a wrong address handed to them, is
what the record names.

**Checked** on the PC: the compiled check read in the device's listing; the start screen's host test; every suite.
**Not yet** on the FM-1: no refused write has been seen.

## PT/FM FROZE, three fixes

**Why.** Two freezes on the FM-1 since the blue screen came, both with LOOPS playing many voices and no hands on it,

**Checked** on the PC: the screen's host test draws a freeze in the audio work's flash read and the longest step

## LOOPS: keys held into PLAY

**Why.** With the loops stopped, keys played freely and wrote nothing; to start a held note with the loops, a key had

loops sound them from the play position, each to its end as the play line comes to it. In the firmware the keys held

`stage_key_up` grow and end them by `ins_pos`; the strip draws them to where their time has run. The playground's
twin: `intoPlay` / `intoTick` (the new run forgets every key, so they are taken up again with their cells moved to the
play's first pass). With the stage off they sound on freely, written nowhere.

**Checked** on the PC: both twins hold a key two rows, press PLAY, hold it two rows more and find its note from the play
position to where the whole hold reaches (row 4 to 10), heard from the loops after PLAY (ui3 `test_loops_insert`,
playground `loops_lfo.js`).

**A review's fixes.** A review of it found: a jump could leave a key's note sounding forever (a forward jump dropped

from. Now the player plays a skipped off where the loops land (`pl_lp_landed`, once a landing, never per sample: not

## LOOPS: putting notes in and erasing by hand, and SEL held + ALGORITHM

## The core supplies raised, and what PT/FM FROZE names

**Why.** Four freezes on the FM-1 came after 10 to 53 minutes of LOOPS under heavy load, with core 2 on and with
core 1 alone, every one inside the sound code on core 1 with the input interrupt stopped too: a stall, not a loop.
The last one stopped in a Juno-106 voice with core 2 off, though STATUS still said SECOND CORE ON afterwards: core 2 had
been held by a fallback earlier in that run, which the screen could not say. Core 2's fallbacks and core 1's freezes
pointed the same way: both cores stalling under long load. The boot loader leaves the chip's supplies at 1.40 V and
1.26 V, under the chip maker's own table for its clock, and at those levels some FM-1s misread their RAM under load on
both cores; the FM-1's stock firmware sets 1.60 V and 1.38 V.

**How.** At every start, before any sound and before core 2, PT/FM raises VDC14 to 1.60 V and SYSVDD (the cores and the
RAM) to 1.38 V, up only, a level every 100 µs, VDC14 first, interrupts off (`fw/hal/fm1_supply.h`). PT/FM FROZE says
more: core 2 stopped by a fallback earlier in that run, the first time, with why, when and where it stood as the hold
came (`CORE 2 STUCK AT 1234 S PC 01C78472`, `MISREAD`, `NO ANSWER`, `FAULT`; the hold now pauses core 2 and reads its
PC); inside a Juno-106 voice, the part of its render the sound stood in and the voice and patch it rendered (the
engine's own marks, kept through the restart); and the supplies as this start raised them (`SUPPLIES 1.60 V, 1.38 V`,
or `: NOT RAISED`).

**Checked** on the PC: the screen's host test draws the new rows at their widest; the raise read in the compiled start
(two calls, the levels read back); every suite (three of the sound's test scripts had stopped linking with the
platform's new weak call and link it again). **On the FM-1:** three hours of LOOPS overloaded with every engine (16
voices taken all the time, about 600,000 notes taken from their voices, core 1 at 51 % on average and core 2 at 37 %),
core 2 on throughout: no freeze and no crash, where the four freezes came after 10 to 53 minutes; its halves ran late
at times under that overload (46 in the three hours, the worst 6,595 µs of 5,804), as before the change. It points to
the supplies as the cause.

## A sampler loop with no room

## LOOPS sets

**Why.** LOOPS keeps one working area. SAVE TO PATTERN and LOAD FROM PATTERN go through a pattern, which keeps the
loops' notes but not LOOPS itself: the loops' instruments by lane, their repeats and SHIFTs, the mutes and solos, the
mixes, the setup and LOOPS' own FX setup. Coming back to an earlier jam meant rebuilding all of that by hand, and a
jam kept meant losing the one being played. A set keeps the whole working area, solos included, under a number of its
own, so one FM-1 holds many jams; a store that left muted loops out was set aside: every loop is kept.

**How.** A set is a record of a new kind, 0x24 (a free number; numbers `00`-`EF`, `F0`-`FF` kept for built-in sets),
version 1: 16 bytes (the name, 16 characters as a song's, the solos a bit a loop), then the loops' record as it is, in
parts as the loops'. The store takes it as it takes LOOPS (one code path with the 16 bytes before the head), the
edit memory keeping its head, its streams' head and each stream as pieces, so a store is an edit SAVE keeps and REVERT
ALL drops, and a piece the same as the flash's leaves nothing unsaved. STORE AS A SET copies the working area's pieces
(or the flash's) into the set's, all the room reserved first and the store held while they are copied, so a stream
read never moves under the copy; LOAD A SET copies a set's into LOOPS' the same way, the solos, the selection and NEW
(the set's defaults) set after it, the loops stopped at their start (a song recording: silent there, PLAY bringing them
on) and the stage fitted to the length, all as LOAD FROM PATTERN does it, whose code the load shares. With the 16
bytes a stream of 3,800 B no longer fits part 0 beside a full head (13 B short; the loops' record has 3 B to spare):
a set's part 0 takes every stream that fits in loop order and leaves the others to the next parts, which keeps a set
to 16 parts but where all 16 loops pass 3,787 B each; such loops are refused (`THE LOOPS ARE TOO BIG FOR A SET`), and
the build checks that the head with the 16 bytes and a stream alone each fit a part. Set aside: a smaller stream limit
for every loop, and a 17th part.

The sets' list draws as the song list (number and name in 8 × 16, the loops and the length in bars in 6 × 12 on the
names' baseline, 10 lines) with its hint under the rows: the proposal's "as the pattern list, 13 lines" in 6 × 12 came
before every list that fits went to 8 × 16, and the sets' list fits. A length not whole bars of the set's METRONOME
reads to tenths (`4.5`, `.1` to `.9`). EDIT renames and LFO deletes after its question; PLAY does nothing. With no loops

## The list of records

**Why.** The store's map lists 560 records at most, 8 B each in RAM. A save that went past it was not refused: the
records were written, but the keys the map could not take were only counted as lost, the edits were dropped as saved,
and the new things vanished at once (a new LOOPS set gone from the list right after SAVE); compaction could then erase
their blocks, and a later power-on, keeping the first 560 keys its walk meets, could drop others. Sets make the limit
easy to reach: a set stores its loops in up to 16 parts, and there are 240 numbers for them.

**How.** A save is now counted against the list before anything of it is written, on every way the edits are written
(SAVE, a reference update's save, CLEAN UP's, SAVE NOW, the MIDI CONTROLLER's): the keys the map lists, plus each
record of the save whose key it does not. A plain guard would not do, for two reasons. The map lists a deleted thing's
key as long as its deleted record is in the flash (it hides the older copies), so deleting made no room until a
compaction, which on a store with room to spare never runs. And compaction dropped a deleted record from the map while
its bytes, and older copies in the same block, stayed in the freed block, so the next power-on listed it again: the map
did not list what a mount lists, and a count on it would refuse saves a power-on would take, or let through ones it
would not. So a save past the list first reclaims as many of the deleted records the map lists as it is over (a deleted
key it writes again counted as new, since its key comes back), the others left listed for a later save at the limit:
each one's older copies spoilt (the kind byte programmed to 0, as a power-on spoils a save cut short), then the deleted
record itself, its key gone; then it counts again. Reclaiming every deleted record at once was set aside: after a mass
delete one save made over 2,000 flash operations, seconds with the screen frozen, where one is needed. The newest save's
last record is never spoilt, since a power-on would take that save as cut short: when it is a deleted record and the
others are too few, the smallest live record is first written again as a save of its own, but only while compaction
keeps more than its 2 free blocks (with no more left, that deleted record stays listed and such a save can be refused by
one more; checking the open block's tail too cost 46 B the debug build lacks). Compaction now spoils
what it drops with the older copies in its block, and carries the newest save's last record along. So a save is refused
exactly when the things live and the records it writes take more than 560 keys, and the map lists what a power-on
lists. Deletions first, which already wrote a SAVE's deletions as a save of their own when room ran short, works for the
list too: at the limit a SAVE that deletes a song and makes a pattern goes through; one that deletes a pattern and makes
another is refused whole, as that deletion cannot go first (the new pattern could be made of the deleted one).

The refusal asks as `NOT ENOUGH STORAGE TO SAVE` does, with the same answers: `TOO MANY RECORDS TO SAVE`, how many
records more than the FM-1 lists, `DELETE SOMETHING, THEN SAVE`, OCT+ running CLEAN UP and then the save again. The
playground counts the same keys from its model: a pattern's tracks and a loop's stream sized as the firmware encodes
them (the demo disk's 64 patterns come out byte for byte as the disk's; the loops' encoder moved into the model, which
also corrects STORE AS A SET's size check and its room), laid out in parts as the firmware lays them out; the
one-of-a-kind records (the settings, DJ's setup, the MIDI map, LOOPS', the keys' song) counted once a save wrote them, as
the FM-1 keeps them, the settings also written when the current instrument, the EDITOR's pattern or the last screen
changed, DJ's stand counted before the save puts it in; deletions first by records, a built-in's BACK TO BUILT-IN among
them. It asks the same question. One difference is left, rare at the limit: the FM-1 also takes as new a thing
made anew under a number the flash holds (a DELETE, then a NEW or CLONE there), which the playground, knowing numbers
alone, sees as a change. With only compaction's two free blocks left, the FM-1 keeps a deleted record that ends the
newest save listed; writing that thing again under its own number keeps its key, so it saves, as the playground does.
The EDITOR page's WRITE already refused a panel past the room the FM-1 reports; it now gives the panel's own count when
the panel does not fit even written anew, not one with the FM-1's records the first plan kept or deleted.

## LOOPS: CUT a loop

**Why.** Every loop played over the one shared length, so loops could not run against each other: no phasing, no
parts of different lengths for generative music. A cut gives one loop a window of its own that it wraps round while
the length stays, so it drifts against the others and comes back in line only after the least common multiple of the
two ([../proposals/202611181212-loops-cut.md](../proposals/202611181212-loops-cut.md)).

**How.** The window is fixed where it was set: the loops' rows, counted from their start across the length's end (the
player's pass × the length + the row), go round it, and at each of its rows the loop plays what it plays at that row
with no cut, its SHIFT included. So SHIFT turns the loop's notes under the window, and other parts of it come into
view; the proposal had SHIFT moving the mark with the notes, changed while it was built. A cut loop's base is the
length, played once: cutting writes a loop's repeats and rest out over the length first (its SHIFT kept), so it
sounds as it did, and the window holds what was heard there. The record needed 7 bits a loop where its entry had one
never read (byte 2's bit 6): that bit says byte 5, a repeat count elsewhere, is the cut. Version 4 stays, nothing is
converted, and an older PT/FM reads byte 5 as a repeat count over a base of the length: the loop played once, uncut. A
bit 6 over another base reads as no cut, as an older PT/FM's edit of it leaves it. The stage over a cut loop is that
loop's window too: the player reads it round the cut of the loop whose track it sounds through, and LOOPS' screen turns
the loops' place into the stage's own (`sf_from`, `sf_to`) wherever the keys write, hold their lanes or ask for a note
at a row, so the keys, the line and OVERWRITE's eraser all work in the window; EDIT brings the loop as heard with no
cut and REC writes it back with the cut kept, SHIFT 0. SAVE TO PATTERN writes the first pass round the window with a
note off at each wrap where a note still sounds at the cut. LENGTH keeps a cut it stays longer than (a shifted cut
loop written out as any), DOUBLE writes a cut loop's notes twice (the cut kept: it plays the same).

The menu item is `CUT HERE` (a menu holds fixed texts; the toast gives the place, `LOOP 03: CUT AT 2.4.3`); SEL held +
EDIT is the screen's own combination, CUT HERE at EDIT's release before HOLD DELAY, REMOVE CUT past it. The strip
draws the whole loop with the notes past the mark dimmed, the mark in the loop's colour and its window's place now as
a second line; the slot's line says `CUT`. The EDITOR page reads and keeps the cut (its LOOPS tab shows `CUT 6` in place
of the repeats) and writes a cut loop's first pass for an FM-1 that reads only version 3.

**Checked** on the PC: the loops' tests (a cut set, moved, removed, refused at the first row; the record's bits; SAVE TO
PATTERN's first pass with and without SHIFT; LENGTH both ways and its drop; DOUBLE; REC into a cut loop; a set; SAVE and
a power cycle; an older PT/FM's edit read as no cut); the player's (the drift over two passes, a note ended at the wrap,
PLAY from the start again, SHIFT under the window, the stage over a cut loop); the firmware's UI test (the menu, SEL +
EDIT tapped and held, the strip's pixels, a key landing on the window's row); the playground's twin of each; the
EDITOR's (byte for byte, a cut refused over another base, the first pass written down). Not tried on the FM-1.

## AUTOMATIONS

**Why.** A performance of mutes, knob sweeps or notes played into LOOPS, done again by the FM-1 itself. The design is
[the proposal](../proposals/202611181734-automations.md); the specs [the screen](../ui/screens/automations.md) and [the
file](../files/automations.md).

**How.**

- **The screen** (`fw/core/ui/scr_auto.c`): HOME + OP6, the last of HOME's round (`ui.c`: SCREEN_KEY, ROUND of 11,
  `ui_round_at` / `ui_round_screen`); a 4 × 4 grid of slots (number, the screen recorded on, the length); SELECT,
  ALGORITHM or a white key picks; REC, PLAY at its release, SEL's RECORD / PLAY / CLEAR / REVERT; the header's `AUTO 03`
  after a red dot (recording) or a green arrow (playing).
- **The recorder and the player** sit in `ui_poll`, after MIDI's learned controls are merged (`ua_input`). Recording
  takes each pass's changes in the order the UI takes them (the clicks before the first press, the presses, the other
  clicks, the releases); HOME's press is held back until something shows it is not the end; HOME tapped, then OP6, is
  rewound out of the stream at the end (to the mark taken before HOME's press, also from bytes already in the edit
  memory), so the end's own controls are never in it; what is held at the end is let go there; a release of what was
  down before is left out. Playing stands in for the pass's input, a recorded pass a pass; a press of the player's stops
  it (masked till let go), MASTER always the hardware's. The reader never reads past a part (a part cut short ends the
  playback there).
- **The file** (kind 0x32): a record a slot's part, part 0's first byte the screen; events bit-packed (time classes of
  6 to 28 bits, actions of 7 to 9): a note about 4.4 B. The recording gathers in RAM (192 B) and goes into the edit
  memory about once a second; a part closes when its next event might not fit, so every writer writes the same bytes.
- **The EDITOR page**: a system item (one on the FM-1 panel, any number on the TABLE), its tab the 16 slots and the
  picked slot's events in words, edited, inserted, deleted, the rows the FM-1 would skip grey; slots copied and pasted
  between files (`editor/js/records.js`, `tab_system.js`; [editor.md](../editor.md)). Disks carry it beside the settings.
- **Room:** about 3.7 KB of code, 364 B of RAM.

from SONGS; HOME + OP4 recorded and played back, landing on PLAYBACK; the stream read by the test's own reader: time 0

## The ghosts' load guard

**Why.** The FM-1's AUTOTEST put DJ HEAVY (PUNCH IN and MIDNIGHT RUN mixed on DJ, VOICES 16) at 15 late halves in 30 s
and a worst half of 118-119 % on the builds before the guard, against 1-3 and about 100 % on 0.27; at VOICES 12 (the first core alone) 79 late
halves. The mean barely moved (63.8 % against 61.2): the difference was in the busiest halves.

**Where it came from.** DJ HEAVY rendered on the PC gives the FM-1's ghosts' figures to the unit (93 made, 3 back into
free engines and 79 into tails', 13,333 units in 299 blocks, the longest wait 57 blocks). A row starting 8-10 notes over
a full pool spends its block's 4 fades on takes, making 3-5 ghosts; in the next block those ghosts take the release
tails the row just let go, 3 fades more, all on the first core, their starts and forward work in the same half. 0.27
dropped those notes and had only the takes' fades there.

**How.** At each block's walk the half's first-core work is counted in the budget's units (`pl_heavy`): each of the
first core's engines with a note in it for the half's 4 blocks at its kind's weight (DX7 78, JUNO-106 64, sampler 52,
TB-303 76, CHIP 40: a block and its mixing in, the FM-1's means), 115 a note started in the half, 64 a fade begun in it,
400 while DJ plays; over 4,200 (about 90 % of a half) the walk waits for the next block, no return beginning or going
on. Nothing is measured, so the FM-1, the emulator and the playground (`dsp-core.js`, per 256-frame half) decide
alike. The cap sits above all the demo disk reaches by itself (its most, CITY LIGHTS at VOICES 8, counts 4,021). Rules
tried and left: holding only new returns, holding only tails' claims, caps from 2,800 to 3,900 (no better at 16); a cap
of 2,800 brings VOICES 12 to 0.27's but holds returns into tails whenever 12 engines are busy, at about 74 % of a half,
and halves the returns: it acts below overload.

**What it changes.** Every demo song and DJ loop at VOICES 8, 12 and 16, core 2 on and off (108 renders of 240 s), is
bit-identical to the code before. In DJ HEAVY it holds 40 walks in 30 s at 16 (236 at 12): returns come 1-2 blocks later,
a few then into a free engine instead of a tail (8 instead of 3; tails' 75 instead of 79); the longest wait 59 blocks
(57). A model of the first core's half from the FM-1's per-piece times over the PC's counts: VOICES 16 worst 104.6 % ->
98.3 % (0.27: 94.5), no half over 100 % (3 before); VOICES 12 113.1 -> 107.5 % (0.27: 103.5), 9 halves over 100 % (23).
What is left at 12 is the takes making ghosts in the busy halves and the fuller pool (742 takes against 0.27's 518), not
the returns' work. The debug build's ghosts' figures count the walks it held (FIGURES 2 now 366 B,
[debug.md](../debug.md#the-ghosts-bytes)). Room: 164 B of code, 2 B of RAM.

**Checked** on the PC: run_sound (c2_test in every mode, the 16-voice session against one core), ui3 (the figures' round
trip), fuzz_loops 4 seeds x 3 min, AUTOTEST's GHOST NOTES (89 walks held, each one that would have found no engine; the
other figures the same), the playground's ghosts, forward, sound and checks (`voices_test.c` and `ghosts.js` a new
case each). **On the FM-1** (the debug build's `FIXED SPLIT` scenarios, which keep this guard and the fixed split): it held as
counted, 47 walks at VOICES 16 and 238 at 12, but the late halves stayed, 16 and 80 in 30 s (15 and 79 before it): the
model's worst halves are under the FM-1's. Moving the split each block brought them to 5 and 4
([below](#the-voices-split-between-the-cores-moved-every-block)).

## LOOPS: the stage's keys heard at once

**Why.** With the stage on and the loops playing, a key was heard exactly as the stage kept it: one played before its
QUANTIZE line waited for it, up to half a line late (a whole beat at `4 ROWS`). It let the take be heard as it would
be kept, but played live it felt laggy. A key now sounds at its press, as everywhere else, and the stage keeps it on
the line as before ([the proposal](../proposals/202611201337-loops-keys-at-once.md)).

**How.** Only the keys' own sound moved; what the stage holds, writes and plays is as it was (lanes, OVERDUB and
OVERWRITE, MONO LEGATO's `3xx`, `LEG` and `SLD` in the cells, the holds from where a note lands, the line, the insert,

asks it at once with `PL_LP_NOW`, and the player still queues the entry, which only stands in for the stage's cell at
that row: the note is heard once in the pass it is played and from the stage after. A key's release ends its sound at
once (a key held into PLAY keeps its own time). REAL TIME's keys were heard their note delay after the press (the
delay passed to an at-once note, up to about 100 ms) while the playground played them at once; both sound at the press
now, the cell keeping its `EDx`. A MONO key's sound and the stage's note it will cut overlap until the key's note lands,

now heard at the press, it is moved to its landing row by the recorder, ahead as well as behind (within three rows),
and so is its end, which is heard at the release too. Notes and ends land in slots of their own (a quick tap lands both
before the recorder reads either); a note's landing stays for a MONO chord's keys, an end's is used once. The landings

## The EDITOR's realtime takes heard at once

**Why.** The EDITOR's takes had the same lag as LOOPS' stage: a key played before its QUANTIZE line was heard only when
the replay reached the cell it wrote ([the proposal](../proposals/202611201537-editor-takes-at-once.md)).

**How.** A take's keys sound on the replay's own tracks, so the cell written ahead has to stand in for itself, its end
as well as its note: an end the replay played later would cut the next note played on that track meanwhile. The take
asks the player for the cell as before (`player_take_cell`); the player now plays it at once whenever its row is ahead
and keeps, a track each, the note and the end it is to leave out, named by that row's count and the row
(`pl_tk_skip`, 128 B), so a jump or a pattern's break cannot meet the wrong row. When the replay reaches that row its
start of the note (with EDx, E9x or Rxy) or its let-go (an off, K00, Kxx at its tick) is left out that once; every
start empties the table. A key's release lets its note go at once even when nothing is written for it (a note already
on the off's row, no room for Kxx, the edit memory full). What a take writes is unchanged: the landing, the track a
note goes to, the offs, the take going round, the starts by PLAY held + a key and COUNT-IN `KEY`. The playground's
replay does the same, its stand-ins named by the track, the pass and the row.

Checks: ui3's three new ones (a key at row 10.5 at `4 ROWS` heard at the press, not again at 12, written there and

## The keys' velocity

**Why.** The FM-1's keys carry no velocity sensor, but each is a carbon pill on one contact of the diode matrix, whose
resistance falls with the force on it, and two of the matrix's rows, PA7 and PA8, are inputs of the chip's converter:
20 of the 27 note keys, F3 to C5, can be read. A tracker played from its keys gains most from playing them as hard as
they are pressed, and the path was there already: a MIDI note's velocity is its note's volume everywhere.

**How.** The scan reads them while SETTINGS' KEYS VELOCITY is not CONSTANT (`fw/hal/fm1_input.h`, `fw/hal/fm1_adc.h`,
`fw/device/keyvel.h`; `hardware.md`'s keys' velocity). Once the tick has latched a column it starts one conversion of
that column's PA7 key (even frames) or PA8 key (odd), and takes it first thing at the next tick, before the rows are
read: every key every 2.2 ms. A touched key's every other reading is driven: its row an output high at the second-weakest
drive (HD0 alone, the level the readings' figures were measured at, where the pull-up reads only the matrix diode), an
input with its pull-up again at the take. A held key's column is dark while it is read (a lit LED lifts the reading);
OCT-'s slot reads MASTER, which the main loop takes from there; the battery is read in a short pause of the readings,
every 2 s as before. A key's readings run a small state machine in one word (open, touched, seated, lifting; the lowest
driven reading, the landing), the tick doing no loop and no divide: the landing is the first driven reading after the
touch, and the digital scan's press waits for it before it reaches `plat_input`'s notes, 10 ms at most (then KEYS
VOLUME); the digital release opens the readings again, so a press never takes an older one's landing. The landing's
velocity is mapped where the press is taken (the main loop, or the controller's 1 kHz tick): t on the log of the pill's
resistance between 530 (127) and 896 (1), velocity = 1 + 126 (1 - t)^c with SOFT 0.25, NORMAL 0.5 and HARD 1.0 (first SOFT 0.47, NORMAL 0.70 and HARD 1.05: NORMAL needed too much force on the FM-1), in
integers (a log2 table and the curves' tables). The top seven keys, which have no converter, play at the mean of the last
four keys read. The core asks the platform (`plat_key_touch`, `plat_key_vel`, `plat_key_reading`; `plat_weak.c`'s
defaults read nothing, so every host and test plays KEYS VOLUME); `ui_key_vol` gives a press its volume, a MIDI note's
velocity, else the key's own (MIRROR's Note On, or the FM-1's touch), else KEYS VOLUME, and every key path takes it: the
keys' notes, the EDITOR's takes and steps, LOOPS' stage and its keys, the previews, the arpeggiator, MIDI OUT's KEYS,
MIDI CONTROLLER's notes (the mode's own 127 / 100 without one). The EDITOR's takes and steps now write a MIDI note's
velocity too, as the settings' spec says they do (they wrote KEYS VOLUME). KEYS VELOCITY is saved in the settings
record's byte 123, bits 5-4, 0 being NORMAL, so a record from before reads NORMAL and the record keeps its 133 bytes.

**MIDI CONTROLLER's aftertouch.** The sound takes no aftertouch in 1.0, but a held key's driven readings go on, so the
controller's general mode sends the keys' pressure (SETTINGS' CONTROLLER AFTERTOUCH, the MIDI page: CHANNEL, Channel
Pressure of the hardest-pressed key; POLY, each key's on its note, 0 before its Note Off; OFF). The pressure is pressing
harder than the key rests: a per-key rest following the reading down for 250 ms after the landing, then creeping after
it, back up at once when the key eases; log2(rest / reading), 0.04 octave nothing, 0.46 more 127, smoothed over ~30 ms,
worked out every 5 ms in the main loop (`fw/core/ctl.h`), its state packed in 16-bit fields in the controller's own
memory (the store's scratch: no RAM of the tracker's), sent on change by the controller's tick. Byte 123's bits 7-6 keep
the setting (0 CHANNEL).

**The twins.** The virtual FM-1's keys give each press the landing its height on the key stands for (the top edge 896,
the bottom 530, evenly on the log between: the curve's middle at the key's middle), a held key dragged down a new one;
the emulator hands it to the firmware's own map (`web_key_reading`), the playground's model maps it with the same
integers (its checksum over every landing and curve is the firmware's), the top row at the same mean; a press with no
position (the computer's keyboard, a test) plays at KEYS VOLUME, so the existing tests run as before. An FM-1 as the
controller in mirror mode gives the page its own velocities. The playground's MIDI CONTROLLER computes the aftertouch
with the same steps (the same sum over a canned key as `ctl_test`'s).

**The DX7's velocity.** A DX7 note's velocity was 100 whatever its volume, which only scaled what it rendered: the
patches' KEY VEL SENS did nothing. Now a note's volume at its start (a cell's, a key's, a MIDI note's, LOOPS', the
arpeggiator's, a DJ deck's, a preview's) is the velocity of the operators that modulate (the ones not on the output),
the carriers staying at 100: harder is brighter, as each operator's KEY VEL SENS sets, and the loudness is the
volume's gain as before (on the PC an operator at KEY VEL SENS 7 on OP2, modulating OP1: 0.5 dB softer at 127 than at
20, and 11.5 times the first differences' energy). `dx7_note_on` takes the two velocities (`fw/core/eng/dx7/dx7.c`);
the voice keeps its start's volume in the byte its parameters' list leaves beside the Juno-106's patch, so a note that
comes back after losing its voice keeps its own, with no RAM more and nothing in the sound's time but the note's start.
At volume 100 (`64`), or with no KEY VEL SENS, a voice sounds as before, and the engine's reference tests are bit for
bit the same. The playground's DX7 does the same (its DX7 notes no longer play at 127 on an ACC, which is the TB-303's).

## A save in parts

On the FM-1, AUTO LEVEL ALL changed about 50 KB of cartridges and its SAVE asked NOT ENOUGH STORAGE TO SAVE with 50 KB
free: a SAVE writes every changed record before the ones they replace are freed (all or nothing), so it needed the
room of all of them at once, though it added nothing. An edits' SAVE that does not fit at once, compaction run and
its deletions written first, now goes in parts (store.md#a-save-in-parts): `part_stop()` asks room_lack for the
edits' first things, one whole thing more at a time (their records counted again each time: a few hundred cartridge
builds at most, well under a second), and takes the most that fit; that part is a save of its own (`sv_stop` bounds the save's things), then its edits go (`parts_drop`), and
the rest is committed again, its compaction freeing what the part replaced. A changed thing so costs room only until
its part is written; only when not even the first thing fits does the SAVE ask, `# KB MORE NEEDED` what that thing
lacks (`save_short`). A deletion an edit could be made from (a MOVE's or a CLONE's, as deletions first decides) is
in no part and waits for its new thing; `parts_drop` keeps a deletion the flash still holds. The save's progress
counts across its saves (`p_done`, `p_all`), deletions first included. The playground counts the user area as it
will stand after a save, which a save in parts now gives: no change there. Checked: store_test's `t_save_parts` (15
songs changed past the room of the free blocks: one SAVE in several saves, the percent never going back, every song
as edited after a power-cycle; a ~30 KB pattern added past the room: refused, short by what it lacks, none of it
written) and `t_move_parts` (a MOVE of a full pattern with FE changed and 2 blocks free: 21 saved before 20's
deletion; cut at each of 252 flash operations, whole or torn, a power-on finds the pattern at 20, at 21 or both, never
neither); `t_editor_room_delete` no longer expects that MOVE refused. store: 9,159 checks (9,161 with the 96 KB
