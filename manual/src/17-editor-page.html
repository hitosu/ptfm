The EDITOR page

The EDITOR page, editor.html, is PT/FM's page for the computer (Chrome or Edge, offline). It comes with PT/FM, as install.html does: get it from PT/FM's home, hitosu.github.io/ptfm. It backs up the FM-1, brings new content to it (tracker modules, MIDI files, DX7 cartridges, sounds), and edits it with the FM-1's own pictures. It is a beta: its two panels are finished, its edit tabs work with plain controls.

Two panels

  • TABLE (left): everything you drop on the page, kept in the browser. Its items have no numbers, so it holds any number of songs, patterns and cartridges, and two imported modules never clash.
  • FM-1 (right): an image of what an FM-1 holds, with its numbers.
  • Drag items between the panels (or use the arrows): an item moves with everything it uses (a song with its patterns, their instruments, their samples), and numbers are given as it lands on the FM-1 panel. DELETE removes items; UNDO takes back any step.
  • A built-in cartridge moved to the TABLE becomes a copy of its own (with its changes, when it was changed), to change there as you like. Moved back to the FM-1 panel it takes a free number, a new cartridge beside the built-in, and a song pointing at it plays the copy. A song moved to the TABLE still plays the built-ins it uses: those never travel, and a changed one goes along keeping its number.
  • Both panels group their items by kind: SONGS, PATTERNS, CARTRIDGES, SAMPLERS, SAMPLES and SYSTEM (the settings, the MIDI map, the loops, DJ's setup, the EDITOR's own pattern and the keys' song). A click on a group's header folds or opens it. Each panel shows its items as DETAILS (a row each, with sizes and what uses them), LIST or ICONS.
  • A sample is an item of its own, not a part of a sampler: on the FM-1 panel it has its number (23 KICK), on the TABLE none. A sampler holds instruments only (its row shows how many samples they play, SAMPLER · 10 INSTR · 7 SMP), and the instruments of any sampler can play any sample on their panel. DELETE on a sample asks first and takes the instruments that play it, in every sampler there; DELETE on a sampler takes its instruments, and ALSO WHAT ONLY IT USES takes the samples no other sampler plays.
  • Older forms are converted as the page loads them (READ, a disk, a backup, what the TABLE keeps): a sampler of an older PT/FM becomes a sampler of instruments and samples of their own, the same sample used by two samplers becoming one. The FM-1 holds 256 samples at most: a sampler whose samples would pass that stays in the older form, an item marked OLDER FORM on the FM-1 panel, listed with its size, not played or edited; the page says how many numbers it needs, and deleting it, or other samples, frees them.
  • Picking works as in a file manager: a click picks one item, Ctrl adds one, Shift a range, Ctrl+A all. A double-click (or Enter) opens the item's tab, Space plays it, Delete deletes it, F2 renames it; the right-click menu has the rest.
  • SET NUMBER… (F4, on the FM-1 panel) gives a cartridge another number, 00 to EF. All the cartridge kinds share these numbers, samplers and FUSION too, so a new cartridge can land after a gap; samples have numbers of their own, so a sample and a cartridge can both be 0A. A free number moves the cartridge; a taken one swaps the two. Everything that names it follows: the patterns' notes, the loops, your favourites. The built-in instruments keep their numbers.
  • The space bar under the FM-1 panel shows the FM-1's room: 103 blocks of 4 KB, as data, audio and free. Keep at least 16 blocks free for the FM-1's own saves: with fewer than 10 free, the FM-1 cannot save new things (it can still delete), and with fewer than 3 it cannot save at all until a WRITE frees room.

An item's tab

Every item opens in a tab of its own: double-click it, or EDIT IN A TAB in its menu. The tab edits a copy: SAVE puts the changes into the item, on its panel, and closes the tab; closing a tab with changes not saved asks SAVE, DISCARD or KEEP EDITING. A change saved on the FM-1 panel reaches the FM-1 only with WRITE. The pictures in a tab are the FM-1's own, drawn by PT/FM's own code exactly as the FM-1's screen shows them, and they follow the controls as you move them.

Song
Its properties (NAME, TEMPO, TICKS A ROW, ROWS A BEAT, SWING, AT THE END), its order (each position's pattern from a list, moved, removed or added), TRIM (tick the positions to keep), the MIXER (each track's LEVEL, PAN, DUCK and SENDS) and the FX setup (the chain's order by dragging, each effect on or off, its knobs).
Pattern
The grid, made for the mouse and the finger: a click picks a cell, a drag marks a block. The cell's panel has a keyboard of the FM-1's 27 keys with NOTE OFF and NO NOTE, the instrument picked from a list of the cartridges by name, the volume, and up to four commands, each with its meaning in words. On the computer's keyboard, the arrows, Page Up / Page Down, Home, End and Tab move; the letter keys write notes as the FM-1's keys play them (Z S X D C F V ... from F-3); = writes a note off; Delete clears; Ctrl+C, Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V copy, cut and paste; Space plays.
Instrument
Its NAME, LEVEL (AUTO LEVEL measures it as the FM-1 does) and its engine's parameters, each page beside the FM-1's picture of it. Every instrument page has a keyboard of the FM-1's 27 keys, played by mouse, touch, the computer's keys, or a MIDI keyboard picked from the computer's inputs. With VIRTUAL MIDI KEYBOARD picked, the computer's keys play anywhere in the tab (Z S X D C ... from F-3 as the FM-1's keys; [ and ] change the octave).
Sample
Its waveform: drag across it to select a part (nothing is selected when the tab opens). PLAY THE SELECTION, TRIM (the sample becomes the selection), TRIM SILENCE, WHOLE SAMPLE, SAVE AS A NEW SAMPLE (the selection as a sample of its own, beside this one); the slices (below); CHANNELS (both mixed, left or right of a stereo sound), RATE, DEPTH (8 or 16 bits), the sizes before and after, and PLAYED BY, the instruments that play it, in every sampler on its panel. Their START, END and loop points follow a cut when you SAVE. OPTIMIZE (below) makes it smaller.
Sampler
The cartridge's name and its instruments in a list (64 slots). An instrument's page has its SAMPLE (any sample on the panel; picking another re-points the instrument, keeping its other settings), START, END, the loop, BASE NOTE, TUNE, VOLUME and the envelope, each with the FM-1's picture. On an instrument, REPLACE SAMPLE picks a sample from the panel and the instrument plays it as a new one would, every setting back to a new instrument's; DELETE takes the instrument and leaves its sample. On an empty slot, CREATE picks a sample and makes an instrument there that plays it whole, named after it. THE SAMPLES IT USES lists each sample its instruments play, with NEW INSTRUMENT FOR IT, which makes an instrument for it at the first empty slot. Pasting an instrument brings its sample along: nothing is copied when the panel holds the same sample, otherwise the sample is added at a free number; with none free the paste is refused (NO FREE SAMPLE NUMBER).
SETTINGS, MIDI MAP, LOOPS, DJ SETUP
Every setting (and your 16 GATE patterns as rows of steps), each MIDI mapping, the loops, and DJ's crossfader, filter, decks and effects. LOOPS shows the length in its title (it is changed on the FM-1) and each loop's instrument, MONO or POLY, repeats and SHIFT: +3 plays the loop 3 rows later, -3 earlier (typed either way); the notes are shown as stored, and a line under them says how SHIFT plays them.

Your own sounds

A sound dropped on the page becomes a sample, a recording. To play it from the FM-1's keys it also needs an instrument that plays it: the instrument sets its note, loop and envelope. The page does not make one for you. On the page:

  1. Drop the sound (WAV, FLAC, MP3, OGG ...) on the TABLE.
  2. Drag it onto the FM-1 panel: it becomes a sample there, at a free number, on its own, in no sampler. Dragged onto a sampler cartridge's item, it comes with an instrument at the cartridge's first empty slot, playing it whole and named after it (64 instruments at most in a sampler).
  3. For a sample that has no instrument yet, open a sampler cartridge's tab: in THE SAMPLES IT USES or on an empty slot, CREATE (a pick of any sample on the panel) makes the instrument. Set its BASE NOTE (the note the recording plays at its own pitch) and its loop, then SAVE.
  4. WRITE.

Or make the instrument on the FM-1, after the WRITE: INSTRUMENTS, a sampler cartridge, an empty slot; SEL, CREATE (or EDIT there) lists the samples (the keys play the one highlighted, ENV picks it) and makes the instrument. Set its BASE NOTE and loop, then SAVE (chapter 8).

Recording and slicing

RECORD on the TABLE records from any input your computer has: a microphone, a line in, or the FM-1 itself over USB (on Windows its input is called Line (FM-1 PT/FM)). Pick the INPUT, watch the meter (CLIP lights when the sound hits the top), then RECORD and STOP (or Space). Its play key plays the take as it is, shown in the footer's player like anything else that plays; SAVE puts it on the TABLE as a sample, TAKE00, TAKE01 and on, at the best quality the FM-1 holds (16 bits, 44.1 kHz). RECORD again replaces the take in the dialog; nothing is heard while it records.

A recording of several hits, a drum played one stroke after another or a phrase of notes, is cut into slices in its tab: DETECT SLICES finds where each sound starts (SENSITIVITY 1-10 finds fewer or more), marking each slice on the waveform; the quiet parts between are silent slices, drawn dim. Drag a marker to move a slice's start, double-click to add one (or ADD A SLICE at the selection's start), click a slice to pick it (Ctrl or Shift for several) and DELETE THE MARKER (or Delete) to take its start away. Each slice has its own play key. Then:

  • REMOVE SILENCE takes the silent slices out, the rest joined with short crossfades; REMOVE takes out the slices picked.
  • SAVE AS SAMPLES makes each slice picked (all but the silent ones when none is) a sample of its own.
  • MAKE A KIT makes a playable kit of them: a sampler cartridge with an instrument for each slice, and a FUSION cartridge whose instrument plays slice 1 on C-4, slice 2 on C#4 and up, a key each. Both open in their tabs.

New samples are named after the one they came from, with two digits (KICK00, KICK01), and are samples of their own on the same panel; on the FM-1 panel they take free sample numbers (the page says when none is left, ALL 256 SAMPLE NUMBERS USED). A sampler holds 64 instruments.

Making room

Samples take most of the FM-1's room. The page can make them smaller: in a sample's tab, OPTIMIZE offers three levels, each with its size and what it saves, heard before you SAVE:

LOSSLESS
Changes nothing you hear: it cuts what no instrument plays (before the first START, after the last END or LOOP END) and the silent tail.
SMALLER
Adds the rate the page proposes for the sound (the lowest that loses nothing heard).
SMALLEST
Adds 8 bits, a softer top, and a level 6 dB lower, made up by the instruments' LEVEL.

On the TABLE, OPTIMIZE SAMPLES does the same to every sample picked (and every sample of a picked sampler) at once, listing each level's size before and after. UNDO takes it back.

Reading, writing and backing up

Connect the FM-1 by USB with PT/FM running. Nothing stays connected: each action finds the FM-1, does its work and lets go.

READ
Reads what the FM-1 holds into the FM-1 panel (after the first time, only what changed). Samplers of an older PT/FM are converted as they are read (above).
WRITE
Makes the FM-1 hold exactly the panel. Before writing it shows what it writes and what it removes (in red), warns when the FM-1 saved something since the last READ (READ FIRST, WRITE ANYWAY or CANCEL) and when fewer than the 16 advised blocks would stay free, and offers to back up first: what the write would replace goes onto the TABLE (BACKUP and the time). When the panel does not fit around what the FM-1 keeps but fits the FM-1 emptied, it writes the whole user area anew and says so first (it takes longer). The FM-1's sound stops while it writes. With unsaved edits on the FM-1 it is refused: SAVE OR REVERT FIRST. After a conversion, the first WRITE rewrites every sample, says so, and offers its backup of the old records first. When the FM-1 runs an older PT/FM that reads only the older form, WRITE converts down: each sampler is written as an older one holding copies of the samples its instruments play (the samples themselves are not sent), and NOT KEPT names what the older form cannot hold (samples no instrument plays, which are not written; a sample several samplers play, written once in each). It then asks WRITE ANYWAY or CANCEL.
REVERT
Puts back what the panel showed after the last READ or WRITE.
DOWNLOAD DISK
Saves the FM-1 panel as a disk file (ptfm-2026-10-26.ptfmdsk). This is the backup: READ, then DOWNLOAD DISK.
DOWNLOAD AS DISK
On the TABLE, under its list: saves only what you picked there as a disk file, with everything it uses, numbered as it would land on an empty FM-1 (the built-ins left out, as every PT/FM has them). One item picked names the file (punch-in.ptfmdsk), several make ptfm-selection-2026-11-04.ptfmdsk. What would not fit an FM-1 is refused: NO DISK SAVED. UPLOAD DISK puts it in an FM-1 panel; dropped on a TABLE, it gives the items back.
UPLOAD DISK
Puts a disk file in the FM-1 panel, numbers kept; WRITE then puts it on the FM-1. A backup put back is exactly as it was.
Before installing another FM-1 firmware, READ and DOWNLOAD DISK: other firmwares erase PT/FM's memory. The same before going back to an older PT/FM: an older version does not read samples that are things of their own (it would leave the longer ones out as damaged), so READ and DOWNLOAD DISK first, install it, then WRITE, which converts down.

Bringing content in

Drop files on the TABLE (or + ADD FILES):

Disks
.ptfmdsk files: their items as they are.
Tracker modules
MOD, S3M and XM: the song, its patterns (a channel a track, 16 at most), its samples as samples of their own, and sampler cartridges (64 instruments each) with an instrument for every module instrument or sample. Samples past the 256th that the FM-1 can number are left out, and the page says how many. IT is not read; XM's envelopes are not kept.
MIDI files
A song: each channel's notes become tracks, notes on the nearest row, the tempo kept, patterns of 4 bars; each channel's instrument mapped onto a built-in sound (all 128 General MIDI programs have one), the drums onto FM DRUM KIT. Pitch bend and controllers are left out.
DX7 cartridges
.syx, the 32-voice dump: a DX7 cartridge, exactly; a single voice's dump: a cartridge of one.
Sounds
WAV, FLAC, MP3, OGG and the rest: a sample, at the lowest rate that loses nothing heard.

Take only a part (some patterns, a cut of a sound) in the item's tab, then move it to the FM-1 panel and WRITE.
