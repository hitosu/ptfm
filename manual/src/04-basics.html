Basic operation

A few rules work the same on every screen. Learn them once and every screen is easy: HOME takes you around, PLAY plays, SAVE keeps your work, the knobs move and change, and every button acts when you let it go.

HOME: back, and to everywhere

HOME backs out of whatever is open: a menu, a list, an edit, a dialog. At the top of a screen, with nothing to back out of, a tap opens HOME's combination: HOME breathes, the keys that work light up, and the next key you press does one of these:

While the combination is open, the white keys show the tracks: dim while a track plays, dark while it is muted, bright while it is soloed. The black keys show the screens, the current one bright.

Mutes and solos are part of the performance: they are not saved with the song. Once the combination has done something (a mute, say), any other key closes it and does its own job.

Playing and stopping

There is one transport: one thing plays at a time, the current song, the EDITOR's pattern loop, the loops or DJ's decks. PLAY acts the moment it is pressed.

  • On SONGS, the EDITOR, LOOPS and DJ, PLAY plays that screen's own thing: the song picked, the pattern shown (looped, from the cursor's row), the loops, both decks. If something else plays, it switches at once.
  • On the other screens, PLAY pauses and resumes whatever played last, so on FX or VISUAL it toggles what you hear.
  • What plays plays on from screen to screen until something else starts. Every edit is heard at once.
  • PLAY held stops: what plays goes back to its start, so the next PLAY plays it from the beginning (BACK TO THE START).
  • With no song to play, PLAY blinks: NO SONG TO PLAY.

The CUT: all sound off

HOME + REC, on every screen, stops every sound at once: notes, echoes, reverb tails. While REC is held there is no sound at all (SOUND CUT: LET GO OF REC), but what plays keeps its time; let go, it is heard again from its next notes.

Keep REC held for 2 seconds and every effect of what plays is switched off too (SOUND CUT, EFFECTS OFF). That is an edit like any other: SAVE keeps it, REVERT undoes it.

Saving your work

Every edit goes into the FM-1's working memory first and is heard at once. SAVE writes all of them to its flash memory, where they survive power-off. Switching off loses unsaved edits.

  • The SAVE light is dim while something is not saved.
  • A save shows its progress (SAVING 3 CHANGES), then SAVED. With nothing to save: NOTHING TO SAVE.
  • SAVE while playing waits for a pause (SAVE WAITS FOR A PAUSE); SAVE blinks meanwhile. A save still waiting is lost if the FM-1 is switched off.
  • REVERT, in a screen's SEL menu, takes the thing that screen shows back to what was saved (a pattern, a song, a cartridge, the loops ...), after a question: REVERTED.
  • REVERT ALL drops every edit since the last save: SAVE + OCT-, or SETTINGS, TOOLS, REVERT ALL. It asks first, then reloads: REVERTED TO SAVED (NOTHING TO REVERT when there is nothing).
  • SAVE held alone only shows what it does with other keys; let go then, it saves nothing. SAVE + OCT+ saves.
There is no undo step by step. What an edit changed only REVERT takes back, to what was saved. So the big actions ask first.

Samples are the one exception to "SAVE writes": CLONE and MOVE of a sampler cartridge write its samples at once.

A power cut during a save loses nothing that was saved before: the FM-1 keeps either the old state or the new one, whole. The same holds when the battery runs out.

The memory for edits holds what differs from what is saved (about 650 changed tracks). When it runs low, the header reminds you: SAVE CHANGES TO FREE MEMORY. When it is full, an edit that needs more is not made and a question asks you to save (MEMORY FOR EDITS IS FULL, SAVE BEFORE MAKING MORE EDITS): OCT+ SAVE NOW, OCT- back. After the save, make the edit again.

Storage full: a save that does not fit writes nothing and asks (NOT ENOUGH STORAGE TO SAVE): OCT+ runs CLEAN UP, which offers to delete unused patterns, samples and instruments; or delete something yourself and save again. Your edits stay in memory meanwhile. STATUS shows the free storage.

Things that use other things

A note uses an instrument, a song uses patterns, a FUSION instrument uses a pattern, a sampler instrument uses a sample. Moving, swapping or deleting something in use asks first (PATTERN 1A IS USED 3 TIMES, UPDATE REFERENCES?):

  • YES (OCT+): everything that used it follows the change, and everything is saved at once (UPDATING 3 REFERENCES, then REFERENCES UPDATED, SAVED).
  • NO (OCT-): the change is made, and what used it keeps the old number, now pointing at whatever is there.
  • HOME: nothing happens.

A reference to something missing is drawn in red and plays nothing. SETTINGS, TOOLS, CHECK REFERENCES lists them.

Built-in things

The built-in cartridges (F0 to FA) live in the firmware. Their numbers are brown wherever they are listed. You can play, use and edit them like anything else: SAVE keeps your changed copy under the same number, and it is used from then on, also after a firmware update. BACK TO BUILT-IN, in the cartridge list's SEL menu, brings the firmware's own back (CARTRIDGE F0 BUILT-IN AGAIN). Built-in things cannot be moved or deleted.

Taps, holds and combinations

  • Most buttons act when you let them go (a tap), so they can also be held as a modifier. PLAY, REC and the KEYS act the moment they are pressed, for timing.
  • Held + another control is a combination: SEL + K1 means keep SEL down and turn K1. The combination repeats while the button stays held (turn on and on), and the held button's own tap does not happen.
  • A slack of 0.1 s: a button let go a moment early still counts (SETTINGS' COMBO GRACE). So buttons that can combine act about 0.1 s after you let them go, and HOME then LFO can never clear a cell by mistake.
  • A button held alone past the hold delay (half a second, SETTINGS' HOLD DELAY) shows what it does with the other keys and knobs, for as long as it is held. Letting go then does nothing else.
  • The key that opens something closes it: a menu, a mode, an editor. EDIT opens an edit and leaves it keeping the changes; HOME leaves it dropping them (CHANGES DROPPED).

The knobs

  • SELECT moves the cursor up and down, ALGORITHM left and right. The cursor stops at the ends; only the instruments, the cartridges and the screens go round.
  • K1 to K4 change values at the cursor; the footer names what each turns. They never move the cursor.
  • Turned fast, a knob takes bigger steps (SETTINGS' KNOB SPEED); turned slowly, always one.
  • SEL + K1 (SEL held, any of K1-K4) takes big steps: an octave for a note, 10 for a value.

The keys and the current instrument

The keys play the current instrument on every screen, unless the screen gives them a job. The footer shows it on the left.

  • PRESETS picks the instrument, one a click, round within its cartridge (a cartridge is a bank of up to 128 instruments).
  • SEL + PRESETS (SEL held, or tapped first) turns the cartridges instead: the list of cartridges opens and each click picks the next. Let go of SEL (or touch another control) and PRESETS turns the instruments again.
  • OCT- and OCT+ shift the keys by an octave, from -4 to +4 (OCT +3).

The keys have no velocity: they play at SETTINGS' KEYS VOLUME. SETTINGS also sets the keys' default octave and how the effects treat them.

The arpeggiator

ARP switches the arpeggiator on and off on every screen but three: the EDITOR and LOOPS, where ARP has a job of its own, and DJ, where the keys are the decks'. While it is on, the keys you hold take turns as a rhythm instead of sounding together. Switched on with no key held, it waits for your first key.

  • The notes follow SETTINGS' ARP RHYTHM (a row a step), in the order of ARP PATTERN (UP, DOWN, UP-DOWN, RANDOM, AS PLAYED).
  • ARP LATCH keeps up to 1-8 notes playing after you let go of their keys; a key pressed with none held starts a new set. OFF: a note stops with its key.
  • Held, ARP changes them from the knobs: ARP + SELECT the rhythm, ARP + ALGORITHM the pattern (ARP UP), ARP + K1 the latch (LATCH 4 NOTES), ARP + LFO lets the latched notes go.
  • To stop latched notes and keep the arpeggiator on, tap ARP with no key held: the first tap lets the latched notes go, and ARP stays on; the next tap switches it off.
  • Changing screens switches it off.

Menus, questions and names

  • SEL opens the menu of what is at the cursor (copy, paste, move, delete ...), its last choice ready. SELECT picks, SEL (or the key that opened it) chooses, HOME closes. Only what can act is listed: REVERT, for example, only when there is something not saved.
  • Groups: an item marked > at the right holds more items (ROWS, TRANSFORM). SEL opens it; its first line, < BACK, goes back up; HOME closes the whole menu from any level.
  • MOVE: SELECT carries the thing, SEL puts it down, HOME puts it back.
  • Questions: the answers sit over their keys, in their own words (REVERT or KEEP, YES or NO): OCT+ on the right answers yes, OCT- on the left no; HOME cancels, and nothing happens. Nothing else acts while a question shows.
  • Typing a name: ALGORITHM moves along the name, SELECT changes the letter, SEL + SELECT jumps to the next group of characters (letters, digits, signs), LFO deletes a letter, OCT+ adds a space; EDIT is done, HOME cancels. Names take capitals, digits, the space and : . - ? | / \ * # $ @ %. An instrument's name holds 10 characters, a cartridge's 12, a song's 16; a name left empty becomes NO NAME.

The help on the FM-1

HOME + LFO, on any screen, opens LOOK FOR ONSWERS: a few pages on the screen you are on, then pages shared by all screens (the global keys, HOME, the screens, buttons, knobs, saving). SELECT turns the pages; HOME gets you back. In the help, keys are yellow, and a button whose print reads wrong gets its reading once: ENV (Explore Named Vaults), SEQ (Show Else, Quick), ARP (Again, Repeat, Please).
