About this manual

PT/FM is a firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1. It turns the FM-1 into a tracker: you write music as patterns of notes, play them as songs, stack live loops, mix two songs like a DJ, and play six kinds of instrument from its keys.

Read chapters 1 to 5 first: they install PT/FM, show the panel, explain the few rules every screen follows, and walk you through your first hour. Chapters 6 to 15 explain each screen in detail. The rest of the manual is for looking things up: every shortcut, every message, troubleshooting, the instruments that come with PT/FM, the specifications, and a glossary of the words used (appendix G).

PT/FM's home is hitosu.github.io/ptfm: the latest version, its pages (the install page, the EDITOR page), the demo disk and this manual. Its source, free software, is at github.com/hitosu/ptfm.

In a hurry? Install PT/FM and the demo disk (chapter 2), then go straight to chapter 5: it plays a song in a minute, and names every key it uses.

The FM-1 also has its own help on every screen: press HOME + LFO. Its pages are short reminders of what this manual explains.

How this manual writes things

WrittenMeans
SEQa button or a knob, by the name printed on the panel. PLAY is the button printed PLAY / STOP.
POLYa black key, by the name printed on it. The last black key has no print: this manual calls it the blank key.
3a white key, counted from the left: white key 1 is the lowest, white key 16 the highest.
HOME + LFOa combination: the first button held (or, for HOME, tapped first), then the second.
PLAY heldpressed and kept down for a moment (half a second unless you set HOLD DELAY otherwise).
SEL held + K1the same, said where the holding matters: keep SEL down and turn K1.
HOME + white key helda combination whose last key is kept down (here, the white key). "Held" after a combination is always about its last key.
SAVEDa message the FM-1 shows, exactly as it shows it. Numbers in messages change with the case (TRACK 02 MUTED).

On the web page on a computer, point at a key in the text and it lights on the FM-1 drawn beside the text; a row of a key table lights all its keys. Point at a key or knob of the drawing and its screen names it.

Numbers on the FM-1 are mostly hexadecimal, two digits: tracks 00 to 0F are the first to the sixteenth track, and patterns and cartridges count 00, 01 ... 09, 0A ... 0F, 10 and so on.
