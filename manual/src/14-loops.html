LOOPS

LOOPS is a loop station: the loops go round, you play over them onto the stage again and again, each round fixing a part, and REC keeps the stage as a loop the moment it sounds right. You keep a stack of up to 16 loops, mute and solo them while playing over them, and can record the whole performance as a new song. Open it with HOME + POLY.

The loops

  • Up to 16 loops, in the order they were made, with + NEW LOOP after them. Four are in view, in slots; the column at the right shows all sixteen, a square each. A loop's number is its place, 00 to 0F.
  • One length for all, set from the start: 4 bars at first (below). PLAY plays the loops even with none: the length goes round, ready for you to play over it.
  • A loop has one instrument and a colour of its own, and is POLY (up to 4 notes at once) or MONO (one), as you played it.
  • A loop repeats without copying notes: after the menu's DOUBLE it plays its notes twice (X2 after its name).
  • A loop's mix (level, pan, duck and the sends) is set by K1 to K4 and on FX's MIXER; on + NEW LOOP the knobs set the next loop's mix. Each slot shows its loop's LEVEL at the right (7F at first), + NEW LOOP the next loop's.
  • A new loop takes the KEYS column's sends: its chorus, delay and reverb sends are those of FX MIXER's KEYS column, the ones you hear as you play, so the stage on + NEW LOOP already sounds as the loop will. A send's knob on + NEW LOOP turns the KEYS column's (a setting: SAVE keeps it). REC into a loop keeps that loop's own mix.
  • LOOPS has its own effects (FX from LOOPS edits them) and metronome, so it sounds the same whatever song is current. SAVE keeps the loops like any change.
What plays (a song, a pattern) plays on into LOOPS until the loops start. The loops, and the stage with them, play on outside LOOPS until a song or a pattern starts. Leaving LOOPS for any screen but FX ends an arm, a count-in, ADJUST, the pattern recorder and a song's recording.

The stage: playing over the loops

The stage is one loop of the length that belongs to no loop. While the loops play, every note you play goes onto it (you overdub it), and you hear it each time the loops come round.

  1. Pick a sound with PRESETS (ENV: another cartridge) and press PLAY.
  2. Play. Each note lands where QUANTIZE puts it and sounds exactly as it is kept. Overdubbing adds and never removes: a note takes a lane that is free (the stage has four), so a long pad already there stays whole. With all four in use, it takes the lane whose note ends soonest and cuts that note where yours starts. A note that starts on that lane while you hold your key moves on with it, to where you let go; one your key covers completely is gone.
  3. When it sounds right, press REC: on + NEW LOOP the stage becomes a new loop (STAGE AS LOOP 06), on a loop it goes into that loop, in place of its notes (STAGE INTO LOOP 03). The stage is empty again, and what sounds goes on without a break. The stage is off now: press REC again to switch it on and start the next take (with the loops stopped, that REC also arms them: below).
  • The stage plays in your sound and through the mix REC would put it in: the selected loop's, or on + NEW LOOP the next loop's (with the KEYS column's sends), and LOOPS' effects (never KEYS GAIN or the keys' DRY), so a loop sounds as you played it. Mutes and solos never silence it.
  • The selected loop is silent while the stage holds notes (its slot grey, STAGED): the stage is what would take its place. Select another loop and the stage stays; that one falls silent instead. With the stage empty, every loop sounds.
  • MONO plays and keeps one note at a time, a note over a held one as the properties' MONO LEGATO says; POLY up to 4 notes, a fifth neither heard nor kept.
  • The length's end: a note you hold over it ends there, kept once from where you started it. The key then stops writing: hold it on and nothing more is kept, let it go and nothing changes; the note plays again where you played it each round, so a pad held to the end keeps its start.
  • Stopped, the keys play freely, nothing written; the stage keeps its notes.
  • The stage is never saved and counts as no change: SAVE and REVERT leave it as it is, so a save mid-jam loses nothing. A power cycle drops it.
The stage holds as many notes as a loop can. A note it cannot take is neither heard nor written, and the header says why: THE STAGE IS FULL, MEMORY FOR EDITS IS FULL (SAVE frees it), or STAGE NOT WRITTEN: STORAGE BUSY.

OVERWRITE, and clearing the stage

Tap LFO and you overwrite instead of overdub, the moment you let it go (OVERWRITE): while the loops play, the play line erases everything on the stage it passes, and what you play meanwhile is written, so that stretch becomes exactly what you played, silence included. Tap LFO again to overdub (OVERDUB). The FM-1 starts in OVERDUB, and the stage's title always says which is on.

  • In OVERWRITE a take goes as the line comes round, unless REC has sent it by then. In OVERDUB it stays.
  • What EDIT or the pattern recorder put on the stage stays: each leaves it in OVERDUB, switching OVERWRITE off if it was on. REC, a clear and moving the selection leave the mode as it is.
  • Clear the stage: hold EDIT for 2 seconds (let go sooner and it is a tap), or tap EDIT on + NEW LOOP (it copies that silence), or pick the menu's CLEAR STAGE: STAGE CLEARED, the selected loop is heard again, and the stage is on. With the stage empty and off, it just turns on (STAGE ON).
  • Stopped, OVERWRITE erases nothing (the play line does not move). In ADJUST, the pattern recorder and with the stage off LFO does nothing; OVERWRITE rests there, and on every other screen, FX too, where the stage plays on, and goes on once you are back. LFO has nothing else to do here: REC with LFO held is plain REC.

The stage off: playing without keeping

Tap ARP to switch the stage off (STAGE OFF), and again, or press REC, to switch it on (STAGE ON; REC with the loops stopped arms them too, STAGE ON: ARMED). REC leaves it off too, each time it keeps the stage. With it off you play over the loops and nothing is kept: to find a line before you record it, or to jam over a finished set.

  • The stage keeps its notes, drawn very faint, and is not heard; the selected loop is heard in its place, on every screen.
  • The keys sound at once in the stage's sound (your instrument and MONO / POLY, the stage's mix and LOOPS' effects), with no wait for QUANTIZE's line, and nothing is written. POLY plays every key you hold, up to 16 at once (a 17th key takes over the sound of one of them in turn); MONO one note at a time, as MONO LEGATO says. Stopped, the keys play freely as ever.
  • REC switches the stage on and records, in one tap, keeping nothing yet: with the loops stopped it arms them as well, whatever the stage holds (STAGE ON: ARMED: your first note or PLAY starts them, below); with them playing, what you play from then on goes onto the stage (STAGE ON). It still starts RECORD LIVE and cancels a count-in. LFO does nothing.
  • EDIT and the pattern recorder's REC fill the stage and switch it on again; a clear switches it on too.
  • Switched off while the loops are armed, it disarms them (CANCELLED).
  • The stage's title reads STAGE OFF and, at its right, REC: STAGE ON; the line under the bars says STAGE OFF: THE KEYS ARE NOT KEPT; ARP is dim, where it is lit with the stage on.
  • ARP's tap waits a moment (COMBO GRACE in SETTINGS) before it acts, as ARP held with REC opens the pattern recorder; held longer, ARP shows what it does with REC and, let go, does nothing.
  • The stage is on at every power-on; SAVE, REVERT and CLEAR LOOPS leave the switch as it is.

EDIT: a loop back onto the stage

EDIT copies the selected loop onto the stage as it is heard, in place of what the stage held; its sound and MONO / POLY become yours, and the loop falls silent at once (LOOP 01 ON THE STAGE). It leaves you in OVERDUB, so its notes stay as the line comes round. Play over it to fix a bar, then REC writes it back into the loop. The loop itself changes only when REC writes it.

  • To copy a loop: EDIT, select + NEW LOOP (the stage stays), REC.
  • On + NEW LOOP, or held 2 seconds, EDIT clears the stage (above). A loop too long for the stage is refused, nothing changed (THE STAGE IS FULL).
  • There is no undo of REC: the menu's REVERT brings back what was saved.

Starting with a note

With the loops stopped and the stage empty, REC arms them: REC blinks, the top line reads ARMED and the line under the bars says where they start (ARMED: A NOTE OR PLAY STARTS AT 1.1). Your first note then starts the loops at once and is the stage's first; the other keys of a chord played with it land with it. PLAY starts them after the properties' COUNT-IN. REC again disarms (CANCELLED); during the count, REC, PLAY and HOME cancel it, and nothing is sent. With the stage off (after REC kept a loop, say), one REC switches it on and arms them at once (STAGE ON: ARMED), whatever the stage holds.

While the loops play, REC with an empty stage does nothing (THE STAGE IS EMPTY).

After each thing REC does, it rests for half a second, so a double tap does one thing: it sends the stage once and never arms after it.

Where PLAY starts, and jumps

  • ALGORITHM moves the play position a row a click (with SEL held, a bar), round the length: stopped, it is where PLAY starts (PLAY FROM 2.4.3: bar, beat, step); while the loops play, they jump there at once (JUMP +1 ROW). At a jump the notes sounding ring on, and a key you hold stops writing.
  • PLAY while the loops play stops them and takes them back to the start; the stage keeps its notes. PLAY held does the same (BACK TO THE START).
  • Outside LOOPS, PLAY pauses the loops and resumes them where they were, as anything that played last.
  • PLAY's lights are a metronome you can see while the loops play: a quick flash on each beat, red on the bar's first, green on the others, dark between. They follow the METRONOME of LOOPS' effects (FX from LOOPS), its TEMPO and BEATS A BAR, even with the METRONOME off: so you can keep time without hearing the clicks.
  • The bar under the stage shows the length's bars, the ones in view white, and where the loops are.
  • With SETTINGS' SYNC EXTERNAL, the other device's START plays the loops from bar 1, STOP pauses them and CONTINUE goes on (chapter 16).

The length

  • 4 bars at first (a bar has as many beats as the METRONOME's BEATS A BAR, on FX), fewer only where 4 do not fit; 128 rows at most: 8 bars of 4 beats at 1/16, 5 at 1/16T, 4 at 1/32.
  • LENGTH in the properties (GLO), a bar a click, with loops or none: longer at once, silence added at the end; shorter only shown while the properties are open (2 BARS (NOW 4 BARS)) and cut as they close, asking first when notes would go (CUT TO 2 BARS? NOTES IN THE BARS CUT GO: OCT+ CUT, OCT- KEEP). Each loop plays on as it did in the bars kept.
  • The menu's DOUBLE doubles it, every loop and the stage playing twice what they played.
  • The stage goes with the length: cut with it, longer with empty rows. CLEAR LOOPS, DELETE LOOP and REC leave the length as it is.
  • If you change the METRONOME's BEATS A BAR, the length keeps its rows and may end inside a bar: it is then shown in beats (21 BEATS), and LENGTH goes to the next whole bar each way.

Your sound: the instrument, MONO or POLY

  • PRESETS picks the instrument the keys play, shown at the footer's left: the stage plays in it, and REC writes it into the loop it sends to. PRESETS never changes a loop's own sound.
  • ENV opens the cartridge list (NEW LOOP: A CARTRIDGE): the keys take the picked cartridge's first instrument.
  • ENV held, or the menu's TAKE ITS INSTRUMENT: the selected loop's instrument and MONO / POLY become yours (the header names them, KEYS: 01:04 BRASS, MONO). The menu's APPLY INSTRUMENT does the opposite: the loop takes yours.
  • MONO or POLY, shown at the footer's right: hold SEQ and press MONO or POLY (KEYS: MONO, KEYS: POLY), or the same keys in CONTROL mode. POLY at first.
  • A new sound or mode keeps the stage's notes, played in the new sound: a POLY stage in MONO plays one note at a time, and POLY again brings its chords back.

The knobs, VIEW and ADJUST

  • K1 to K4 turn LEVEL, PAN, REVERB SEND and TEMPO at first; the properties give each knob its job: LEVEL, PAN, DUCK, CHORUS SEND, DELAY SEND, REVERB SEND (the selected loop's, on + NEW LOOP the next loop's, a send there the KEYS column's), TEMPO or VIEW. With SEL held a mix value moves by 16, TEMPO by 10.
  • VIEW: 1, 2 or 4 bars a screen (2 at first). While the loops play, the view turns a page as the play line leaves it.
  • ADJUST (the menu, on a loop) moves the whole loop round the length without moving a note: K1 a row, K2 a bar, heard and drawn at once. SEL keeps it (LOOP 03: SHIFT +17 ROWS), HOME cancels (CANCELLED). A moved loop shows its SHIFT after its name, the nearer way round (SHIFT -3 is 3 rows earlier). REC writing the stage into a loop sets its SHIFT back to 0.

QUANTIZE

Where a played note lands: the properties' QUANTIZE, or the black keys OP1-OP5 in CONTROL mode (QUANTIZE: 2 ROWS).

2 ROWS, 3 ROWS, 4 ROWS
The nearest line of that many rows, from the loops' start.
ROW
The nearest row (at first).
REAL TIME
The row it falls in, its exact time kept as a note delay, its end as a key off.

A note played a little early waits for its line; one just after it sounds at once and is kept on the line. The menu's QUANTIZE moves a kept loop's notes onto a grid (LOOP 03 QUANTIZED: 4 ROWS).

CONTROL mode: mutes and solos

In CONTROL mode the keys stop playing notes and become a mixer for the loops. Each white key is a loop: white key 1 is the first loop, white key 16 the sixteenth.

  1. Press SEQ. The FM-1 shows KEYS: CONTROL and the header reads SEQ MUTE.
  2. Tap a white key: its loop mutes (LOOP 02 MUTED) or plays again (LOOP 02 ON) at once.
  3. Press SEQ again to play notes: KEYS: PLAY.

Or hold SEQ and press a key: that key acts as in CONTROL mode once, and the keys stay playing notes.

Hold a white key a moment instead (longer than HOLD DELAY in SETTINGS) and the change waits for the next bar line (LOOP 02 MUTED AT THE BAR); the loop shows a yellow M while it waits.

Press the black key GLO to make the white keys solo instead of mute (SEQ: SOLO; again: SEQ: MUTE). One loop is soloed at a time: a white key solos its loop alone (LOOP 02 SOLO), and on the soloed loop it ends the solo (LOOP 02 SOLO OFF). Held, the solo waits for the bar line (a yellow S).

While a loop is soloed only it sounds (and the stage), but the mutes are kept and come back when the solo ends. SAVE keeps the mutes with the loops; a solo is never saved, so after a power-on no loop is soloed.

The pattern recorder

Hold ARP and press REC (it opens as you let go of REC), or pick the menu's RECORD PATTERN: a step sequencer opens over the loops, which play on, so the pattern is heard in the mix while you build it; REC puts it onto the stage. (The EDITOR has a simpler one of its own: SEL, RECORD PATTERN.)

  • 4 rows of up to 16 steps, a note a row (with a kit, each row a drum), each step a row of the grid (16 steps at 1/16 are a bar). SELECT picks the row the white keys switch.
  • A white key switches its step on or off as you let go. Held a moment, the step is a tie: the note before rings on through it. A held key after a step that is off, or on the first step, makes a plain note, so a held run starts with one; switching off a note before its ties turns the next tie into a note.
  • The row's note: K3 moves it a semitone a click; or tap SEL, then press a key: that key is the note (KEYS: THE NOTE, then KEYS: THE STEPS). SEL held + a key does it at once. OCT- / OCT+ move the row's note with the octave (NOTE C-5).
  • K1 NOTE LENGTH, how long each step's note sounds: 1/32 to 1 BAR (the lengths the grid has), or TO THE NEXT (it rings until the row's next note); 1/16 at first. A row's next note always ends the one before; notes on different rows overlap, so a chord rings. K2 LENGTH: 4, 8, 12 or 16 steps. The pattern repeats over the loops' length from their start; the box's last lines say how (REPEATS OVER 4 BARS, 4 X 16 = 64 ROWS, 2 LANES).
  • The preview plays from the moment the recorder opens, in your sound (opened on a loop, in that loop's): in time with the loops while they play, else on its own at LOOPS' tempo. ARP switches it off and on. ALGORITHM turns the tempo here.
  • REC puts the pattern onto the stage, in place of what it held, and closes (PATTERN ON THE STAGE); the stage plays in the preview's sound, what sounds goes on as the stage's, and a preview playing on its own starts the loops from where it was. REC on the screen then keeps the stage as a loop. No step on: NO STEPS ON. HOME closes it, nothing written.

The recorder opens clean every time: no steps, the first row on C, 16 steps, NOTE LENGTH 1/16. It is refused while the loops are armed or count in. With the stage off it opens, and its REC switches the stage on.

RECORD LIVE: the performance as a song

SEL, RECORD LIVE records everything that sounds into a new song, whatever LOOPS does meanwhile: the loops as they are heard, the stage, the keys, the pattern recorder's preview, effects, punch-ins, tempo changes and the mix.

  1. SEL, RECORD LIVE opens its panel: the song's NAME (EDIT types it) and K1 TEMPO.
  2. REC arms it (REC blinks; the top line's REC reads SONG; the line under the bars, SONG ARMED: A NOTE STARTS HOME CANCELS). HOME cancels.
  3. It starts with your next REC, a note, or, when the loops were stopped as you armed it and still are, PLAY (after COUNT-IN).
  4. Perform, and work as usual: everything on LOOPS does what it always does, heard and so written, and REC keeps its jobs. PLAY drops the loops out and brings them back, the song's time running on; PLAY held takes them to their start. The line under the bars reminds you how it ends: RECORDING: HOME ENDS AND KEEPS IT.
  5. HOME ends it (SONG 12 RECORDED: 1:23, 5 PATTERNS): the row you pressed it in is the song's last, and the loops play on. The song is on SONGS under its NAME, and plays once and stops; SAVE keeps it.
  • Held, HOME is its combination as ever. In the pattern recorder or a dialog HOME closes that; HOME again ends the song. Armed, HOME cancels it.
  • Only notes that have a voice are written: a note given no voice is not written, and a note losing its voice ends there. The song's rows are its own, fixed as it starts, so GRID, LENGTH or a change to a loop never moves what was written; the notes go where they were heard.
  • With the stage on, the keys are written as they land on it, by QUANTIZE; with it off, or while the loops are silent, as played. A FUSION note is written as one note.
  • Started by REC or a note with the loops stopped, they stay stopped until PLAY.
  • LOAD FROM PATTERN leaves the loops silent at their start, as the song's time goes on; PLAY brings them on.
  • Leaving LOOPS (but for FX) ends it, keeping what was written; so does PLAY on FX, which pauses the loops. SAVE NOW and REVERT ALL end it first; SAVE itself waits for a pause, as ever.
  • Notes that do not fit the song's 16 tracks are left out and counted (SONG 12: 3 NOTES LEFT OUT). It stops by itself when the memory, the pattern numbers or 256 positions run out (SONG 12 STOPPED: MEMORY FOR EDITS IS FULL): the rows waiting for their pattern, up to 128, go, and with no pattern written yet there is no song. With no song number free it is refused (NO SONG NUMBER FREE).

Loops to a pattern and back

LOOPS has no precise editing; for that, store the loops in a pattern and edit it in the EDITOR.

SAVE TO PATTERN
Writes one pattern of the loops' length: every loop as it is heard, repeats written out, each note with its instrument, and each loop's mix (LOOPS STORED IN PATTERN 2A). Pick NEW or a pattern; a pattern that is not empty asks first. The stage is not written.
LOAD FROM PATTERN
Turns a pattern into loops, a loop an instrument, replacing the loops after a question (PATTERN 2A IN LOOPS: 3 LOOPS). The length is the pattern's, in whole bars; what does not fit 16 loops of 4 notes is left out and said. The loops stop at their start; the stage stays.
