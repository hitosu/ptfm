FX

FX holds the effects on the whole mix, the mixer, the metronome and the punch-in effects. Press FX on any screen to open it, and FX again to go back where you were. SEQ turns its three pages: LIVE, EDIT and MIXER.

Whose effects

Every song has its own effects (its FX setup: the chain's order, routing, each effect's knobs and on/off, the mixer and the metronome). LOOPS and DJ have their own. FX always edits what you hear:

  • the song playing (or the one played last); a pattern from the EDITOR uses the current song's;
  • in LOOPS, the loops'; on DJ, DJ's (FX from LOOPS or DJ does not leave them);
  • before anything has played, THE KEYS': the keys' own setup, with the reverb on, so the FM-1 sounds round as a synth from its keys alone.

A new song starts with only the LIMITER on. THE KEYS' setup, which the keys play through at power-on, also has the REVERB on. While what FX edits plays, nothing but your hand changes the effect under the cursor: the song's own commands for it are skipped.

The keys keep the last song's effects. Once a song has played, the keys go on playing through its effects and at its tempo after it stops or pauses, until something else plays. THE KEYS' own setup comes back at the next power-on, or at once with UNLOAD SONG (SONGS' SEL menu, chapter 6). With LOOPS on the screen and nothing playing, the keys play through the loops' setup.

LIVE: playing the effects

  • The white keys are the 16 punch-in effects, each while its key is held (below); the keys play no notes on LIVE.
  • The black keys pick an effect, a fixed key each: OP1 GATE, OP2 FILTER, OP3 DRIVE, OP4 LO-FI, OP5 CHORUS, OP6 DELAY, PIT REVERB, GLO LIMITER, MONO DUCK, POLY METRONOME.
  • The BLANK key is BYPASS: every effect off while held (the metronome clicks on).
  • SELECT picks the effect too. OCT+ switches it on, OCT- off (GATE ON, GATE OFF).
  • K1 to K4 turn its knobs (SEL held: steps of 10); ALGORITHM shows its next knobs (FILTER has 8, DELAY 5). LFO resets the knob turned last.
  • EDIT shows the effect on the EDIT page; SEL opens its menu (RESET, REVERT).
  • ARP on LIVE plays the last note you played, hands free (with SETTINGS' ARP LATCH not OFF), so you can play the effects over an arpeggio: the keys stay punch-ins.

EDIT: the chain

  • The chain is a list: the sound goes from top to bottom. The LIMITER is always last; DUCK and the METRONOME sit apart, outside the chain.
  • SELECT picks an effect; K1-K4 its knobs; ALGORITHM its next knobs.
  • OCT+ / OCT- switch it on and off, so you can set it first and switch it on after. The keys play notes on EDIT, to hear the effect; SEL + OCT+ and SEL + OCT- change their octave.
  • SEL opens the menu: MOVE (SELECT moves the effect through the chain, heard as it goes; SEL puts it down, MOVED; HOME puts it back, NOT MOVED; the LIMITER, DUCK and the metronome stay: LIMITER DOES NOT MOVE), RESET (the effect as at first), REVERT (every effect as last saved).
  • Each effect's picture shows what it does: the filter's curve, the echoes, the reverb's decay. The footer shows what the effect costs the processor (GATE 0.4% CPU). Each effect, its picture and its knobs are below.

The effects

Ten effects, each in a section of its own below, in the order FX EDIT lists them, with all its knobs. On EDIT, SELECT picks the effect, K1-K4 turn its first four knobs and ALGORITHM shows the next four (FILTER and DELAY have more than four); LFO puts the last knob turned back to its default. A knob with no range given runs 0-100 %. A new song starts with only the LIMITER on (THE KEYS' setup has the REVERB on too); OCT+ switches the picked effect on.

GATE

Chops the sound on and off in a rhythm, locked to the song's bars: a trance gate on whatever passes through it. Its rhythm is a pattern of 16 steps; each step is OFF (closed), ON (open for the first three quarters of the step) or a ratchet, X2, X3 or X4 (two, three or four short hits within the step).

PATTERN
Which rhythm. 00-0F are built in (the table below); 10-1F are yours, MY GATE 10 to MY GATE 1F, written on the white keys (below). 00-1F; 00 STRAIGHT at first
RATE
How long a step is: at 1/16 the 16 steps make one bar of 4 beats, at 1/8 two bars, at 1/32 half a bar; 1/8T and 1/16T are triplet steps (3 and 6 to a beat). At 1/16, step 1 falls on the bar's first beat. 1/8, 1/16, 1/32, 1/8T, 1/16T; 1/16 at first
DEPTH
How far a closed step closes: 100 % is silence, 50 % only dips the sound, 0 % leaves it as it is. 100 % at first
SMOOTH
How soft the edges are: low, a hard click-on, click-off chop (0.5 ms); high, a soft pulse that swells in and out (up to 20 ms). 20 % at first

Writing your own steps: on EDIT with GATE picked, EDIT puts its 16 steps on the white keys: a key switches its step on or off; held, ALGORITHM gives it ratchets (X2, X3, X4). A built-in pattern is copied into yours first (STRAIGHT COPIED TO MY GATE 11). EDIT keeps the changes, HOME drops them. ENV on GATE lists the patterns.

FILTER

One filter on the whole mix: a low-pass (darker, the top taken away) or a high-pass (thinner, the bass taken away), with an envelope that can open it on every note or beat, as a synth's filter envelope does. Its eight knobs are on two pages (ALGORITHM turns to the second).

CUT
Where the filter cuts, and which kind: turned left of the middle it is a low-pass closing from LP 20K down to LP 100 (Hz); turned right, a high-pass opening from HP 20 up to HP 8.0K; in the middle, OFF. Turned by hand it glides, so no step is heard. With the envelope on, CUT is where the filter rests between notes. LP 100 ... LP 20K, OFF, HP 20 ... HP 8.0K; OFF at first
RESO
Resonance: a peak at the cutoff. Low is smooth; high makes the cutoff ring and whistle, the acid squelch when the cutoff moves. 30 % at first
ENV
How far each note (or beat) opens the filter, as a part of the way from CUT to fully open: a low-pass opens upward, a high-pass downward. 0 % switches the envelope off. 0 % at first
SOURCE
What opens it. KEYS: every note the keys play (the FM-1's, MIDI's, the arpeggiator's), held as long as the key. TRACK 00 ... TRACK 0F: every note that track starts, muted or not, so a silent track can carry a rhythm for the filter. 4 BEATS, 2 BEATS, BEAT, 1/2 BEAT, 1/4 BEAT: the beat itself, every so many beats, each hit held half its length. KEYS at first
ATTACK
How long the filter takes to open from where it is. 2 ms-3 s; 2.0 MS at first
DECAY
How long it then takes to close down to SUSTAIN. 5 ms-5 s; 296 MS at first
SUSTAIN
How open it stays while the note is held, as a part of ENV's opening; 0 % closes it fully after DECAY, a pluck. 0 % at first
RELEASE
How long it takes to close back to CUT once the note ends. 5 ms-5 s; 296 MS at first

There is one envelope for the whole mix, not one a note: each new note starts it again from wherever it is, and it holds while any note of the source is held.

LO-FI

The sound of an old sampler: fewer bits, a lower sample rate and record crackle.

BITS
How finely the level is measured: 16 is clean, 8 grainy, 4 and under harsh and noisy, 2 a broken buzz. 2-16; 8 at first
RATE
The sample rate it imitates: lower takes the top away and adds the metallic ring of aliasing; 44 kHz leaves the rate as it is. 1-44 kHz; 22 at first
DUST
Crackles and specks, as from a record. They are a sound of their own: heard even when nothing plays. 20 % at first

CHORUS

A JUNO-style chorus: two short delays, swaying in opposite ways left and right, mixed with the sound, so it widens, thickens and gently detunes.

MODE
The JUNO-106's three chorus settings. I: slow (0.5 Hz) and gentle. II: a little faster (0.83 Hz) and deeper, a lusher swirl. I+II: fast (9 Hz) and shallow, a shimmering vibrato. I, II, I+II; I at first
RATE
The sway's speed, around the mode's own: 50 % is the JUNO's, 0 % about a third of it, 100 % about 1.7 times. 30 % at first
DEPTH
How far it sways, around the mode's own in the same way: more is a stronger pitch wobble. 50 % at first
MIX
How much of the chorus is heard against the sound itself. With ROUTING SENDS (below) it is the chorus's return level instead, and each track sends to it by its MIXER send. 50 % at first

DELAY

Echoes locked to the song's tempo. The echoes are mono; PING-PONG puts them left and right in turn.

TIME
The time between echoes, as a note value; T marks triplets, a dot dotted values (1/8. is three sixteenths). Up to 1 s (0.5 s with PING-PONG): a value longer than that at a slow tempo plays the longest that fits. 1/32, 1/16T, 1/16, 1/8T, 1/16., 1/8, 1/4T, 1/8., 1/4, 1/2T, 1/4., 1/2; 1/8. at first
FEEDBACK
How much of each echo comes back as the next: low, one or two echoes; high, a long trail. 40 % at first
TONE
How bright each echo is: every repeat passes through a low-pass, so lower makes the echoes darker and softer as they go, as a tape or analogue delay; 100 % keeps them bright (up to about 13 kHz). 60 % at first
PING-PONG
ON: the echoes bounce between left and right. OFF: they stay in the middle. OFF, ON; OFF at first
MIX
How loud the echoes are against the sound. With ROUTING SENDS, the delay's return level instead. 30 % at first

REVERB

A plate reverb: the sound in a large, smooth, bright space.

SIZE
How long the reverb rings: from about 1.3 s, a room, to about 11 s, a cathedral (the time it takes to die away). 60 % at first
DAMP
How dark the tail gets: more damping takes the highs out of the tail as it rings, warmer and less hissy; none leaves it bright. 40 % at first
PRE-DELAY
A gap before the reverb starts, so the sound's attack stays clear in front of it. 0-100 ms; 10 at first
MIX
How loud the reverb is against the sound. With ROUTING SENDS, the reverb's return level instead. 25 % at first

DRIVE

Warm distortion: the mix pushed into a soft clipper, adding harmonics as an overdriven amplifier or tape does. The deep bass (under about 55 Hz) is kept out of it, so it stays clean; the level is evened out as the drive rises.

DRIVE
How hard it is pushed: low, a little warmth and grit; high (up to about nine times the level), heavy saturation. 30 % at first
TONE
The brightness after the distortion: low is dark and smooth, high bright and edgy. 50 % at first

LIMITER

Keeps the mix from going over a ceiling, catching its peaks so the sound never clips. Always last in the chain, and on in every new song.

CEILING
The most the mix may reach. Lower catches more of the peaks, and the mix comes out quieter. -12 to 0 dB; -1 at first
RELEASE
How fast the level comes back after a peak: short keeps it loud but can pump; long is smoother. 10-1000 ms; 100 at first

DUCK

One track pushes the others down while it plays, as a kick making room for itself in the bass and the pads (sidechain). It sits outside the chain. Which tracks duck, and how much, is set in the MIXER's DCK row, none at first: raise DCK on the tracks that should duck (MIXER). The source never ducks itself.

SOURCE
The track that pushes the others down, or KEYS: every note the keys play, so the song can make room for a part played live. 00-0F, KEYS; 00 at first
DEPTH
How far the others go down when the source hits (each also by its own DCK). 60 % at first
RELEASE
How long they take to come back up after it: short, a quick dip; long, a slow breathing pump. 20-1000 ms; 150 at first

METRONOME

A click on the beats, to play or record in time. It is mixed after the effects, takes no voice, and stays off USB audio. It sits outside the chain. The count-in before a take is not the METRONOME's: SETTINGS' COUNT-IN (chapter 13).

TEMPO
FOLLOW: the tempo of what plays (on THE KEYS, their own TEMPO). A number: its own tempo, so it clicks with nothing playing. FOLLOW, 30-300 BPM; FOLLOW at first
BEATS A BAR
How many beats a bar; the first is accented, louder and higher. In LOOPS it is also the loops' bar. 1-16; 4 at first
SOUND
CLICK: a short tick of noise. BEEP: a clean tone (higher on the accent). WOOD: a woodblock knock, its pitch falling. COWBELL: the classic drum machine's two-tone cowbell. CLICK, BEEP, WOOD, COWBELL; BEEP at first
LEVEL
How loud it clicks. 60 % at first

Routing and the keys

GLO opens FX PROPERTIES, titled whose setup it is (a song's name, THE LOOPS, DJ or THE KEYS):

ROUTING
GLOBAL: one chain on the whole mix. SENDS: CHORUS, DELAY and REVERB leave the chain and become shared sends, each track sending its own amount (the MIXER's CHO, DLY, REV); their returns join the chain at the SENDS line (marked with a diamond): effects above it work on the dry sound only, below it on everything (ROUTING SENDS, ROUTING GLOBAL).
KEYS, EVERYWHERE
THROUGH FX, or DRY: the keys skip every effect (as SETTINGS' KEYS) (KEYS DRY, KEYS THROUGH FX).
TEMPO, SWING
For THE KEYS: their own tempo and swing.

MIXER

  • A column for each of the 16 tracks (8 at a time), a row for each value: LVL (level), PAN, DCK (how much it ducks, shown while DUCK is on), and with ROUTING SENDS CHO, DLY, REV (its sends).
  • ALGORITHM picks the track, SELECT the value, K1 changes it (SEL held: by 16), LFO resets it.
  • KEYS, the last column, is the keys' own strip, the same in every song (its LVL is SETTINGS' KEYS GAIN).
  • In LOOPS the columns are the loops (MIXER: THE LOOPS).
  • A value a song's command moved shows white, BY THE SONG; muted tracks are dark.

Punch-in effects

Hold FX and press a white key, on any screen: that effect plays on the whole mix for as long as the key is held, locked to the tempo. On LIVE the white keys alone do it. Let go, it fades back into the mix.

ARP, with a punch-in's key heldHolds the punch-in on after you let go of its key (LOOP 1/4 HELD ON: ITS KEY STOPS IT), so your hands are free; FX lights full meanwhile. Its key again (or another punch-in's) stops it, as does HOME + REC.

The song goes on underneath every punch-in, in time: let go, and you hear it where it is now, faded back in over 80 ms (VINYL STOP spins back up instead). FX LIVE's map shows short names (LP SWEEP, ECHO THRW, HALF SPD ...); FX held's list and the toasts, the full ones below.

LOOP 1/4, LOOP 1/8, LOOP 1/16, LOOP 1/32
Catch the last quarter, eighth, sixteenth or thirty-second note of the mix, just before the key, and repeat it for as long as the key is held: LOOP 1/4 repeats the last beat, as a DJ's loop does; LOOP 1/32 repeats so fast it becomes a buzzing roll.
STUTTER 1/16T
The same with a sixteenth-note triplet (a sixth of a beat): the fast, off-grid stutter of a glitch edit.
BIT CRUSH
The live mix ground down to a handful of levels (8 each way from silence, about 4 bits) at a sixth of the sample rate: gritty, aliased, old-sampler noise.
GATE 1/16, GATE 1/32
The live mix chopped on and off in time: sound for the first half of every sixteenth (or thirty-second) note, silence for the second, with soft edges. A trance gate on the whole song.
LOW-PASS SWEEP
A resonant low-pass filter closes over one bar, from 18 kHz down to 200 Hz, and stays closed while held: the song sinks under water.
HIGH-PASS SWEEP
A resonant high-pass filter rises over one bar, from 30 Hz up to 6 kHz: the bass and the kick fall away and the song thins to its top, the classic build-up before a drop.
ECHO THROW
Throws the mix into the delay while held: dotted-eighth echoes bouncing left and right, with 65 % feedback. Let go, nothing more goes in, and the echoes ring on for about 2.4 s over the song. It borrows the DELAY's line, so the song's own DELAY is not heard meanwhile.
REVERB SWELL
The mix rises into a huge reverb over two beats, the dry sound falling to half under it: a wash. Let go, its tail rings on in the song's own REVERB (with REVERB off, it fades out over 80 ms). It borrows the REVERB, so the song's own is not heard meanwhile.
REVERSE
The last beat played backwards, again and again while held.
HALF SPEED
The mix from the moment pressed, played at half speed: an octave down, dragging behind the song. It falls further behind the longer it is held, and every second it jumps back to the present, so each second held plays the half second that began it.
TAPE STOP
The sound slows down and stops over one beat, pitch falling with it, as a tape machine switched off. Stopped, it is silent until you let go.
VINYL STOP
A turntable's stop button: the record brakes to a standstill in half a second. Stopped, it is silent. Let go, it spins back up in a tenth of a second, straight into the song, landing on the beat.
TAPE STOP or VINYL STOP? Both slow the sound to a stop and stay silent while held. TAPE STOP follows the song: it takes one beat (half a second at 120 BPM, a whole second at 60), so it is slower than VINYL STOP in slow songs and faster in fast ones, and it simply fades back into the song when let go. VINYL STOP always takes half a second, whatever the tempo, and when let go it spins back up audibly, a DJ starting the record again.
The loops, the stutter, reverse, half speed and both stops play back the last half second of the mix, which the FM-1 keeps for them. In songs slower than about 120 BPM a whole beat does not fit in it, so LOOP 1/4 repeats an eighth note instead and REVERSE plays the last eighth backwards. The others (bit crush, the gates, the sweeps, the echo throw and the reverb swell) work on the live mix.

A pattern can hold punch-ins as commands (!08: as if its key were held for 8 rows), so songs can carry them. In LOOPS a punch-in is heard, and written only by RECORD LIVE.
