The panel

Seven endless knobs and a volume knob, fourteen buttons, 27 keys and a 240 × 240 colour screen. Many of the buttons are printed for the FM-1's original synth; PT/FM gives each one job on every screen.

The keys

The 27 keys play notes, F-3 to G-5 at octave 0; OCT- and OCT+ shift them down to C-0 or up to G-9. At octave -4 the seven lowest keys, which would be below C-0, are silent. Many screens give the keys other jobs:

  • The 16 white keys are 16 tracks, 16 loops, 16 steps or 16 punch-in effects, depending on the screen. White key 1 is the leftmost.
  • The 11 black keys open the screens (with HOME): OP1 to OP5, PIT, GLO, MONO and POLY. OP6 and the last, unprinted key, the blank key, have no screen yet.

The lights

Every button and key has a light, and the lights tell you what works:

  • dim: it does something on this screen;
  • off: it does nothing here;
  • on: its mode or its list is open (REC on: writing; ARP on: the arpeggiator runs).

PLAY has two lights. Green means playing (brighter on each beat); red means paused. Green with a dim red means a loop is playing (the EDITOR's pattern or the loops), not a song. Red dim alone: nothing to resume yet. Both dark: PLAY has nothing to play here.

Some lights have rules of their own:

  • SAVE is dim while something is not saved yet, dark when everything is saved, and blinks dim while a save waits for the music to pause.
  • OCT- and OCT+ show the octave. Both are dark at octave 0; OCT- lights for the octaves below, OCT+ for those above: dim at 1, full at 2, blinking slowly at 3 and fast at 4.
  • A fast blink on a button means it cannot act just now: PLAY with no song to play, for example.
  • HOME breathes (dim to full and back) while HOME's combination is open; then the black keys show the screens (the current one bright, the others dim, OP6 and the blank key dark) and the white keys show the tracks: dim while a track plays, dark while it is muted, bright while it is soloed.
  • REC is on while the EDITOR writes, and blinks during a take.
  • The keys light with their notes: full while a note is loud, dim as it fades, dark once it is quiet. A plucked sound flashes and fades, a held pad stays lit, so the keys show what sounds, not only what is pressed.
  • While FX is held (and on FX LIVE) the white keys are the punch-ins: keys 1, 5, 9 and 13 light dim so you can find each group of four by touch, and a running punch-in lights full. Other screens that use the white keys as buttons (steps, DJ's pads) light them the same way.

The screen

Every screen has the same frame:

  • The header (the top line): the screen's name in its group's colour, then what it shows (a pattern's number, a page, SEQ and the view SEQ switches to); on the right the voices sounding, the transport (a green triangle playing, two red bars paused, a red dot recording), the processor's load and the battery. The battery has three bars, its shell turning red when none is left; while a computer powers the FM-1 over USB, the bars fill one after another in light blue (a plain USB charger cannot be told apart, so it shows the battery). Short messages (toasts) take the name's place for two seconds, and so does a knob's name and value while you turn it.
  • The footer (the bottom line): on the left the current instrument, the one the keys play (F8:00 and its name: cartridge F8, slot 00); on the right what the knobs turn here.
  • The body between them: the screen's own content.
