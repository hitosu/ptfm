EDITOR

The EDITOR writes patterns: a grid of tracks across and rows down. Each cell holds a note, the instrument that plays it, a volume and up to four commands. Open it with HOME + OP2.

The grid

  • SELECT moves up and down the rows, ALGORITHM across the tracks. Nothing wraps; past the last row of a page the next page shows.
  • SEQ turns the view: 8, 4, 2 or 1 tracks at once (8 TRACKS ... 1 TRACK). Fewer tracks show more of each cell: 8 tracks the notes only, 4 the instrument too, 2 a command as well, 1 the whole cell with all four commands.
  • Row numbers are hexadecimal, 00 to 7F. Beat rows are cyan, each bar's first row light cyan. When this pattern plays, the playing row sits on a blue band; the view stays with the cursor.
  • In a cell, --- is no note, === a note off, .. no volume (the instrument's own) and ..:.. no instrument (the track's plays on).
  • The footer's right side says the command (or volume) under the cursor in words: A04 VOLUME -4.

Which pattern you edit

The EDITOR edits one pattern at a time: its own, or a numbered one.

  • Its own pattern (the header says just EDITOR): a sketch pad no song can play. SAVE keeps it after switching off. A fresh FM-1 opens on it.
  • A numbered pattern (EDITOR 12): your edits go straight into pattern 12, and every song that plays it hears them.

From the cell's SEL menu (nothing marked):

NEW PATTERN
Empties the EDITOR's own pattern (8 tracks × 64 rows) and opens it.
LOAD A PATTERN
Copies a numbered pattern into the EDITOR's own, to start from it (PATTERN 1A LOADED). The numbered one is not changed.
STORE IN A PATTERN
Gives what the EDITOR shows a number, so songs can play it: pick a pattern, or + NEW PATTERN for the first free number (STORED IN PATTERN 1A); the EDITOR follows it there. Storing over a pattern that holds anything asks first (PATTERN 0C WILL BE REPLACED, DO YOU WANT TO CONTINUE?).
Opening another pattern empties the EDITOR's own. When it holds anything, a question asks first: EDITOR CHANGES WILL BE LOST, DO YOU WANT TO CONTINUE?. Store it in a pattern first if you want to keep it.

The pattern list: ENV opens it (PATTERNS: number, size, a map of notes by track, the songs using it; unused patterns dim). ENV again opens the highlighted pattern; PLAY hears it on its own, looped, and what played before pauses, keeping its place. Moving the highlight moves the preview; PLAY again stops it (ARP does what PLAY does here). Picking a pattern or leaving the list ends the preview, and the next PLAY resumes what played before. HOME closes. Its SEL menu:

NEW
An empty pattern, 8 tracks × 64 rows, at the first free number (PATTERN 13 MADE); with all 240 used, ALL 240 PATTERNS USED.
CLONE
A copy of the highlighted pattern at the first free number (12 CLONED TO 13).
MOVE
To another free number: SELECT carries it, SEL puts it down (PATTERN 12 MOVED TO 20); with none free, NO FREE NUMBER. Songs using it ask to follow (references).
DELETE
Deletes it (PATTERN 12 DELETED), asking first when something uses it.

Writing notes

REC switches writing. With REC off (REC OFF: KEYS PLAY) the keys only play the current instrument, so you can try sounds freely. With REC on (REC ON: KEYS WRITE, REC lit), the keys write:

  1. Pick the sound with PRESETS: the note is written with the current instrument and SETTINGS' KEYS VOLUME.
  2. Play a key: its note is written at the cursor and sounds; the cell's commands stay.
  3. The cursor moves down by the edit step (GLO's EDIT STEP; 0 stays put). Keys pressed together (a chord) land on the same row, spreading to the next tracks.
  • LFO clears the cell and moves on; on an empty cell it writes a note off (===).
  • REC while a song plays pauses the song (SONG PAUSED: WRITING).
  • REC while the EDITOR's loop plays: the loop plays on and the keys write step by step at the cursor; it plays what you write as it reaches it. PLAY then pauses the loop (LOOP STOPPED: PLAY FOR A TAKE), and the next PLAY starts a take.
  • Every change is heard at once by whatever plays this pattern (the EDITOR's loop, a song, a DJ deck).
  • K1-K4, LFO, the menu and the note editor change cells whether REC is on or off.
  • A note written with no instrument (..:..) plays the track's last instrument.
  • To change the sound of notes already written, change each cell's INSTR in the note editor (below); there is no change for many cells at once. On LOOPS, APPLY INSTRUMENT gives a whole loop another sound.

PLAY with REC off plays the pattern in a loop from the cursor's row.

Volume and commands: K1 to K4

The knobs change the cell at the cursor, without moving the cursor:

K1
Picks which part of the cell the other knobs change: the volume, or command 1 to 4 (one past the last, to add one) (K1 VOLUME, K1 CMD 1 ACCENT). The grid shows the command in that place.
K2
The command there, through every command there is (below); back to NO COMMAND removes it.
K3, K4
Its value, a digit each. On the volume, K3 steps by 16 and K4 by 1. For an instrument parameter (*), mixer value (=) or FX knob (#), K3 picks which and K4 sets it. SEL held: K4 by 16.

The volume runs from 00 (silent) to 7F, the instrument at its full level. Turned past 7F it becomes .., no volume: the note plays at the instrument's own default (for a sampler instrument, its VOLUME). The VOLUME command (Cxx) uses the trackers' smaller scale, 00 to 40, where 40 is as loud as a volume of 7F.

An example: to fade a note out, put the cursor on it, turn K1 to CMD 1, K2 to VOL SLIDE (Axy), and K4 to 4: the cell shows A04, and the note's volume falls by 4 on every tick of the row. The footer says what the command at the cursor does, in words.

The note editor

EDIT on a cell opens it as a list of fields, a line each: NOTE, INSTR (its sound), VOLUME, CMD 1 to CMD 4, and + COMMAND to add one.

  • SELECT moves a line up or down, ALGORITHM across a command line (the command, its value).
  • K1 changes the part at the cursor a step; K2 in big steps: an octave, a cartridge, 16, or the next group of commands (each command is explained below).
  • SEL makes SELECT and ALGORITHM move to the neighbouring cells instead (SELECT, ALG: CELLS), without leaving; SEL again goes back to the fields (SELECT, ALG: FIELDS).
  • The KEYS change the note; LFO clears the cell.
  • EDIT closes it keeping the changes; HOME drops them.

Recording live: takes

With REC on and nothing playing, PLAY records a take: from the cursor's row round and round the pattern, adding on each pass, until PLAY or REC stops it.

  1. Press PLAY: recording starts as you let go, after SETTINGS' COUNT-IN (1 or 2 bars of clicks, or none). With COUNT-IN set to KEY, your first note starts it.
  2. Or hold PLAY and press a key: the take starts on that note at once. Hold PLAY alone to see both ways (LET GO: START THE TAKE, PRESS A KEY: START ON THAT NOTE).
  3. Play. Notes land on SETTINGS' QUANTIZE grid (2, 3 or 4 rows, a row, or REAL TIME); letting go of a key writes a note off. A melody stays on the cursor's track; a chord spreads to the next tracks.
  4. Press PLAY or REC to stop (TAKE STOPPED). REC stays on and the cursor goes back to the row the take started on.

During a count-in a box in the middle of the screen counts it: COUNT-IN, the beats left in big figures, and the bar (BAR 1 OF 2). With COUNT-IN set to KEY, the box says TAKE ARMED, PLAY A NOTE, until your first note.

REC blinks during a take and its count-in. An armed take is cancelled with HOME, PLAY or REC (TAKE CANCELLED). A full edit memory ends a take, keeping what was written.

Listening to a row

ARP on the EDITOR repeats the cursor's row at the song's tempo, in SETTINGS' ARP RHYTHM (ROW PREVIEW #___; again, ROW PREVIEW OFF). Whatever plays stops first, so the row is heard alone; moving the cursor moves the preview. ARP + SELECT changes the rhythm (RHYTHM ##_: # plays a row, _ rests one).

Blocks

  1. SEL, MARK: then SELECT and ALGORITHM stretch a block over rows and tracks from the cursor. Or hold SEL and turn them.
  2. SEL again opens the block's menu.
COPY, CUT, PASTE
Through a clipboard kept until power-off (COPIED 4 X 1: rows × tracks). Over 2 KB: BLOCK TOO BIG TO COPY; an empty clipboard: NOTHING COPIED.
PASTE MIX
Pastes into empty cells only.
CLEAR
Empties the block.
MOVE, SWAP
Carry the block with the cursor; SEL puts it down. MOVE leaves its old place empty, SWAP puts what was there in its place; HOME cancels (CANCELLED).
INSERT ROW, DELETE ROW
Under ROWS. INSERT ROW puts an empty row at the cursor's row, the rows below moving down (the pattern's last row is lost); DELETE ROW takes the cursor's row out, the rows below moving up. With a block marked, it acts on the block's tracks; with nothing marked, on the cursor's track and every track to its right, so from the first track it is every track.
TRANSPOSE
Under TRANSFORM: K1 a semitone, SEL held an octave, SEL ends it (TRANSPOSED +02).
INTERPOLATE
Under TRANSFORM: a sweep. A command on the block's first and last rows goes on every row between, its value stepping from one to the other; without one, the volumes step.

The pipette: SEL, TAKE ITS INSTRUMENT, or ENV held: the cursor's cell's instrument becomes the current one (the toast names it, 01:05 EL PIANO DX7); none there: NO INSTRUMENT HERE.

RECORD PATTERN: steps on the white keys

SEL, RECORD PATTERN opens a step sequencer: the white keys switch 16 steps on and off, and REC writes their notes into the cursor's track from the cursor's row (PATTERN: 12 NOTES). It is the same recorder LOOPS has; LOOPS explains its keys. Here K4 picks TO THE END (the steps repeated down to the pattern's last row) or ONCE. Nothing already there is removed: a step off leaves its cell as it is.

The commands

A cell holds up to four commands, acting in their order. Each takes 3 characters in the grid: the command, then its value. Values are hexadecimal; x and y are one digit (0-F), xx two (00-FF).

Rows and ticks. Every row is cut into ticks: TICKS A ROW in the song's properties, 6 at first. A command works either once, as the row starts (the "fine" ones, a volume, a pan), or on every tick after the first (slides, vibratos, volume slides move a little each tick, so the same command is stronger with more ticks a row). In the slides, vibratos and volume slides, a value of 00 goes on with the last one that track used.

K2 turns through the commands in this order: the tracker effects (0-9, then A-Z), the volume-column ones (~), PT/FM's own, then *, = and #. Below they are grouped by what they change.

Pitch

GridNameDoes
0xyARPEGGIOPlays the note, the note x semitones up and the note y semitones up in turn, a tick each, over and over for the row: a chord from one voice, the chip-music arpeggio. 037 is a minor chord, 047 a major one.
1xx, 2xxSLIDE UP, SLIDE DOWNThe pitch slides up (or down) by xx sixteenths of a semitone every tick: 10 slides a semitone a tick. It keeps the new pitch until the next note.
3xxSLIDE TO NOTEPortamento: the cell's note is not started; instead the note playing glides toward it at speed xx (sixteenths of a semitone a tick) and stops there. Put 300 on the following rows to keep gliding.
4xyVIBRATOThe pitch wobbles around the note: x is how fast, y how deep. 400 goes on with the last speed and depth.
5xySLIDE TO NOTE + VOLGoes on gliding to the note as 300 does, and slides the volume as Axy does.
6xyVIBRATO + VOLGoes on with the vibrato as 400 does, and slides the volume as Axy does.
E1x, E2xFINE SLIDE UP, DOWNMoves the pitch up (or down) by x sixteenths of a semitone, once, as the row starts: a small correction rather than a slide.
X1x, X2xEXTRA FINE UP, DOWNAs E1x and E2x, four times finer.
E3xGLISSANDOE31 makes slides to a note (3xx, 5xy, ~Mx) heard in semitone steps, as a glissando on a keyboard; E30 makes them smooth again.
E4xVIBRATO WAVEThe shape of the vibrato's wobble: 0 sine, 1 saw, 2 square, 3 random.
E5xFINETUNETunes the note as it starts, by x - 8 eighths of a semitone: E50 is a semitone flat, E58 in tune, E5F nearly a semitone sharp.
UxyFINE VIBRATOAs 4xy, four times shallower: a gentle shimmer.
~Sx, ~VxVIBRATO SPEED, DEPTHThe volume column's vibrato: ~Sx sets its speed, ~Vx vibrates with depth x.
~MxSLIDE TO NOTEThe volume column's 3xx, at speed x × 16.

PT/FM's own

GridNameDoes
*xxPARAMETERTurns a knob of the track's instrument, as on its edit page (a JUNO-106's VCF FREQ, a DX7's OP2 LEVEL, a TB-303's CUTOFF ...): K3 picks which, K4 sets it. The sound keeps it until changed, so a row of them turns the knob over time; another instrument on the track drops it.
=xxMIXERSets the track's place in the mixer: K3 picks LEVEL, CHORUS SEND, DELAY SEND, REVERB SEND or DUCK AMOUNT, K4 the value.
#xxFXTurns any knob of the song's effects (K3 picks it, K4 sets it), or switches an effect on or off. It holds until the next song starts.
!xxLOOP 1/4 ... VINYL STOPA punch-in effect, as if its key were held for xx rows (!08: 8 rows), so a song can carry its own loops, sweeps and stops (FX).
  • B, D, E6, EE, F, G, H, # and ! act on the whole song, from any track. In a FUSION pattern they are ignored, shown dim.
  • A command for what you are editing right now (an effect on FX, the mixer, an instrument's edit page, a song's properties) is skipped while you edit it, so nothing jumps under your hands.
  • EDIT (the note editor) says each command in words.
