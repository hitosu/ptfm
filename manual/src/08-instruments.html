INSTRUMENTS

INSTRUMENTS picks, plays and edits the sounds. Sounds live in cartridges: a cartridge is a bank of up to 128 instruments of one type, each in a numbered slot (01:05 is cartridge 01, slot 05). Open it with HOME + OP3.

Browsing and playing

  • The cursor is the current instrument: SELECT moves a slot at a time, ALGORITHM by 8 (a column).
  • PRESETS steps to the next or previous sound there is, skipping empty slots, round.
  • K1 turns the cartridges, round.
  • The KEYS play the sound picked; ARP arpeggiates it, so with ARP LATCH both hands are free for the knobs.
  • The bottom half shows the sound: a DX7's operators, a JUNO-106's sliders, a TB-303's knobs, a CHIP sound's steps and tables, a sample's waveform, a FUSION's pattern as a little map.
  • GLO shows the cartridge's name, type and slots used, and renames it.
  • ENV lists all the cartridges (CARTRIDGES: ID, name, type, slots used); ENV again opens the highlighted one.

Changing a sound: edit mode

Press EDIT: the list gives way to the sound's settings below and a picture of it above (the DX7's algorithm and envelope, the JUNO-106's envelope and filter, the sample's waveform ...).

  • The settings are a table, four to a row (a group). SELECT picks a row; K1 to K4 change its four values (RELEASE 33 MS in the header as you turn).
  • ALGORITHM or SEQ turn the pages of settings (SEQ goes round).
  • The KEYS play the sound as it changes; every change is heard at once, by everything that plays it.
  • LFO puts the knob turned last back to its default (LEVEL 0 DB (DEFAULT)).
  • PRESETS moves to another sound, still editing.
  • SEL on NAME types the name; elsewhere it opens the menu: COPY, PASTE, INIT, RENAME, AUTO LEVEL, REVERT (FUSION: EDIT PATTERN too).
  • SEL held + a knob: big steps (LEVEL by 6 dB, a value by 10).
  • EDIT leaves, keeping the changes; HOME leaves, dropping them (CHANGES DROPPED).

An empty slot has nothing to edit: EMPTY SLOT: SEL, INIT (except FUSION, below).

LEVEL
Every instrument has one: how loud it is, in dB, half a dB a click. AUTO LEVEL (in the SEL menu) measures the sound and sets it as loud as the others (LEVEL SET TO -1.5 DB; a silent sound: NO SOUND TO MEASURE). -64 to +63.5 dB
NAME
The sound's name; SEL on it types it.

DX7

The DX7 makes sound by FM: six sine-wave operators, each with its own envelope. Some (the carriers) are heard; the others (the modulators) bend their waves, which gives bells, electric pianos, basses and brass. Its four pages:

OPERATORS
The first page: a row for each operator.
OP1, OP2, OP3, OP4, OP5, OP6
The six rows, one an operator. Each has the same four knobs:
LEVEL
K1: how loud the operator is (a carrier), or how strongly it bends the others (a modulator). 0-99
COARSE
K2: the operator's frequency as a multiple of the note's: 1 is the note, 2 an octave up, 3 an octave and a fifth; 0 is half. Whole multiples sound harmonic, the others bell-like. 0-31
FINE
K3: adds to COARSE in small steps, up to almost twice its multiple. 0-99
DETUNE
K4: a slight detune, for a slow beating between operators. -7 to +7
ENVELOPES
Two rows for each operator: how its volume moves over a note.
OP1 RATE, OP2 RATE, OP3 RATE, OP4 RATE, OP5 RATE, OP6 RATE
An operator's four rates, R1 to R4 on K1 to K4.
OP1 LVL, OP2 LVL, OP3 LVL, OP4 LVL, OP5 LVL, OP6 LVL
Its four levels, L1 to L4 on K1 to K4.
R1, R2, R3, R4
How fast the envelope moves to each level: R1 to L1, R2 to L2, R3 to L3, and, once the key is let go, R4 to L4. 99 is the fastest, 0 the slowest. 0-99
L1, L2, L3, L4
The levels it moves to: it rises to L1, goes on to L2, then to L3, where it stays while the key is held; let go, it moves to L4 (usually 0: silence). 0-99
PITCH / LFO
The pitch envelope and the LFO.
P RATE, P LEVEL
The pitch envelope: its rates R1-R4 and levels L1-L4 move as an operator's envelope does, but on the pitch of the whole voice. Level 50 is the note's own pitch; above it bends up, below it down. 0-99
LFO
The LFO's first row. The LFO is a slow wave that moves the pitch or the volume:
SPEED
K1: how fast the LFO's wave goes. 0-99
DELAY
K2: how long after the key the LFO fades in. 0-99
PM DEPTH
K3: how much it moves the pitch: vibrato. 0-99
AM DEPTH
K4: how much it moves the volume: tremolo, on the operators whose stored sensitivity lets it (below). 0-99
LFO 2
The LFO's second row:
WAVE
K1: the LFO's wave; S+H is random steps. TRI, SAW-, SAW+, SQR, SINE, S+H
SYNC
K2: ON, the LFO starts again with each key; OFF, it runs on freely. OFF, ON
P SENS
K3: how strongly PM DEPTH acts on the pitch. 0-7
VOICE
The last page: NAME, LEVEL, and the row VOICE.
ALGORITHM
K1: which of the 32 ways the six operators are wired, which are heard and which modulate which; the picture shows it. 00-1F
FEEDBACK
K2: the loop drawn in the algorithm's picture, an operator modulating itself: brighter, then noisy. 0-7
OSC SYNC
K3: ON, every operator's wave starts again from zero with each key, so every note starts alike. OFF, ON
TRANSPOSE
K4: shifts the whole voice, in semitones. -24 to +24

A DX7 voice also stores what its pages do not show: keyboard scaling (louder or softer towards the ends of the keyboard), the sensitivities to velocity and to the LFO's AM, and fixed frequencies. Voices from DX7 cartridge files keep them, and PT/FM plays them as they are.

JUNO-106

The JUNO-106 is an analogue polysynth: one oscillator (DCO) with a pulse, a saw, a sub-oscillator and noise, through a resonant low-pass filter (VCF) and a high-pass, shaped by one envelope, with a chorus. Browsing, its sound shows as the original's sliders.

Its pages:

DCO
The first page, the oscillator. Its first row is named DCO too:
LFO
K1: vibrato, how much the LFO moves the pitch. 0-127
PWM
K2: the pulse's width: how far the LFO moves it (PWM BY LFO), or where it stands (PWM BY MAN). 0-127
NOISE
K3: how much noise is mixed in. 0-127
SUB
K4: the sub-oscillator, a square an octave down, mixed in. 0-127
WAVES
The second row:
RANGE
K1: the octave, 16' the lowest. 16', 8', 4'
PULSE
K2: the pulse wave on or off. OFF, ON
SAW
K3: the saw wave on or off; the two can sound together. OFF, ON
PWM BY
K4: LFO, the LFO moves the pulse's width; MAN, PWM sets a fixed width. LFO, MAN
FILTERS
The page of the filters.
VCF
The low-pass filter's row:
FREQ
K1: the cutoff: how bright the sound is. 0-127
RES
K2: resonance, a peak at the cutoff; turned high, the filter whistles. 0-127
ENV
K3: how far the envelope moves the cutoff over a note. 0-127
LFO
K4: how far the LFO sweeps the cutoff. 0-127
VCF 2
The second row:
KYBD
K1: how much the cutoff follows the note: higher notes brighter. 0-127
ENV +/-
K2: +, the envelope opens the filter; -, it closes it. +, -
HPF
K3: the high-pass filter: 0 boosts the bass, 1 leaves it flat, 2 and 3 make the sound thinner. 0-3
ENVELOPE
The page of the envelope, the amplifier and the LFO.
ENV
The envelope's row. The envelope shapes the volume (with MODE at ENV) and the filter (by VCF's ENV):
A
K1: attack, how fast the sound rises. 0-127
D
K2: decay, how fast it falls to the sustain level. 0-127
S
K3: sustain, the level it holds while the key is down. 0-127
R
K4: release, how long it fades once the key is let go. 0-127
VCA
The amplifier's row:
LEVEL
K1: the amplifier's level, before the instrument's own LEVEL in dB. 0-127
MODE
K2: ENV, the volume follows the envelope; GATE, full while the key is held, silent at once when let go. ENV, GATE
CHORUS
K3: the JUNO's own chorus, II the deeper. OFF, I, II
LFO
The LFO's row: the slow wave that DCO's LFO and PWM and VCF's LFO use.
RATE
K1: the LFO's speed. 0-127
DELAY
K2: how long after the key the LFO fades in. 0-127
VOICE
The last page: NAME and LEVEL.

TB-303

The TB-303 is the acid bass line: one oscillator through a squelchy resonant filter. In a pattern, the commands ACC (accent) and SLD (slide) play its two famous tricks (EDITOR). One page:

KNOBS
The page, and its first row, the original's knobs:
WAVE
K1: the oscillator's wave: saw at 0, square at 127, the two mixed between. 0-127
CUTOFF
K2: the filter's cutoff: how bright the sound is. 0-127
RESO
K3: resonance, the squelch: a peak at the cutoff. 0-127
ENV MOD
K4: how far each note's envelope opens the filter. 0-127
KNOBS 2
The second row:
DECAY
K1: how fast the filter closes again after each note. 0-127
ACCENT
K2: how much an accented note stands out: louder, brighter, a sharper sweep. 0-127

CHIP

CHIP is a virtual sound chip: it makes the sounds of 8-bit computers and consoles the way their chips made them, square waves at a few widths, a stepped triangle, a drawn wave, noise, 16 volume steps moving 50 or 60 times a second. Its four families: BRK (the handheld), 8BIT (the console), PSG (the computers' programmable sound generator) and 16BIT (that chip's finer later version). Any part works on any voice.

Its pages:

TONE
The first page: the oscillator, the noise, the clock and the sweep.
OSC
The oscillator's row. K1 picks the oscillator: PULSE, TRIANGLE, WAVE (one of 16 drawn waves), BUZZ (the PSG's envelope run fast enough to be a tone) or NOISE. The row's other knobs are that oscillator's own:
DUTY
PULSE's: the pulse's width; 50% is the hollow square, the narrow ones thinner. (A table can move it: below.) 12.5%, 25%, 50%, 75%
WAVE
WAVE's: which of the 16 waves it plays, by name (ORGAN ...); the WAVE page shows them.
SHAPE, STEPS, GATE
BUZZ's: SHAPE the envelope's shape (SAW DN, SAW UP, TRI), STEPS how many steps it has (16 or 32), GATE ON cuts it with a square at the same note (the "sync buzzer").
NOISE
The noise row. Its first knob, NOISE, ON mixes noise into the tone:
MODE
K2: LONG, a hiss; SHORT, a metallic, pitched noise. LONG, SHORT
RATE
K3: how high the noise sounds. 0-F
KEY
K4: KEY ON, the rate follows the note played; KEY OFF, it stays as set. KEY OFF, KEY ON
CLOCK
The row of the chip's own manners. Its first knob, CLOCK, is the chip it is tuned on (BRK, 8BIT, PSG 1.77, PSG 1.00, PSG 1.79, 16BIT), so high notes go out of tune as they did on that chip; EXACT is always in tune.
CURVE
K2: how its 16 volume steps sound, as on each chip. BRK, 8BIT, PSG, 16BIT
FILTER
K3: the chip's own output filter, or NONE. BRK, 8BIT, PSG, NONE
FRAMES
K4: how often its tables and envelopes step: 60 or 50 times a second, as the old machines' screens did. 60 HZ, 50 HZ
SWEEP
The sweep's row: the pitch slides by itself from each note's start. Its first knob, SWEEP, sets the way: OFF, DOWN or UP.
SHIFT
K2: how big each step of the slide is. 0-7
PACE
K3: how often it steps; 0, never. 0-7
VOLUME
The page of the volume. Its first row is named VOLUME too:
VOL
K1: the volume, in the chip's 16 steps. 0-F
ENV
K2: NONE, the volume stays; DOWN or UP, it steps down or up by itself; PSG, the chip's own envelope (the next row). NONE, DOWN, UP, PSG
STEP
K3: how often DOWN or UP takes a step. 0-7
RELEASE
K4: how fast the sound fades once the key is let go; 0 stops it at once. 0-F
PSG ENV
The PSG's envelope, used with ENV at PSG:
SHAPE
K1: one of the chip's 16 shapes, drawn beside its number: down, up, repeated, triangles. 0-F
PERIOD, FINE
K2 and K3: the envelope's speed, a four-digit hex number: PERIOD sets its first two digits, FINE its last two. Bigger is slower.
TABLES
The page of the tables: built-in lists that change the sound a step a frame (FRAMES: 60 or 50 a second).
VOL, ARP, PITCH, DUTY, NOISE
A row for each table: VOL shapes the volume, ARP adds notes (chords, drum drops), PITCH bends the pitch, DUTY changes the pulse's width, NOISE the noise's rate. Each row has the same two knobs:
TABLE
K1: which table, by number and name, or -- for none.
SPEED
K2: how many frames each step of it lasts. X1-X4
VIBRATO
The vibrato's row: DELAY (K1, how many frames after the note it starts, 0-62), SPEED (K2, X1-X4) and DEPTH (below).
DEPTH
K3: how far the pitch swings. 0-62 cents
WAVE page
The row TABLE: the wave the WAVE oscillator plays (00 SAW ...), drawn large.

Sampler

A sampler cartridge holds samples (recorded sounds, uploaded from the computer: the FM-1 does not record audio) and up to 64 instruments that play them. One sample can serve several instruments: a drum loop cut into hits, say. To bring your own sounds and make instruments for them, see chapter 17.

SAMPLE
The first page. Its first row is named SAMPLE too:
SAMPLE
K1: which of the cartridge's samples it plays. ENV on it lists the samples (name, length, rate, waveform): the keys play the one highlighted, and ENV again picks it.
START, END
K2 and K3: the part of the sample that plays, a click moving 1/200 of the sample (SEL held: 10 clicks).
LOOP
The loop's row. Its first knob, LOOP, sets how the part from FROM to TO repeats while the key is held: OFF, it plays once; FWD, over and over; PING, forwards and backwards in turn.
FROM / LOOP START, TO / LOOP END
K2 and K3: where the loop starts and ends (the header says LOOP START and LOOP END), a click 1/200 of the sample.
RELEASE
K4: how long the sound fades once the key is let go; ENDLESS lets it ring. 4 MS ... 118 S, ENDLESS
PITCH
The pitch's row:
BASE NOTE
K1: the note at which the sample plays as it was recorded. Set it to the note the recording sounds, and the keys play it in tune.
TUNE
K2: finer tuning, in 1/128 of a semitone, up to almost a semitone either way.
VOLUME
K3: the volume a note gets when its cell has none. Songs converted from MOD, S3M or XM files bring their samples' default volumes here; for your own sounds, leave it at 64. 0-64
ENVELOPE
The second page, an envelope as a DX7 operator's.
RATES, LEVELS
Two rows: RATES, R1 to R4 on K1 to K4, and LEVELS, L1 to L4. The volume rises to L1, goes on to L2, then L3, where it stays while the key is held; let go, it moves to L4. Each R sets how fast it moves to its L, 99 the fastest. 0-99

In the samples list, SEL, DELETE removes a sample. When instruments use it, it warns first (DELETE SAMPLE?, 3 INSTRUMENTS USE IT AND ARE DELETED TOO), and they go with it; an unused one goes at once (SAMPLE 03 DELETED). Deleting an instrument never deletes its sample: SETTINGS' CLEAN UP removes samples nothing uses.

FUSION

A FUSION instrument is a sound made of a pattern. Each key plays its own row of the pattern, every note of that row at once: C-4 plays row 3C (its MIDI note number), and letting go stops them all. So one key can play a chord, a layered sound, or a different drum on every key (a kit).

FUSION
The one page: the pattern's map above.
PATTERN
Which pattern it plays. On it, K1 or ENV pick another (in ENV's list the keys try each before you pick); SEL opens it in the EDITOR to write it.
  • Writing it: SEL on PATTERN opens the EDITOR on the pattern, locked to it: with REC off the keys play this FUSION as you write it, its rows lighting green; with REC on they write. HOME comes back.
  • EDIT on an empty FUSION slot makes a new pattern (128 rows, 4 tracks) and first asks (A NEW PATTERN FOR THIS SLOT, FILL IT WITH 01:00 EL PIANO?): K1 or SELECT pick another sound (the keys hear it); OCT+ (FILL) writes that sound's note on every row, so the slot plays as that sound, ready for layers; OCT- (EMPTY) leaves it empty.
  • Rows the keys do not reach can keep ideas for later; SEL, MOVE puts one on a key.
  • A FUSION inside a FUSION is silent: no chains, no loops. Voices are shared: rows with many notes take voices from other sounds.

The menus

SEL on a slot:

MARK
Several slots: SELECT (ALGORITHM by 8) stretches the mark; then SEL for their menu. COPY, INIT and DELETE act on all of them. SEL held + SELECT marks too.
COPY, PASTE
Copy a sound (4 at most) and paste it over a slot (COPIED 3; nothing copied: NOTHING COPIED).
MOVE, SWAP
MOVE carries the sound to an empty slot (full cartridge: NO EMPTY SLOT: SWAP INSTEAD); SWAP trades places. SELECT carries, SEL puts down, HOME takes back. Songs using it are asked about (references).
DELETE
Empties the slot.
INIT
A plain starting sound in the slot.
RENAME
Type its name.
AUTO LEVEL
As loud as the others.
REVERT
The sound as last saved (REVERT SLOT 01:00 TO SAVED?).

SEL in the cartridge list (ENV):

NEW
A new cartridge: pick its type (DX7, JUNO-106, TB-303, CHIP, SAMPLER, FUSION). It goes at the end of your cartridges (CARTRIDGE 11 MADE); all numbers used: ALL CARTRIDGE NUMBERS USED.
CLONE
A copy (01 CLONED TO 11). A sampler cartridge's samples are copied too, written at once (CLONING ITS SAMPLES); without room for them, NO ROOM IN THE USER AREA.
MOVE
To another free number; a sampler cartridge with unsaved changes must be saved first (SAVE IT FIRST).
DELETE, RENAME
As for a slot.
AUTO LEVEL ALL
Sets every sound's LEVEL at once, after a question (AUTO LEVEL 32 SOUNDS?, EACH ONE'S LEVEL SET AGAIN): LEVELS SET.
BACK TO BUILT-IN
On a changed built-in cartridge (brown number): your changes dropped, the firmware's own back.
REVERT
The whole cartridge as last saved.
